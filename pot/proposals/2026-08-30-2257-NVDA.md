## 6. The case against

NVIDIA may be manufacturing part of the demand it reports. The Q2 filing shows $99bn of equity
investments, $25bn of future equity commitments, $56bn of AI-cloud agreements and third-party
leases, $108.5bn of maximum guarantee exposure, and extended payment terms on large multi-quarter
agreements; receivable days have already risen from about 51 to 60, and five direct customers make
up 70% of receivables
([Q2 filing](https://investor.nvidia.com/files/doc_financials/2027/NVDA-2027-Q2-10Q-Final-including-exhibits.pdf)).

Those gross amounts are different instruments rather than one accounting liability, but together
they show that NVIDIA is investing in, lending support to and guaranteeing the ecosystem that buys
its systems. Because the filing does not identify supported-customer revenue, reported revenue and
gross margin can stay excellent while economic end demand weakens; if financing is masking demand,
the eventual stop would hit revenue, credit losses and the multiple together. The main book already
has 7.06% in NVIDIA, China remains constrained, and a 31× recurring multiple is cheap only against
NVIDIA's own extraordinary history, which is why this proposal is deliberately smaller.
