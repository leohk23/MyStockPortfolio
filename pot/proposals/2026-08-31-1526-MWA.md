## 2. Thesis

Mueller supplies valves, hydrants, pipes and metering products used to transmit, distribute and measure water, tying demand to replacement and modernisation of essential North American networks rather than a discretionary consumer cycle. From FY2022 to FY2025, local filed revenue rose from $1.247bn to $1.430bn while operating margin improved from 10.1% to 18.8%, and Q3 FY2026 added 4.1% sales growth, a 20.4% GAAP operating margin and $110.6m of nine-month free cash flow. The market is treating the margin step-up as temporary, while the small position bets that foundry investment, pricing discipline and faster-growing Water Management Solutions can preserve materially better economics even if headline volumes remain subdued.

## 6. The case against

The latest margin expansion may not be durable operating leverage. Q3 sales grew only 4.1% and
primarily through pricing while overall volumes were slightly lower; Water Flow sales fell 0.6%,
and the margin bridge explicitly included tariff refunds and lower incentive compensation.
Residential construction remains slow, foundry capital spending is rising, and reversing those
temporary benefits could expose Mueller as a low-growth cyclical whose improved multiple was earned
on peak margins. The local $11.2m operating-income mismatch is an additional warning that the
reported bridge must be read from the filing rather than trusted mechanically.

Why this is not the 7532.T mistake again: the order is small, the valuation filter is checked after
removing a disclosed one-off, and the thesis is tied to filed margin, cash conversion and segment
growth with explicit tests for a reversion—not to a generic infrastructure rebound.
