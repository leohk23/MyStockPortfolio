## 2. Thesis

NVIDIA's case is operating performance, not the screen: FY2023–FY2026 revenue grew eightfold while operating margin rose from 20.7% to 60.4%, and Q2 FY2027 revenue, Data Center revenue and GAAP operating income grew 106%, 117% and 124% respectively ([local earnings](../../earnings.json), [Q2 results](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)).
Why now: Rubin is ramping into production across multiple cloud partners and management guides Q3 revenue to $108.0bn ±2% without assuming China Data Center compute revenue, while a recurring-earnings cross-check still sits near the cheapest end of the local five-year distribution ([Q2 results](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx), [local valuation](../../prices.json)).
The market may be over-discounting a near-term hyperscaler pause and underestimating how inference, sovereign AI and physical-AI demand can broaden beyond today's largest buyers, but margin—not a cheap-looking headline multiple—is the test.

This is not the 7532.T mistake again: four filed years show accelerating USD revenue and high
margins, the latest company filing refreshes the forward prospect, and the proposal corrects the
valuation distortion from unrealised equity gains. It also confronts customer concentration,
circular financing, China exposure and supply commitments rather than assuming an industry rebound.

## 6. The case against

NVIDIA is increasingly financing and underwriting the ecosystem that buys its products. The 10-Q
shows supply and capacity commitments rising from $119bn to $279bn in one quarter, $25bn of future
equity commitments and $36bn of AI-cloud agreements; customers can stop providing that capacity to
NVIDIA or fail to turn infrastructure into profitable demand
([Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)).
That can broaden the market, but it can also make revenue look less independent precisely when the
company is taking enormous inventory, credit and capacity risk.

Equity gains inflated first-half GAAP earnings by $23.7bn, export controls can remove markets or
strand inventory, and the human book already has 7.06% in NVIDIA. If hyperscaler returns disappoint,
customers' own accelerators improve, or circular financing masks end demand, earnings and the
multiple can contract together; 31× recurring earnings is cheap only against NVIDIA's extraordinary
recent history, not in absolute terms.
