## 2. Thesis

R&S Group supplies the transformers that grids, renewable projects, storage and data centres need,
and its earnings quality is visible in both the accounts and cash: FY2025 organic sales grew 8.6%,
EBITDA margin was 20.9%, free cash flow was CHF48.1m and leverage fell to 0.7×
([2025 annual report](https://ir.the-rsgroup.com/media/document/0fec9fb0-b91a-4deb-aef1-5cb2afb265d8/assets/RSG_Annual_report_2025_def.pdf?disposition=inline)).
Why now: H1 sales fell 13% and EBITDA fell 23% as utility inventories normalized and longer-cycle
power-transformer deliveries slipped, but backlog still rose 17% to a record CHF357.9m, book-to-bill
was 1.2× and deliveries now extend into 2027–2028
([H1 update](https://ir.the-rsgroup.com/news/rands-group-provides-trading-update-for-h1-2026/bb41967c-ac88-4041-be50-200b3f26951b)).
The market may be treating that delivery delay as the start of a transformer down-cycle, while the
backlog mix and the new Łódź capacity suggest it is partly a timing and capacity-conversion problem;
if management converts those signed orders without surrendering its 19%–21% margin range, earnings
can recover without requiring another commodity-price surge.

This is not the 7532.T mistake again: the order does not rely on a rebound story, headline reported
growth inflated by an acquisition, or favourable currency. It separates FY2025's 47% reported
growth from 8.6% organic growth, confronts the current sales and order-intake declines, verifies
cash conversion and leverage in the annual report, and makes backlog conversion—not the share
price—the falsifiable claim.

## 6. The case against

The share may be cheap because earnings are already rolling over. H1 adjusted order intake fell
10%, sales fell 13%, EBITDA fell 23%, and management now expects FY2026 revenue of CHF410m–CHF420m—
roughly flat with FY2025 and below its mid-term organic growth range. Utility customers have excess
inventory and installation bottlenecks, while the higher power-transformer mix takes longer to
produce; backlog can therefore look reassuring for years before becoming cash, and factory delays
would hurt both revenue and margins ([H1 update](https://ir.the-rsgroup.com/news/rands-group-provides-trading-update-for-h1-2026/bb41967c-ac88-4041-be50-200b3f26951b)).

The accounting also deserves scepticism. Much of FY2025's reported growth came from the first full
year of Kyte Powertech, bought for CHF256.9m; CHF180.7m of acquisition goodwill was written directly
against equity under Swiss GAAP FER, and its theoretical residual goodwill was still CHF131.4m at
year-end. The report's own theoretical capitalization view shows CHF39.7m of 2025 goodwill
amortisation that does not pass through reported profit, while copper and aluminium input prices
were already rising ([2025 annual report](https://ir.the-rsgroup.com/media/document/0fec9fb0-b91a-4deb-aef1-5cb2afb265d8/assets/RSG_Annual_report_2025_def.pdf?disposition=inline)).

Finally, this is a short listed history—only 179 locally priced weeks—and a small SIX line where
the bid/ask spread and a second commission can matter more than the modelled fees. The strongest
reason not to buy is that the market may correctly see peak transformer margins, acquisition-
flattered growth and an expensive capacity build meeting normalized utility demand. The 12.70×
multiple is only the gate RSGN.SW passed; it does not make that cycle risk disappear.
