industrial plant, ships, consumer products and commodities meet the technical standards and
regulations that apply to them, and certifies companies' management systems. Customers (builders,
manufacturers, retailers, energy and marine operators, governments) pay per inspection, test or
audit, often under multi-year framework contracts.

## 2. Thesis

Bureau Veritas sells compulsory or contract-required checking, and that demand is growing: H1 2026
organic revenue grew 5.0%, and on 29 July management lifted its 2026 organic-growth guidance to
"mid-to-high single-digit" from "mid-single-digit", while holding "adjusted operating margin
improvement at constant exchange rates". The business underneath earns well on the capital it uses:
ROIC 13.4%, gross profit 66% of total assets and asset turnover 0.92x in FY2025, with reported
operating margin steady at about 14–15% for three years, which is the consistency Leo's §2.4 asks
for. What the share price is weighing is the H1 reported operating profit, **down 16.0%**, and a
pivot to acquisition-led growth on more debt; whether that pivot leaves owners with the earnings the
adjusted figures show is not assumed here, it is what P3 tests.

**Why this is not 7532.T again:** there is no rebound story. The case rests on filed organic growth,
a margin record and capital returns that already exist, and the falsifier is about whether
management's capital allocation erodes them.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** T3 — a tailwind as optionality (independent assurance of AI systems) and a headwind for
the part of the business that is priced-by-the-hour compliance work an agent can increasingly do;
T3 itself says mandated assurance is narrower than it sounds.

**Dominant risk (`financial`):** the acquisition-led strategy, funded by higher leverage, turns
recurring disposal, exit and restructuring costs into a permanent gap between the adjusted earnings
the market prices and the reported earnings owners keep.

**Earliest observable:** acquisitions and provisions announced at or before the **21 October** Q3
release; the full-year adjusted-to-reported gap and all-in cash in the FY2026 results, due before
**1 March 2027**. Both within two quarters, so Rule 4 does not halve the order.

**Rule 5, what the discount is.** The de-rating is small: 23.0x reported is 5% below its 237-week
median, P/OCF 11.8x is 10% below its median, and the share is down 4.7% over a year. That is not
T3 being priced; nothing in the record says investors are discounting AI displacement of
inspection. It looks like the market pricing H1's reported 16% fall and the
[22 September capital-markets update](https://group.bureauveritas.com/newsroom/bureau-veritas-confirms-leap-28-ambition-upgrades-total-revenue-growth-double-digit-2027)'s
call for faster acquisitions inside a 1.5–2.0x leverage range (cited by the 17:11 run, not
re-read here). I think the first is mostly
a comparator effect (the 2025 Food Testing gain, €64.9m in the stored evidence), which is why P3's
break 1 tests exactly that. The second is the genuine risk, and it is the dominant one above.

**Secondary arguments, put as strongly as I can:**

- A 66% gross-profit-to-assets ratio is high, and a high return invites price competition from SGS,
  Intertek and local labs. FY2025 margins were flat, not rising, while revenue grew 3.6%.
- Adjusted EPS grew 4.8% in H1 while reported attributable profit fell 26.2% (stored evidence).
  Twelve months of that would make "adjusted" a label rather than an earnings measure.
- The 79.4% H1 cash ratio (my measure, not the company's KPI) is below the level a services
  business should convert at.
- T3's headwind: certification audits and document review are exactly the work that agent tools
  are being sold into. No external figure isolates this yet; the next place it would show is
  organic growth in Certification at the 21 October release.
