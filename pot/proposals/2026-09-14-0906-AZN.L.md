## 3. Falsifier

Define **cash after investment** as net cash from operating activities minus purchases of property, plant and equipment minus purchases of intangible assets, using the issuer cash-flow statement. It is a deliberately demanding analytical measure, **not company-defined free cash flow**; acquisitions and financing flows remain separate.

**Warning:** At the **30 October 2026** nine-month result, re-read if this cash measure is **below 5% of nine-month revenue**, or operating cash flow divided by reported operating profit is **below 85%**. H1 baselines are 5.15% and 83.99%: the latter is **already a warning**, not a newly discovered future trigger. The present proposal explicitly accepts that warning at half size; failure to improve at the next result requires a fresh decision. These are my thresholds, not management guidance.

**Break:** At FY2026 results, checked no later than **28 February 2027**, **cash after investment at or below zero**, or **operating cash flow below 70% of reported operating profit**, breaks the cash-funded growth thesis under §5.1. Either route suffices, irrespective of Core EPS growth. Also break if reported operating profit is non-positive. A one-period investment surge can trigger this rule even with a promising pipeline; that is the chosen protection against continuously buying growth that fails to fund itself.

The [cash-flow statement](https://www.astrazeneca.com/content/dam/az/PDF/2026/h1q2/H1-and-Q2-2026-results-announcement.pdf) supplies each field.
