## 2. Thesis

Tradeweb is a multi-asset electronic-marketplace network whose value grows as institutions,
dealers and workflows meet on the same venues, while integration, liquidity and regulatory burden
help its high margin resist competition. Why now: four locally filed years show revenue rising
from $1.189bn to $2.052bn and GAAP operating margin widening from 35.9% to 41.2%, while Q2 2026
still produced 9.0% revenue growth and higher net margin in a more normal volatility backdrop
([earnings.json](../../earnings.json), [Q2 release](https://investors.tradeweb.com/news-releases/news-release-details/tradeweb-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results)).
The market appears to treat lower fee capture as erosion; what it may be missing is that lower-fee
automated products can deepen adoption and market share while transaction, subscription and
market-data revenue compound together.

## 6. The case against

Tradeweb's network may be adding volume faster than economic value. In Q2, ADV rose 18.2% but
transaction fees grew only 8.3%; total variable fees per million fell 7.2%, cash-credit fees fell
11.4%, and growth skewed toward lower-fee short-tenor derivatives, European credit and portfolio
trading. If that mix shift persists, impressive volume and share statistics can coexist with
structurally weaker monetisation, while competition and client bargaining power stop margin
expansion ([Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1758730/000175873026000133/tw-20260630.htm),
[Q2 release](https://investors.tradeweb.com/static-files/e5696c92-ff61-4d32-85b0-678deedf8d9a)).

Why this is not the 7532.T mistake again: this is not a generic bet on electronic trading or
volatility. The case starts with four filed years of revenue and GAAP margin progression, admits
that two local capital-return measures are unavailable, uses the multiple only as a gate, and
tests the exact weakness in the latest filing—whether volume becomes transaction revenue while
electronic market share continues to rise.
