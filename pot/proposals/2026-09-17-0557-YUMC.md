## 2. Thesis

My inference is that these existing operations and the cost change merit a small investment at today's price; the claimed opportunity is not a broad China-consumption rebound, and competitive pricing remains an open test.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none. T1–T3 do not directly describe the restaurant business; Alibaba's AI tools are not an assumed Yum China advantage.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** delivery-platform competition forces lower tickets and higher fulfilment costs, so rising transaction counts fail to create lasting restaurant profit.

**Earliest observable:** quarterly brand margins and same-store sales in Yum China's Q3 release; Alibaba's quarterly Quick Commerce revenue and unit-economics commentary provide the independent channel check above. Alibaba's broader category also includes groceries, so its growth is not restaurant market share. Both are observable within two quarters; Rule 4 adds no second size cut.

Pizza Hut's Q2 ticket average fell **11%** while its restaurant margin declined **40 basis points**. That is an adverse baseline, not a risk disproved by more transactions ([Q2 release](https://ir.yumchina.com/static-files/bca2bcb5-0485-4d34-9000-72195675bc86)). Refinancing the brand purchase can absorb the royalty benefit, and sterling returns still depend on USD and the underlying RMB business. The cash multiple is not in its cheapest decile.
