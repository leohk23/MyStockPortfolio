### MWA — first to revisit, but the margin bridge is incomplete

Mueller manufactures valves, hydrants, meters and related equipment used by water utilities and
construction customers. It earns money from selling those products and associated services.
The [Q3 release](https://ir.muellerwaterproducts.com/press-releases/2026/08-05-2026-211118748)
reports 4.1% sales growth, driven by pricing while volumes were slightly lower; tariff refunds
and reduced incentive compensation also helped profit. Management raised adjusted EBITDA guidance
to $367m–$372m. That is evidence of current earnings, but does not isolate repeatable improvement
from temporary assistance.

**Theses: none. Dominant risk: financial — temporary price/cost and expense benefits are being
capitalised as a lasting margin improvement.** The local absence of a `oneOff` flag does not settle
this question. The filing bridge in P5 corrects the margin, and still shows improvement: rejecting
the local label does not erase that improvement. However, I could not quantify a clean tariff-refund
and compensation bridge from the retrieved disclosures. I therefore do not claim that the stored
16.30× “recurring” P/E establishes the price of sustainable earnings.

The reason to wait is the combination of unproven margin persistence and an inadequately reconciled
denominator, not a new rule that any data discrepancy prohibits a purchase. At the next result,
check volume, price, tariff refunds, incentive compensation and GAAP operating profit separately.
An EBITDA guidance miss alone would detect the problem too late if temporary benefits keep the
headline green. All interpretation above is this run's judgement.

**Theses: T2, because automating trading workflows changes how execution is purchased; T1 is a

are in P5. This is not proof TW is expensive; it means its stored recurring percentile is not a

### REL.L — cheapness may be T2 being priced

RELX sells risk analytics, legal research tools, scientific information and exhibition services.
**Theses: T2 directly bears on legal and scientific workflows; T1 may lower AI input costs for both
RELX and substitutes. Dominant risk: secular — agents reduce the value of paid research seats.**

Local reported P/E is **20.58×**, versus its **34.10× reported-history median**, a **39.7% discount**;
the comparison uses the same reported basis. Local recurring P/E is 20.43×. These are observations,
not an attribution of the entire discount to AI. My hypothesis is that the market is discounting
weaker long-run pricing and customer economics, of which T2 is one plausible cause.

There is evidence against immediate displacement: H1 Legal underlying revenue grew 10% and adjusted
operating profit grew 13%. But growth does not separate units, price and AI attach, so it does not
establish that the long-run discount is wrong. Source:
[RELX H1 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/929869/000092986926000054/relx-20260723xex99d1.htm).
Outside RELX, Harvey reported more than **25,000 customer-built agents on 25 March 2026**:
[Harvey announcement](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-raises-growth-round-at-dollar11-billion-valuation-co-led-by-gic-and-sequoia).
That is substitute adoption, not a count of lost Lexis customers. Its publication cadence is
unscheduled, and it supplies no causal crosswalk to paid RELX seats.

I cannot presently write the required early, dated displacement test or an evidence-backed
rebuttal of that discount. Rule 4 would halve the allocation if a proposal otherwise cleared;
halving does not supply the missing Rule 5 argument. RELX is therefore deferred on the evidence,
not automatically excluded for belonging to T2, and not rejected because an older draft exists.

## 6. The case against holding cash

**Theses: none for cash itself.** T1 and T2 were engaged for relevant businesses above; neither
is a reason to reject unrelated companies or a guarantee that cash is the best investment.

**Dominant risk (financial): opportunity cost — the strongest businesses can rerate before the
missing evidence arrives.** MWA's corrected margins still improved, Tradeweb still has a profitable
transaction network, and RELX's current legal growth is evidence in its favour. Waiting for
perfect disclosure would become a permanent excuse; the next review must test whether the named
gaps can be resolved, not merely repeat them. Price appreciation alone would not validate the
earnings claims this run disputes.

**Earliest observable:** the next regular-session marks and the scheduled 9 September reassessment
can show the immediate cost of waiting, while the company releases in P3 can resolve the business
questions. No cash yield is assumed. Cash also remains available if the VIX standing order fires.

**7.49%** of market value; MWA, TW and REL.L are absent from the actual holdings. Those facts are
disclosures, not ranking penalties. Buying US names would add to an already large geographic
exposure even where it adds a new business. Sources: [book weights](../../signals.json),
