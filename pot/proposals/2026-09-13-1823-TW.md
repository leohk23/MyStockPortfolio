# TW — Tradeweb Markets Inc.

Ranked first in the [run report for this cycle](2026-09-13-1823-ranking.md), which accounts for all
93 watchlist names.

**What it does:** Tradeweb runs electronic marketplaces where institutions trade bonds, interest-rate
swaps, ETFs and money-market instruments. Asset managers, hedge funds, insurers and central banks
sit on one side and dealer banks on the other, and Tradeweb matches them through a set of trading
protocols — request-for-quote, click-to-trade, portfolio trading and session-based auctions. It
earns a fee on each executed trade, mostly variable and scaled to the notional amount traded, plus
smaller fixed subscription and market-data fees.

## 2. Thesis

Institutional fixed income is still moving off the telephone and onto screens, and Tradeweb is where
a large share of that flow has gone: FY2025 revenue grew **18.92% to $2,052.4m** and filed operating
margin rose for a third consecutive year, **37.05% to 39.73% to 41.18%**, which is growth and
improving profitability at the same time rather than one bought with the other. Why now is that the
market has re-rated the franchise from a 53.15–66.39× band in FY2024 and 35.79–64.63× in FY2025 to
**23.09–36.39× so far in FY2026**, a halving of the multiple inside two fiscal years while those
filed accounts were being produced. What the market may be missing is on the balance sheet rather
than the income statement: **asset turnover rose from 0.237× to 0.251×** as revenue grew 18.92%, so
the network was extended without a proportionate call on capital — modest in absolute terms, but
moving in the right direction in the one capital measure this company discloses.

## 3. Falsifier

**Warning** — at the Q3 2026 results, a **confirmed** date of **29 October 2026**. Any one of these
triggers a re-read, not a sale:

- Transaction-fee revenue growth trails total ADV growth by **15 percentage points or more**. The
  Q2 2026 baseline is ADV **+18.2%** against transaction-fee revenue **+8.3%**, a gap of about 10
  points, so this asks whether an existing gap is widening.
- Cash-credit fees per million fall **15% or more** year on year. Q2 2026 was **−11.4%**.
- Filed GAAP operating margin below **38%**, against 41.18% in FY2025.

All three are revenue-per-unit-of-volume or profitability measures rather than market-share
measures, deliberately: the dominant risk in P6 is that Tradeweb keeps winning the market while the
toll on it shrinks, and a share statistic would stay green throughout that.

**Break** — assessed at the FY2026 10-K, checked no later than **1 March 2027**. Either route alone
calls for exit under §5.1, and they must not be required to fire together:

- **(a)** Total transaction-fee revenue is flat or falling year on year in **both Q3 and Q4 2026**
  while total ADV grows in each. That is the toll shrinking on a growing network, which is the
  dominant risk arriving in the accounts.
- **(b)** Filed GAAP operating margin below **35% in both quarters**, independent of volumes. This
  route exists so that growth in another asset class cannot conceal lost pricing in credit.

**External evidence (Rule 3).** The dominant risk is `secular`, so it is checked outside Tradeweb's
own filings as well as inside them:

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** [T2](../theses.md#t2--ai-agents-compress-the-value-of-seat-priced-workflow-software)
bears, on its **tailwind** side — Tradeweb charges per unit of notional executed and has never sold
a seat, which is T2's own description of who gets paid when buyers stop paying for seats.
[T1](../theses.md#t1--chinese-open-weight-models-reach-rough-parity-with-us-frontier-models-at-a-fraction-of-the-price)
and [T3](../theses.md#t3--ai-agents-create-a-control-layer-and-regulation-makes-it-compulsory) do
not bear: Tradeweb neither sells nor resells models, and it is not a governance vendor. A tailwind
thesis is not a reason to buy and is declared here so it cannot quietly become one.

**Dominant risk (`secular`):** electronification compresses the price of an executed trade faster
than it grows the volume executed, so Tradeweb can go on winning the market while the revenue per
unit of what it wins falls — Q2 2026 already shows ADV **+18.2%** against transaction-fee revenue
**+8.3%**, with cash-credit fees per million down **11.4%**.

**Earliest observable:** the transaction-fee-to-ADV growth gap and cash-credit fees per million, in
the Q3 release on **29 October 2026** — six weeks away and well inside two quarters, so Rule 4's
timing reduction does not apply. The half size comes from §7.2a and the missing capital measures
instead, as P1 states.

Then the secondary arguments, and they are not weak ones:

- **The trend is already against the thesis, not merely a risk to it.** Fees per million have been
  falling for several quarters and MarketAxess's Q2 shows the same category-wide shape. A proposal
  that buys into a visible compression is betting that operating leverage outruns it — a bet the
  falsifier in P3 tests directly and which this document deliberately does not assert in P2.
- **Two of three capital measures are blank.** Leo's §2.4 extension is explicit that a margin is
  profit over sales and he invests capital. For TW the repo gives only asset turnover, and at
  **0.251×** that is low in absolute terms: the balance sheet is heavy with goodwill and intangibles
  from acquisitions, so a large part of the asset base is purchase accounting rather than working
  capital. I cannot say what the capital earns, and I have not substituted the 41.18% margin for it.
- **A 41% operating margin is a question, not a comfort.** It has risen three years running in a
  business whose unit price is falling, which is exactly the combination that invites a competitor
  to price below it. The P3 break at 35% exists for that.
- **Related-party dependency.** LSEG is both a large shareholder and a distribution and data
  partner. That relationship is a material part of how Tradeweb reaches customers and prices data,
  and its renewal terms are not a figure this repo carries.
- **Combined exposure with the human book: none.** `holdings.json` holds no Tradeweb and no
  fixed-income venue. The nearest adjacencies are Blackrock at 1.86% and Visa at 1.28% of the book,
  both different businesses. This buy therefore adds a genuinely new exposure rather than doubling
  one, which is the one thing §3.3's warning about overlap is not triggered by here.
- **Concentration within the pot.** At £75.39 this would be the pot's largest position at 15.1% of
  contributed capital, ahead of MWA's 10.6%. Inside every limit, and worth seeing.

**Why this is not 7532.T again.** That regret was a top-line rebound story bought without the
fundamentals, the valuation, the forward outlook or the currency. Here the case is filed margin over
three years, filed revenue growth, and the one capital measure the repo has; the valuation is a
filter this name passed and is deliberately not the argument; the forward test is a dated company
disclosure six weeks out; and the currency exposure is a straight USD ticket priced at a stated FX
rate. What is genuinely uncertain — what the capital earns — is named in P1 and paid for with half
the position rather than argued away.
