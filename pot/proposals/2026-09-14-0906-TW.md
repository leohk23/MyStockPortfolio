## 6. The case against

**Theses:** **T2**, automation can lower the price of execution and associated workflow services even while activity grows; Tradeweb's transaction business is an adjacent exposure, not a pure seat-licence business. T1's model-price thesis and T3's mandated-control thesis are not material premises here.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** Rivals and cheaper automated protocols take fee income faster than electronic trading expands the addressable activity.

**Earliest observable:** September market share, checked by 8 October, followed by Q3 fee yield and credit revenue on 29 October. The competitor fee decline above is already an external observation. ICE's [announced acquisition of MarketAxess](https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2026/Intercontinental-Exchange-to-Acquire-MarketAxess-Creating-a-Premier-Fixed-Income-Marketplace/default.aspx) proposes combining execution with data and analytics; it is pending, and its competitive benefit is not yet established.

August portfolio trading grew faster than overall credit trading and carries lower fees ([August release](https://investors.tradeweb.com/news-releases/news-release-details/tradeweb-reports-august-2026-total-trading-volume-612-trillion)). This could leave volume strong while economics weaken, which is why P3 tests both. The reported multiple is **56.3% below** its own historical median, but part of that apparent discount comes from investment gains. T2 may explain another part: I am buying a small test of operating growth at 26.77× adjusted earnings, not asserting a return to the old 55.44× reported median. The fee decline is evidence against that test.

**Combined exposure:** No TW listing appears in [public holdings](../../holdings.json), so direct human-book exposure is 0%; this adds financial-market activity to a human book already **66.95% US** ([signals.json](../../signals.json)). That overlap disclosure does not alter the rank.
