## 6. The case against

The share may be cheap because earnings are already rolling over. H1 adjusted order intake fell
10%, sales fell 13%, EBITDA fell 23%, and FY2026 revenue guidance of CHF410m–CHF420m is roughly
flat with FY2025 and below the mid-term growth range. Utility customers have excess inventory and
installation bottlenecks, while the higher power-transformer mix takes longer to produce; backlog
can look reassuring for years before becoming cash, and factory delays would hurt revenue and
margins ([H1 update](https://ir.the-rsgroup.com/news/rands-group-provides-trading-update-for-h1-2026/bb41967c-ac88-4041-be50-200b3f26951b)).

The accounting deserves equal scepticism. Much of FY2025's reported growth came from the first full
year of Kyte Powertech; CHF180.7m of acquisition goodwill was written directly against equity under
Swiss GAAP FER, and the report's theoretical capitalization view shows CHF131.4m residual Kyte
goodwill and CHF36.1m of 2025 Kyte goodwill amortisation that did not pass through reported profit
([2025 annual report](https://ir.the-rsgroup.com/media/document/0fec9fb0-b91a-4deb-aef1-5cb2afb265d8/assets/RSG_Annual_report_2025_def.pdf?disposition=inline)).
This is also a short listed history—179 weeks—and a small SIX line where the spread and a second
commission can matter. The strongest bear case is that the market correctly sees peak transformer
margins, acquisition-flattered growth and an expensive capacity build meeting normalized utility
demand; the 12.70× multiple is only a gate, not an answer.
