## 6. The case against

The backlog may be less economically firm than it looks. H1 adjusted order intake fell 10%, sales
fell 13%, EBITDA fell 23%, and FY2026 revenue guidance is roughly flat with FY2025 and below the
mid-term growth range; utility customers have excess inventory and installation bottlenecks, while
the higher power-transformer mix takes longer to produce. A record CHF357.9m backlog can therefore
sit for years, be postponed or consume working capital before becoming revenue and cash
([H1 update](https://ir.the-rsgroup.com/news/rands-group-provides-trading-update-for-h1-2026/bb41967c-ac88-4041-be50-200b3f26951b)).

Much of FY2025's reported growth came from the Kyte acquisition, the 2025 margin already fell from
2024, and 143 public-market weeks have not seen a full transformer cycle. The strongest bear case
is that the market correctly sees peak margins, acquisition-flattered growth and an expensive
capacity build meeting normalized utility demand; if the backlog does not convert at the guided
sales and margin, the 12.78× recurring multiple is a value trap rather than an answer.
