## 2. Thesis

Halma is one of the few businesses on this watchlist that is growing quickly and getting more productive on its capital at the same time. FY2026 revenue rose 14.9% and operating profit 28.4%. Operating margin went from 18.1% to 20.4% over three years. ROIC rose every year for four years, 10.1%, 11.1%, 11.3%, 12.5%, while gross profit over assets stayed at 33–35%. That consistency across fifty unrelated niches is what a decentralised acquirer is supposed to produce and rarely does. The market has it at 34.8× trailing earnings, at the 9th percentile of its own 173-week history (median 37.8×), down 1.4% over a year while operating profit rose 28%. What the price may not credit is that this year's growth came with higher returns on capital, not lower.

## 3. Falsifier

**Warning**, read at the **19 November 2026** half-year results. Re-read if any of these happens:

- Group organic constant-currency revenue growth for H1 is below **8%**.
- Management cuts its full-year organic-growth outlook or its adjusted EBIT margin range.
- The photonics business shows materially less growth than the roughly 30% the 24 September update built into the outlook. "Materially less" means below **15%**, or not disclosed at all. Not disclosed counts as unresolved, not as a pass.

Either of the first two is a miss against management's own guidance. The third tests the customer directly.

**Break.** Propose a sale under §5.1 on either of these:

1. The **FY2027 annual report** (results expected June 2027, checked by **30 June 2027**) shows the largest customer's share of group revenue fallen **below 12%**, with group organic revenue growth **below 5%**. That means the customer cut back and the rest of the group did not replace it.
2. Any Halma filing discloses the loss of, or a material reduction in orders from, that customer, and the same or the next report shows **sector organic revenue down year on year**.

Separately, and secondary: net debt/adjusted EBITDA above **2.0×** at any reporting date. That is the top of Halma's own stated operating range, so acquisitions would then be outrunning cash.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** T1, because the largest customer buys optical switches, which is AI-datacentre equipment by description. T1 says inference volume grows while model pricing collapses, and a hyperscaler whose model revenue is squeezed can cut infrastructure spending. T2 and T3: none, since Halma sells no seat-priced software or agent control.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** one customer, about 20% of revenue and rising, insources, dual-sources or pauses its optical-switch orders. The photonics growth the outlook depends on then reverses.

**Earliest observable:** the 19 November interim, for sector and photonics organic growth against the roughly 30% assumption. Outside Halma's filings, the large US cloud companies' quarterly capex, filed in their 10-Qs at the end of October and again at the end of January. A cut there comes before Halma's own numbers move. Both are within two quarters, so no Rule 4 halving.

This is a concentration Halma never used to have. It sits uneasily with the reason anyone owns Halma, which is fifty small unrelated niches. If that customer is a large cloud company, Leo is also buying more of a capex cycle his book is already 59.5% exposed to through Technology (40.3%) and Communication Services (19.1%) ([signals.json](../../signals.json) `book.bySector`). This run could not read the customer's name in anything, so that link is an inference, not a fact.

**Secondary arguments.**

- **The multiple is not low.** 34.8× is cheap only against Halma's own history, which has always been dear. If the market decides a UK compounder is worth 25×, that is a 28% fall with nothing wrong in the business.
- **Acquisitions.** The 05:58 run worked out core cash of £352.0m and £−108.8m after deals for FY2026. Growth is partly bought, and the net debt/EBITDA test above is there for that.
- **FY2026 includes a licence profit** that the adjusted EPS does not strip out.
- **Overlap with Leo's book:** Halma is not among the human book's named holdings, so this adds a new name, not more weight on an existing one.
