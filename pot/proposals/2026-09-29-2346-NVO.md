## 2. Thesis

Novo earns an adjusted operating margin of 44.6% (H1 2026) and a 31% return on invested capital,
and turned out DKK 55.3bn of free cash flow in six months. It sits in a category whose volume is
still growing 80%+ a year, and it has a first-to-market oral obesity drug that sold DKK 3.1bn in its
second quarter. The price, 9.6× trailing earnings, a 4.8% yield and the 2.7th percentile of its own
P/E, treats those earnings as shrinking for years. Management's stated 2030 ambition is revenue
growth in line with pharma peers at a broadly stable margin, so the purchase is a bet that the
decline priced in is deeper than the business will deliver. Whether volume actually outruns price is
the question P3 answers; it is not assumed here.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none. T1 to T3 concern AI models, seat-priced software and agent governance, and none
of them touches a drug maker's pricing or volume.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** Eli Lilly's tirzepatide, and its oral successors, together with
US price concessions, push Novo's realised price down faster than its volume grows. Revenue then
shrinks inside a market whose volume is growing 80%+.

**Earliest observable:** US Operations sales growth at CER, and Wegovy pill sales, in Novo's Q3
report on **4 November 2026**. Alongside that, Lilly's own Q3 US tirzepatide revenue, published at
the same time of year. Both are in filings and dated, and both are within two quarters.

- **The trend is already against it.** US sales fell 4% at CER in H1 while US obesity volume grew
  87%. That is share and price being lost at the same time, not a market that is shrinking. Q2's
  +4% is one quarter.
- **The margin is the question, not a comfort.** 44.6% is extraordinary for a manufacturer. It
  persists only while a patent and a supply shortage let Novo price above competition. ROIC has
  already fallen from 57% to 31% in two years, as capacity spending (including the Catalent sites)
  landed before the revenue did. The US semaglutide patent runs to 2032, and management called
  exclusivity loss "the elephant in the room" at the Capital Markets Day, where the shares fell
  7.7%. That day's plan was to match peers, not to beat them.
- **Pipeline setbacks are recent.** CagriSema failed its head-to-head non-inferiority test against
  tirzepatide in February. Q2 carried DKK 6.3bn of pipeline impairments, DKK 4.0bn of it
  monlunabant. The next-generation defence of the franchise is weaker than it was a year ago.
- **The cheap multiple may be the right price.** A drug company facing patent expiry six years out
  should trade at a low multiple. At 9.6× with a 4.8% yield, the market is not wrong unless earnings
  hold roughly flat to 2030. P3's break is written to catch it if they do not.
- **Exposure:** Leo's human book does not hold Novo, so there is no overlap
  ([holdings](../../holdings.json)). Inside the pot, however, this would be the **third healthcare
  name of six**, after AZN.L and EUZ.DE. Pharma pricing policy would then hit two of the pot's
  positions at once.
- **Why this is not 7532.T again.** That purchase followed a rebound story without checking
  valuation, prospects or the currency. Here the valuation already assumes decline, the margin and
  the cash are measured from the filings, the risk named above is the one the price is discounting,
  and the falsifier reads it on the next results date. The currency is the USD line of a DKK
  reporter. Currency moves change the dollar value of Novo's earnings and are not part of this
  thesis.
