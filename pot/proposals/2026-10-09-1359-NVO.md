## 2. Thesis

**Novo guides 2026 adjusted sales and adjusted operating profit to fall 0–6% at constant exchange
rates.** That is a narrower expected decline than the 4–12% fall it guided in May, so this is a
shrinking business and the case is a recovery (§2.4b). FY2025 still delivered a 41.3% operating
margin and a 30.6% return on capital. It trades at 9.8× on both reported and company-adjusted
trailing earnings, against a 31.2× median over its 185-week window, with a 4.8% yield. Outside the
US, sales grew 10% at CER in Q2. I think the market is pricing the US price-and-share problem as a
permanent decline of the whole franchise. Growth should resume in **2027**, and the evidence will be
the 2027 outlook in the Q4 2026 report, early February 2027. P3 tests that.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none. Novo sells drugs, not seats or models. T1 to T3 do not reach its revenue line.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** Eli Lilly's tirzepatide and compounded or cash-pay alternatives
keep taking US share and price, so the US decline (Wegovy injectable −22% at CER in Q2) outruns
growth everywhere else and 2027 shrinks too.

**Earliest observable:** Lilly's Q3 10-Q (about 30 October) and Novo's Q3 report (4 November), both
inside a month. The decisive one is the 2027 outlook in early February 2027, within two quarters.

Secondary arguments, at full strength:

- **The recovery is guided, not filed.** Unlike PDD, no filed period yet shows growth returning.
  "Narrowed its expected decline" is management's word for 2026, and nothing has been said about
  2027. This is closer to Leo's 7532.T mistake than the PDD order is. The defence is only that the
  falsifier is dated and close: if 4 November or February goes the wrong way, it trips.
- **Return on capital is falling fast.** ROIC went from 56.6% in FY2023 to 30.6% in FY2025, and
  gross profit over assets from 62.5% to 46.1%. Assets nearly doubled (DKK314bn to DKK543bn) on
  capacity built for demand that has now partly gone to a rival. That capacity may earn less than it
  cost.
- **US policy pricing.** Medicare and direct-to-consumer price agreements cut the US price per
  patient regardless of share.
- **Semaglutide loses exclusivity in several markets from 2026.** Generic competition would hit
  International Operations, which is the part of the thesis carrying the growth. I have not sourced
  the dates per market.
- **Pipeline write-downs.** Q2 2026 carried DKK6.3bn of non-cash impairments, DKK4.0bn of it on
  monlunabant (from the store's evidence entry). The next obesity products are not proven.
- **Repeat proposal.** This is the seventh NVO proposal since 12 September, none bought. That
  changes nothing in the ranking. It is disclosed so Leo sees it.
