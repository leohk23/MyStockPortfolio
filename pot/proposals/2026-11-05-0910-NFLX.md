## 2. Thesis

**The business:** Netflix licenses and produces films and series and sells monthly subscriptions
to watch them, with a cheaper tier carrying advertising. Nearly all revenue is subscription fees;
advertising is expected at about $2.7bn of $51.1bn in 2026 ([Q3 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001065280/000106528026000290/ex991_q326.htm)).
Content is a largely fixed cost spread over members, so revenue growth falls through to margin.

**The engine:** Operating margin rose every year: 17.8%, 20.6%, 26.7% and 29.5% over FY2022-25,
then 34.6% in Q3 2026. Revenue grew 12.6% a year over those three years. FY2025 ROIC was
**32.6%** and asset turnover **0.81×**. FY2025 free cash flow was $9.46bn, 0.86× net income
(`earnings.json`, `prices.json` `capital`). The margin is high, so the question is whether
pricing can keep outrunning attention.

**Why now:** The shares are down 39.6% over a year. They hit their low on 30 October, after the
Q3 letter cut 2026 advertising from about $3bn to $2.7bn and acknowledged "investor concern about
our view hours growth".

**What the market is missing:** The market is pricing a fade in attention. Netflix's share of US TV
time fell from **8.7% to 7.7%** between August 2025 and August 2026, while YouTube's rose from
13.1% to 15.8% (Nielsen). On corrected history, the P/E has de-rated from a **40.7× five-year
median to 19.2×**, down 53%. The different view is that revenue per hour still outruns hours:
global view hours grew **2%** in H1 2026, while revenue grew **12%** in Q3 and the
operating-margin target holds at 31.5%.

**What has to happen:** Revenue must keep growing at least 8% a year on flat to slightly falling
hours, and global hours must not shrink. P3 tests both.

## 3. Falsifier

**Warning** (re-read, not a sale). **Already tripped at entry on one month:** Nielsen's August 2026
Netflix share of 7.7% is **11.5% below** August 2025's 8.7%. It stays active until a comparable
month is back within 5% of its year-ago share. It re-trips on any of these:

- Nielsen's combined September and October 2026 release shows Netflix **5% or more below** the
  same months of 2025. Nielsen is changing its methodology from September. If its release gives no
  comparable year-ago figure, the warning counts as **cannot tell**, not as a pass.
- Q4 2026 revenue comes in below the **$13.4bn** guided in the Q3 letter.
- Netflix lowers its 2026 advertising expectation below **$2.7bn**.

**Break** (sell under §5.1), checked at the Q4 2026 letter and the 2026 engagement report, **by
31 March 2027**. Any one of these breaks it:

1. **Hours shrink.** Netflix's 2026 engagement report shows total hours viewed below 2025's
   **191bn**: 95bn in H1 plus 96bn in H2, from Netflix's two 2025 reports.
2. **Monetisation stops outrunning attention.** FY2027 revenue guidance in the Q4 letter implies
   F/X-neutral growth **below 8%**, against 12% for 2026.
3. **Sustained share loss.** Two consecutive comparable Nielsen releases after August 2026 show
   Netflix **10% or more below** the same month a year earlier, and revenue growth in the quarter
   they fall in is below 10%.

The break is not on margin. A margin can be defended for a while by cutting content spend while
attention leaks away (the Q3 letter's Q4 guide already includes headcount reductions). Tests 1
and 3 move as soon as viewing moves, without waiting for revenue to miss.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** T1 bears at one remove. Cheap open-weight video models lower the cost of generated and
creator video that competes for viewing time. They also lower Netflix's own production cost, as a
buyer of AI. T2 and T3 do not apply: Netflix sells consumer access, not workflow seats or control.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** YouTube and creator or generated video take viewing time faster
than price rises and advertising can make up, so revenue growth fades and pricing power goes with it.

**Earlier risks:**
- `competitive` ([2026-09-09-2039-NFLX](archive/2026-09-09-2039-NFLX.md)): **still dominant**.
  It is the same risk, and it has moved the wrong way since: August's Nielsen share is 11.5%
  lower year on year, the first month to cross that proposal's 10% line. P3 tests it externally.

**Earliest observable:** Nielsen's combined September and October 2026 release, due "later this
year" (under two months). Then the Q4 letter, estimated 19 January 2027, and Netflix's 2026
engagement report, which moves to once a year from Q1 2027. All three fall within two quarters, so
Rule 4 does not apply.

**Secondary arguments, at full strength:**

- **The warning is already lit.** The pot is buying into its own falsifier's first tier. The
  earlier proposal set its break at three consecutive months 10% down, and August is the first.
  July 2026's Netflix share is not in Nielsen's release, so I cannot tell whether the run started
  earlier.
- **Hours are flat.** +2% global hours with +12% revenue is a price-and-mix story, the same pattern
  T2 describes for software: units flatten while price carries growth. It can last years, but it
  does not compound for ever.
- **Advertising is behind plan.** Advertising was meant to monetise the same hours a second time,
  and 2026 was cut by 10%.
- **Content can protect the margin until it can't.** Q4's guide includes headcount cuts. Margin
  bought by spending less on content would show up later as weaker engagement, which is exactly
  what P3 watches.
- **The fee flatters 2026.** It is in EPS, cash and the 2026 FCF guide. Ex-fee, the P/E is 21–24×,
  at or just outside the decile on corrected history.
- **Nielsen is US-only, and its methodology changes from September.** P3's sharpest external test
  may not be comparable at the next print.

**17.04%** and the US **63.85%** (`signals.json` `book`). One share adds a little to an exposure
