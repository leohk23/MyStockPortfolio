## 2. Thesis

**The business:** PDD runs the Pinduoduo and Temu marketplaces, charging merchants for advertising and transaction services. This is a recovery proposal: audited FY2025 operating profit **fell 14.1%**. It is not a claim that the latest full year was growing.

**The engine:** Filed operating margins were 23.7%, 27.5% and 21.6% in 2023–25: substantial but inconsistent. Current-method FY2025 ROIC is 26.9%. H1 2026 operating profit subsequently rose 13.0%, while operating cash exceeded net income. Those are separate tests of operating recovery and cash backing; neither proves that merchants will keep paying.

**Why now:** Two reported quarters show an operating recovery after the annual setback. The next quarterly disclosure can test its continuation before committing more capital. The opportunity is to own a recovering merchant-services business, rather than extrapolate the share-price fall or a Chinese consumer rebound.

**What the market is missing:** My inference is that the price allows little credit for the operating recovery because reported net profit is still falling. I assign value to that divergence, without treating the unexplained losses below operating profit as temporary or assuming PDD wins the subsidy contest.

**What has to happen:** Merchant-advertising revenue must remain growing, and FY2026 operating profit must exceed audited FY2025. The proposal fails if that recovery does not reach the filed accounts by March 2027.

Figures and their reconciliation are in P5. The merchant-retention question remains open and is tested in P3, not assumed here.

## 3. Falsifier

**Warning:** Re-read at the first Q3 release, with an analyst check on **30 November 2026**, if any of these occurs:

- Quarterly online-marketing-services revenue growth is **below 2% year on year**, against 3.47% in Q2.
- Its growth trails **Alibaba's same-quarter, like-for-like China customer-management revenue growth by at least 3 percentage points**. Compare the quarter, not PDD's year-to-date figure with Alibaba's quarter. This is a competitive-spending proxy, not a market-share calculation.
- Q3 operating profit fails to grow year on year, or nine-month operating cash flow is below nine-month net income.

The external source is **Alibaba's quarterly results**, customer-management revenue and any merchant-incentive accounting bridge. Its [June-quarter release](https://data.alibabagroup.com/ecms-files/1532295521/fa5d65fc-9b3e-4e82-a8fc-4ce1c3e2c407/Alibaba%20Group%20Announces%20June%20Quarter%202026%20Results.pdf) reports −7% customer-management revenue but **+1% like for like**, excluding its new merchant-incentive programme; quick-commerce revenue rose 45%. Using −7% without that bridge would flatter PDD. Recheck both companies each quarter. If the comparison or release is unavailable by 30 November, record the test as unresolved and re-read; never mark it passed.

**Break:** Recommend exit under §5.1 if **online-marketing-services revenue is flat or falling year on year in both Q3 and Q4 2026**, regardless of transaction-service growth or group margin. Independently, the recovery fails if **FY2026 reported operating profit is no higher than RMB93.102131bn**, tested on the annual release and audited filing. Review by **31 March 2027**; a missing release is unresolved evidence requiring review, not a fabricated failure or pass.

These are analyst thresholds, not management guidance. A slowing marketing line or relative shortfall can warn before two quarters of contraction. Price increases could still conceal falling merchant numbers: PDD does not supply a sufficiently current active-merchant series here. That residual blind spot is another reason not to use a full allocation.

**Earliest observable:** PDD's next quarterly online-marketing growth, and Alibaba's same-quarter customer-management growth after its accounting bridge. Both are quarterly issuer disclosures; the next publication dates have not been verified as announced. The first scheduled check is 30 November. The historical 3.47% versus +1% comparison is not evidence that future competition is won. Secondary risks are substantial. The ADS is an interest in a holding company with contractual VIE arrangements, not direct ownership of the Chinese operating assets. The annual/preliminary discrepancy and other-loss bucket justify the smaller position; explaining them could change the valuation materially.
