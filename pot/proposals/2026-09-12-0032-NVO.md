# NVO — Novo Nordisk A/S (New York-listed ADR)

**What it does:** Novo Nordisk discovers, manufactures and sells prescription medicines, overwhelmingly
injectable and oral peptides. Two product families carry it: GLP-1 medicines for type 2 diabetes and for
obesity (Ozempic, Rybelsus, Wegovy, including an oral Wegovy tablet), and insulins. It sells to national
health systems, hospital groups and, in the United States, to insurers and pharmacy benefit managers
through rebated list prices and increasingly direct to patients for cash; it manufactures the active
ingredient and the devices in its own plants in Denmark, the US and France. Revenue is patented drug sales;
cost is a very large and still-growing factory estate.

## 2. Thesis

Novo Nordisk has earned a **41–44% operating margin in each of the last four filed years** — 42.3%, 44.2%,
44.2%, 41.3% on revenue that compounded from DKK 176.9bn to DKK 309.1bn, a 20.4% annual rate — and it earned
that on capital, not just on sales: **ROIC 30.6%, gross profit over total assets 46.1%, asset turnover 0.569×
in FY2025**, after four years in which total assets more than doubled to DKK 542.9bn to build the capacity
the GLP-1 category needs. Why now: the company has raised its own 2026 outlook twice this year, most
recently on **4 August 2026** from −4%/−12% to **0% to −6% at CER** on both adjusted sales and adjusted
operating profit, and Q2 adjusted operating profit grew **+11% CER** after Q1's −6% — while the shares
trade at **12.3–12.9× recurring earnings against a 31.0× five-year median**, a 60% de-rating.

What the market may be missing is arithmetic rather than opinion: the de-rating prices the loss of this
category's economics as already decided, and the filed accounts do not yet contain it. The operating margin
in FY2025 was one point below FY2022's. The decline the company itself guides for 2026 is nought to six per
cent, not a collapse. And the two most recent quarters are distorted in **opposite** directions — a DKK 26.8bn
non-cash 340B provision reversal inflates Q1 2026, DKK 6.3bn of pipeline impairments depress Q2 2026 — so the
screen that flagged this name and the headlines written about it are reading two different companies, neither
of which is the one filing the accounts. Correcting both is what P5 does, and it leaves the name still in the
cheapest part of its own range rather than removing the reason to look.

explanation whichever rule is or is not in force.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** Eli Lilly's orally dosed orforglipron and its next-generation injectable
take the marginal obesity patient, and Novo defends on price — so US prescription volumes hold or grow while
realised net price per script falls faster, contracting revenue and the 41% operating margin together.

**Earliest observable:** Novo's own Q3 2026 result, giving reported North America Operations obesity-care
sales at CER and the company's volume-versus-price split — locally dated **4 November 2026**
([prices.json](../../prices.json), `estimate: true`, so my deadline is 6 November). Eli Lilly's Q3 2026

so Rule 4 does not size this down**; the half allocation in P1 comes from §7.2a and nothing else.

Then the secondary arguments, written to persuade:

- **The de-rating may be right and early rather than wrong.** Novo's ROIC has fallen in a straight line for
  three years — 56.6% → 41.1% → 30.6% — while total assets more than doubled. A margin of 41% invites
  competition by definition, and this is what the first stage of that looks like on the balance sheet. The
  P2 case rests on returns that are, on the record, in decline; "still 30%" is a comfort, not a trend.
- **US pricing is under attack from more directions than Lilly.** The 340B reversal in P5 is itself evidence
  of how much of Novo's US revenue is a negotiated rebate rather than a price, and most-favoured-nation
  pricing proposals and direct-to-consumer cash channels all push the same way. A company whose reported
  sales can move DKK 26.8bn on a provision estimate is a company whose realised price is not a fact you can
  read off a label.
- **Patent expiry is dated and known.** Semaglutide's protection lapses first outside the US — Canada and
  China from 2026 — before the US cliff early in the next decade. Generic entry in large non-US markets is
  not a risk to discover; it is a schedule.
- **The management change and the pipeline.** DKK 4.0bn of the Q2 impairment was monlunabant, a compound the
  company was presenting as a pipeline asset a year ago. Writing off your own next thing is the most
  expensive sentence in a results release.
- **What this does to Leo's total exposure — the §3.3 disclosure.** Novo Nordisk appears **nowhere** in the
  human book: it is not in `signals.json` → `book.names`, which prices 60 of 60 positions totalling
  £128,690, nor in `byCompany`. So this adds a **new** exposure rather than concentrating an existing one —
  the opposite of NVDA's case, which sits at 7.15% of the book and has been proposed seven times. On

  depositary receipt is a European economic exposure in a US-listed wrapper. Healthcare does not appear in
