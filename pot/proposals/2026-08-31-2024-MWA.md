## 2. Thesis

Mueller sells mission-critical valves, hydrants, metering and leak-detection equipment into North American water networks, an installed-base business where replacement and reliability matter more than fashion. Why now: four locally filed years show revenue rising every year and GAAP operating margin widening from 10.1% to 18.8%, while Q3 FY2026 still delivered sales growth and a 20.4% GAAP operating margin ([earnings.json](../../earnings.json), [Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1350593/000135059326000034/exhibit991pressreleasefy26.htm)). The market appears to treat the margin gain as temporary pricing and tariff help; what it may be missing is that portfolio work, foundry investment and a larger Water Management contribution have reset normalized economics upward, which FY2027 will test ([Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1350593/000135059326000036/mwa-20260630.htm)).

## 6. The case against

The margin expansion may be a peak, not a reset. Q3 gross profit explicitly benefited from non-recurring tariff refunds and pricing while volumes were slightly lower; SG&A also benefited from lower incentive compensation, and Water Flow Solutions sales fell 0.6%. If those aids reverse while inflation, foundry capex and municipal or residential demand soften, a low-looking P/E is simply capitalising temporarily elevated earnings, and both earnings and the multiple can fall together ([Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1350593/000135059326000034/exhibit991pressreleasefy26.htm), [Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1350593/000135059326000036/mwa-20260630.htm)).

Why this is not the 7532.T mistake again: the order is not based on a water-infrastructure theme or rebound story. It rests on four filed years of revenue, GAAP margin and cash evidence, applies valuation only as a gate, and makes the strongest accounting objection—temporary margin support—the exact subject of both falsifier tiers.
