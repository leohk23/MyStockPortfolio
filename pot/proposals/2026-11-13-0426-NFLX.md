## 2. Thesis

**The business:** Netflix sells access to films, series and live entertainment by subscription, and sells advertisements on its cheaper plan. Its content spending supports a service sold across many markets.

**The engine:** Local FY2022–25 operating margins are 17.8%, 20.6%, 26.7% and 29.5%; revenue compounded at 12.6% annually. FY2025 ROIC is 32.6%. Spreading content cost across a larger paying audience explains operating leverage; sustaining the audience is a condition to test, not a presumed moat.

**Why now:** The lower limit permits a new, small entry under D93. The independent annual cash screen predates the termination fee. The business has not suddenly become safer because the share price fell.

**What the market is missing:** The price implies a substantial fade in the economics of attention. Reported P/E is 55.5% below its historical median, but the fee exaggerates that discount. My different view is conditional: the international service can retain enough engagement to monetise its content base. The [H1 engagement release](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2026) records over 97bn hours; falling US share is contrary evidence, not explained away.

**What has to happen:** Comparable global engagement must hold and audience-share losses must not persist. These observations, rather than management's confidence about pricing, determine whether this small position deserves to remain.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** T1, indirectly: cheaper models can lower the cost of competing video content. This is a possible mechanism, not evidence that AI has caused the observed audience loss. T2 and T3 do not directly describe this consumer subscription business.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** competing entertainment takes viewing time faster than Netflix can preserve its customer economics.

**Earlier risks:** `competitive` ([2026-11-05-0910-NFLX](2026-11-05-0910-NFLX.md)): **still dominant**. The August warning is retained, and P3 preserves the examiner's revenue-independent audience break and 193bn-hour floor. No waiting call is treated as passed.

**Earliest observable:** Nielsen's September/October release, expected before year-end without a promised day, followed by Netflix's annual engagement report in Q1 2027. The publisher confirms the cadence. Both are within two quarters; unavailable comparators stay unresolved.

Advertising expectations have weakened, and higher pricing can temporarily conceal fewer hours. Content amortisation and the fee make reported earnings an incomplete description of recurring cash. The US audience measure covers one market and changes methodology; global hours are reported only annually. The small order accepts those limitations rather than making margin the audience falsifier.
