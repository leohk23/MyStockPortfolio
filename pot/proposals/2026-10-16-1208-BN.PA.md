## 2. Thesis

**This is a recovery case. Danone's FY2025 reported operating profit fell 13.0%, to €2,940m from €3,379m, and reported sales fell 0.3%.** The first half of 2026 is the first evidence of recovery. Reported operating income rose 7.6%, to €1,693m from €1,573m. Like-for-like sales grew 3.5%, 1.7 points of it from volume and mix rather than price. Management guides 2026 like-for-like sales **up 3% to 5%**, with recurring operating income growing faster than sales. The market prices it at 20.2× trailing reported EPS and 10.4× operating cash flow, both near the bottom of its own 186-week history. The share price is down 21% over a year. What the market may be missing is that the decline was in reported profit while volume has turned positive, which is the order a real recovery happens in.

**When growth resumes, and what shows it:** FY2026 reported IFRS operating profit above FY2025's €2,940m, in the FY2026 results (expected late February 2027). P3's break tests exactly that.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none. Danone sells food, water and nutrition, not software or models, so no thesis in pot/theses.md bears on it.

**Dominant risk (`financial`):** the profit recovery does not reach cash. Working capital, capex and acquisitions, including the Huel consolidation, absorb it, so recurring earnings grow while free cash flow falls and net debt rises.

**Earliest observable:** already printed. H1 FCF −27.3% and net debt +€544m are in the H1 release. The next cash read is the FY2026 statement (late February 2027, within two quarters). The 28 October sales update tests demand, not cash.

**Secondary arguments.**

- **The cheap multiple may be the market pricing a slow-growth staple correctly.** 36.8× was the median of a period with distorted earnings: FY2022–23 reported EPS of €1.48 and €1.36 after impairments. A 20× reported multiple is cheap against that history, but it is not cheap in absolute terms for a company with ROIC around 8–9%.
- **The recurring basis excludes costs shareholders actually pay.** Danone's reported-to-recurring gap has been large every year. 16.0× recurring is only the right multiple if those exclusions really stop.
- **Category risk.** Weight-loss drugs reducing packaged-food volumes, private label in Europe, and Chinese infant-formula demand are all outside the company's control. None is visible in H1's positive volume yet.
- **Overlap with Leo's book:** Danone is not among the human book's holdings. Consumer Defensive is 3.30% of it ([signals.json](../../signals.json) `book.bySector`), so this adds a new name in a small sector.
