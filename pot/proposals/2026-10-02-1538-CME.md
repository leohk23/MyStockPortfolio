# CME — CME Group Inc.

**What it does:** CME runs exchanges where banks, asset managers, companies and traders buy and
sell futures and options on interest rates, equity indices, currencies, energy, metals and farm
products, and it runs the clearing house that stands between buyer and seller. It earns a fee on
each contract traded and cleared, and it sells the resulting market data. Most of its revenue is
these clearing and transaction fees.

## 2. Thesis

Businesses, banks and funds have to move interest-rate, currency, equity and commodity risk
somewhere, and the deepest pool for most of it is CME's, where liquidity attracts more liquidity.
That turns into a 64.9% operating margin (FY2025, up from 60.1% in FY2022) and revenue that has
grown every year since 2021 ([earnings](../../earnings.json)), and September 2026 was the busiest
September on record. At 22.2x trailing earnings, the 5.4th percentile of five years, the price
treats 2025–26 activity as a peak. **Whether fee income holds as activity normalises is what P3
tests, not a premise (Rule 2).**

- **Monthly, from the November release for October:** total ADV down year on year in any month.

  so this leg is **not** tripped.
- **At Q3 results on 21 October 2026:** clearing and transaction fees down year on year **despite**
  Q3 ADV up 16%. That would mean revenue per contract is falling faster than volume grows, which is
  the earliest form of the dominant risk below. Also a GAAP operating margin under 65%.

**Break** — propose an exit under §5.1 if **both Q3 and Q4 2026** show clearing and transaction
fee revenue down **at least 5%** year on year and a GAAP operating margin **below 60%**; or if either
quarter's margin is **below 55%**. Q3 is read on 21 October, Q4 by **28 February 2027**. Use
reported operating profit over revenue. These are my thresholds, not management guidance.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none. T1 to T3 cover AI model pricing, seat-priced software and agent governance. A
derivatives exchange and clearing house sells neither models nor seats, and it is not an agent
control layer.

**Dominant risk (`financial`):** record trading activity normalises, or holds only at falling fees
per contract, while costs stay high, so fee income and operating leverage shrink together.

**Earliest observable:** CME's monthly ADV release (the next is early November for October), then
clearing and transaction fees and GAAP operating margin in the **21 October** Q3 result. P3's Q3 leg
tests fees **against** volume, because record volume with falling fees is the form this risk takes
first.

- **Volume is at a record, which is the worst place to buy a volume business.** September and Q3 2026
  both set records. Anything that calms rate markets lowers ADV, and the stock trades on the
  assumption it does not.
- **The fee leg already says price per contract is slipping.** Q2 fees fell 2.6% while revenue grew on
  market data. If Q3 repeats that on +16% volume, mix and pricing are eroding.
- **The cheapness is modest.** 22.2x is 11.9% below the five-year median, and P/OCF is outside the
  cheapest decile. This is a fair price for a good franchise, not a bargain.
- **A 64.9% margin invites challengers.** Rival futures venues and regulators pressing on clearing
  fees and market-data pricing are standing threats.
- **Combined exposure.** CME is not in Leo's human book ([signals](../../signals.json) `book`), so
  there is no overlap. The book is already **68.1% US**, and this adds to that.
- **Previously proposed.** The 30 September and 1 October runs proposed one CME share; Leo has not
  acted on either. This proposal stands on its own.
