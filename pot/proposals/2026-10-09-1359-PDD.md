# PDD — PDD Holdings

goods to consumers, and Temu, its international equivalent. It earns two ways: merchants pay it for
advertising and placement ("online marketing services"), and it takes fees on transactions

shares trade in New York as ADSs, each representing four ordinary shares.

## 2. Thesis

**PDD's operating profit fell 12.7% in FY2025** (RMB108.4bn to RMB94.6bn) as it spent on merchant
support and Temu, and that is what took it to 8.5× trailing earnings. In the first half of 2026,
operating profit **rose 13%** (RMB47.4bn against RMB41.9bn) on revenue up 9%. So the recovery
§2.4b asks for is already in two filed quarters, not only in a story. The business earns an 18.3%
return on capital (FY2025). It holds RMB456.4bn of cash and short-term investments, about USD68bn,
against a market value of about USD119bn. I think the market is pricing it as if FY2025's fall
were the new trend, when the filings now say that fall has stopped.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none. PDD buys AI rather than selling it, so T1 touches its costs at most. It sells no
seats (T2) and no control layer (T3).

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** the domestic marketplace's monetisation is stalling. Online
marketing services, PDD's core profit pool, grew only 2.5% and 3% in H1 2026 while merchant support
and the Alibaba, JD and Meituan subsidy war continue. If it turns negative, Temu's lower-margin
transaction fees have to carry the whole company.

**Earliest observable:** online marketing services revenue in the Q3 2026 release (about
18 November, quarterly in the 6-K), set against the NBS online physical-goods series (monthly). Both
are inside two quarters.

Secondary arguments, at full strength:

- **Earnings per share are falling even while operating profit grows.** H1 2026 GAAP EPS per ADS
  was RMB26.93 against RMB30.72 a year earlier (−12%). Attributable net income fell 15% and 12% in
  the two quarters. The non-operating income that flattered 2024 and 2025 is shrinking, plausibly
  interest on the cash pile as Chinese rates fall. I have not sourced the bridge. A P/E of 8.5× on
  a falling EPS line is less cheap than it looks.
- **The cash may never reach a shareholder.** The 20-F says there is no present plan to pay a
  dividend, and I found no equity buyback. RMB456bn is gross, before merchant payables, which I did
  not source. If it stays in China and on the balance sheet, the cash-adjusted multiple is a
  number, not a return.
- **Temu is exposed to policy, not only to competitors.** The end of US de minimis and the EU's
  customs reform both raise its cost per parcel. PDD does not report Temu separately, so its margin
  cannot be watched directly.
- **Disclosure is thin.** No guidance, no segment split and no capital-allocation policy. This is
  the Hong Kong earnings-quality concern Leo raised about the HSI, in a US listing.
- **Currency.** Earnings are in renminbi. The rate has moved in the pot's favour, at 6.79 per USD in
  Q2 against about 7.3 a year earlier, which is a tailwind that can reverse. This is part of Leo's
  7532.T regret.

**Why this is not 7532.T again.** That purchase bought a rebound in visitor numbers before the
accounts showed it. Here the recovery is in two filed quarters of operating profit already, and the
falsifier is set so that a third quarter going the other way trips it. What this does share with
7532.T is currency and policy exposure, and those are written down above rather than assumed away.

**Overlap with Leo's book:** none in PDD itself. It adds to China, which is 6.46% of the book (plus
Old Hong Kong at 7.84%), so it adds to an exposure that is moderate, not large.
