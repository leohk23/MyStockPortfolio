## 2. Thesis

NVIDIA's business case is operating performance rather than the screen: FY2023–FY2026 revenue grew eightfold while operating margin rose from 20.7% to 60.4%, and Q2 FY2027 revenue, Data Center revenue and GAAP operating income grew 106%, 117% and 124% respectively ([local earnings](../../earnings.json), [Q2 results](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)).
Now is the entry because the fresh quarter confirms that Rubin is ramping into production across multiple cloud partners and management guides Q3 revenue to $108.0bn ±2% with no China Data Center compute revenue assumed, while the recurring-earnings cross-check still sits near the cheapest end of the local five-year distribution ([Q2 results](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx), [local valuation](../../prices.json)).
The market may be over-discounting a near-term hyperscaler pause and underestimating how much inference, sovereign AI and physical-AI demand can broaden beyond today's largest buyers, but that claim is explicitly tested against margin rather than assumed from a cheap-looking headline P/E.

This is not the 7532.T mistake again: it is supported by four filed years of accelerating USD
revenue and high margins, the latest primary filing, and a current order book-independent demand
check. The proposal also corrects, rather than ignores, the valuation distortion from unrealised
equity gains and confronts customer concentration, circular financing, China and supply commitments.

## 6. The case against

NVIDIA is increasingly financing the ecosystem that buys its products. The 10-Q shows supply and
capacity commitments rising from $119bn to $279bn in one quarter, $99bn of equity investments plus
$25bn of further equity commitments, and $36bn of cloud-service commitments with selected AI-cloud
partners; customers can stop providing that capacity to NVIDIA and sell it elsewhere
([Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)).
That can broaden demand, but it can also make revenue look less independent precisely when the
company is taking enormous inventory, credit and capacity risk.

One direct customer supplied 16% of Q2 revenue, export controls can remove markets or strand
inventory, and $23.7bn of first-half equity gains inflated GAAP earnings while exposing the balance
sheet to market reversals ([Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)).
The human book already has 7.06% in NVIDIA, so this does not diversify Leo's wealth. If hyperscaler
returns disappoint, customers' own accelerators improve, or circular financing masks end demand,
earnings and the multiple can contract together; 31× recurring earnings is cheap only relative to
NVIDIA's extraordinary recent history, not in absolute terms.
