## 2. Thesis

**The business:** ResMed sells breathing devices and replacement masks for sleep apnoea and respiratory care, plus software used by care providers. Initial equipment sales create a subsequent supply business, so both new treatment starts and continuing use matter.

**The engine:** Reported revenue grew 10.21% a year over FY2023–26. After restoring restructuring charges omitted locally, annual operating margins were 27.28%, 28.17%, 32.75% and 33.37%. This is a profitable expanding business, not a sales recovery disguised by improving margin. The September quarter's cash after PP&E remained positive.

**Why now:** The latest [quarter](https://investor.resmed.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/433/resmed-inc-announces-results-for-the-first-quarter-of-fiscal-year-2027) delivered 7% core constant-currency growth, while the price remains inside the reported-history filter. A one-share addition is affordable without approaching the concentration limit.

**What the market is missing:** My inference is that part of the discount prices T2's software disruption too broadly across the group. Care software represents approximately 11% of quarterly sales, limiting that thesis's direct revenue reach without proving the segment's margins safe. The current reported multiple is about 30% below the historical median, which also reflects unusually favourable competitive conditions. The separate pharmaceutical-substitution discount could be justified; continued treatment demand, rather than this valuation comparison, must test it.

**What has to happen:** New device demand and repeat mask demand must persist, with operating cash exceeding reinvestment. Those observations, rather than a return to an old multiple, must earn the additional share.
