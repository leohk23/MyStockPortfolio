# CME — CME Group

**What it does:** CME Group runs the main US futures and options exchanges (CME, CBOT, NYMEX, COMEX)
and the clearing house behind them. Banks, asset managers, hedge funds, energy and farm businesses
use its contracts to hedge or take positions in interest rates, equity indices, currencies, energy,
metals and crops. It earns a fee on every contract traded and cleared, and also sells its price data
to people and machines that use it.

## 2. Thesis

CME is the clearing house for most US-listed rates, equity-index, energy and agricultural futures.
That position earns a **64.9% operating margin** (FY2025, up from 56.5% in FY2021) and company-adjusted
EPS that rose from $7.97 to $11.20 between FY2022 and FY2025, and Q3 2026 volume was a record
29.4m contracts a day, up 16%. The market has taken the multiple from a FY2025 average of 26.6x to
22.5x reported (5.4th percentile of five years) and the shares down 8% in the month in which those
volumes printed. That is a market discounting a volume peak and fee pressure, about 15% of
de-rating, while the earnings base is still compounding at roughly 12% a year. What it may be
missing is that interest-rate volume grew 22% in September with the US ten-year at 5.2%. Hedging
demand from heavy government borrowing and an energy shock is a reason to use CME, not a one-off
spike. Over five years, only the weeks around the June 2026 floor priced this earnings stream
lower.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** T2 bears at the margin. Part of CME's market-data revenue is priced per display user,
the seat model T2 says agents compress. It is a minority of revenue, and machine (non-display)
licences are the growing side. T1 and T3 do not bear.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** a rival venue, or CME's own price concessions to keep volume
from one, cuts the fee per contract, so record volume stops turning into revenue.

**Earliest observable:** RPC and clearing-fee revenue against ADV, in the Q3 release on
**21 October 2026**. From outside the company, the CFTC's weekly Commitments of Traders open
interest in US Treasury futures shows any shift between exchanges, if a rival venue's contracts are
reported. Both are inside two quarters, so the order is full size.

**Secondary arguments, put to persuade:**

- **Peak-cycle volume.** 2026's records were made in an energy shock with the ten-year yield at
  5.2%. When volatility subsides, volume falls and a 65% margin works hard in reverse. A 22x
  multiple on peak earnings is not cheap, and the June 2026 floor of 18.9x shows how far the
  market will go when it thinks so.
- **The rate per contract is already falling.** It was $0.690 in Q2 2025 and $0.678 in Q2 2026,
  down 1.7%. Mix (more interest-rate and micro contracts) explains some of that, but mix is also
  what a price war looks like early.
- **Overlap inside the pot.** The pot already holds TW, another financial-market tollbooth. CME at
  26.5% of contributed capital plus TW makes market-infrastructure the pot's largest theme, so a
  single regulatory change to US market structure would hit two holdings.
- **Human book.** Not held, so this adds no overlap with Leo's own exposure. US weight in his book
  is already 67.8%, and this adds to that geography.
- **This has been proposed before.** CME has been proposed on 30 September, 1 October and
  2 October and not bought. That is a remark, not evidence either way.

**Why this is not 7532.T again.** 7532.T was a narrative (the tourist rebound) bought without the
numbers. CME is bought on four years of filed margin expansion and a price-per-contract test dated
three weeks out. No yen or foreign-currency earnings are involved, and if the fee test fails it
fails within two quarters.
