## 2. Thesis

Yum China is a restaurant operator whose existing network pays for its own expansion: in H1 2026
it produced **$705m** of operating cash after capital spending, and its filed FY2025 operating
margin rose to **10.94%** from 10.28% while revenue grew 4.4%. The shares have fallen **17% in a
month** (from $49.52 to $40.81, local closes) with no filing, guidance change or results to
explain it. That fall reflects the market's worry about lower average tickets and higher delivery
costs across the whole system. What the market may be under-weighting is that this is a
cash-generating operator returning capital at a mid-teens multiple, not a growth story that needs
a Chinese consumer recovery to work. Whether discounting is eroding the economics of each
restaurant is not assumed away here; it is what P3 tests.

## 3. Falsifier

The thresholds below are my decision rules, not management guidance.

**Warning:** At Q3 results (locally estimated **4 November 2026**, read by **6 November**), a
warning fires if either:

- **Brand demand:** KFC or Pizza Hut reports negative same-store sales while its same-store
  transactions are still positive. That would mean traffic is being bought with price.
- **Brand margin:** either brand's restaurant margin falls **100bp or more** year on year. The Q2
  baselines are KFC **17.1%** and Pizza Hut **12.9%**. For Pizza Hut, first remove the royalty
  saving the company actually discloses for the post-purchase period. Management expects a
  **2.8-point** steady-state benefit, so an unadjusted flat margin could hide deterioration. If
  the company gives no bridge, the test is unresolved and still triggers a re-read.

Outside the company, check Meituan's next quarterly filing by **1 December 2026**. A warning fires
if its Core Local Commerce operating margin is below **5%** (Q2 baseline **7.9%**) and the filing
attributes the fall to renewed subsidies or incentives. This measures delivery-platform price
competition, not Yum's share.

**Break:** By **1 March 2027**, propose a sale under §5.1 in any of three cases:

- **Two quarters:** the same brand reports two consecutive post-entry quarters with negative
  same-store sales, positive transactions and a restaurant-margin fall of at least 100bp in each.
- **One quarter:** a single quarter with a 300bp margin fall.
- **Cash:** FY2026 operating cash less capital spending is zero or negative.

All three can trip while openings and buybacks still lift group EPS.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none. Yum sells food, not seats, models or compliance. No entry in `theses.md`
bears on it.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** Price and delivery competition, much of it funded by the
platforms, erode each restaurant's economics. Lower tickets and higher delivery costs could do
this while openings, royalty savings and buybacks keep group EPS rising.

**Earliest observable:** At Q3 results (locally estimated 4 November): each brand's same-store
sales against its transactions, and its restaurant margin after the disclosed royalty saving.
Outside the company, Meituan's quarterly filing by 1 December. Both are within two quarters, so
Rule 4 does not halve the order.

Secondary arguments, stated to persuade:

- **Growth is slow.** Revenue grew 4.4% in FY2025. Leo's filter wants growth as well as value, and
  a mid-single-digit grower at 15x is fair rather than cheap unless margins keep rising.
- **The recent fall is unexplained, and that is not comforting.** A 17% slide in a month with no
  filing can mean the market already sees Q3 ticket pressure that the Q2 figures do not show yet.
  Buying before 4 November means buying before that evidence exists.
- **The Pizza Hut royalty purchase changes the comparison.** It was bought with **$1.2bn** of
  consideration and financed partly with a **CNH 8.4bn bridge facility**
  ([closing filing](https://ir.yumchina.com/static-files/b860fd22-dbf0-42d4-adb1-50183734b945)).
  Margin gains from it are paid for, not organic.
- **Concentration in China.** The human book already has **6.9%** in China and **8.1%** in Hong
  Kong (signals.json `byGeography`). This adds China consumer exposure. It is a new company for
  the combined position, not diversification away from China. YUMC itself is **0%** of the human
  book, so there is no direct overlap.
- **Currency and policy.** RMB earnings, a US listing and Sino-US policy risk sit on top of the
  business risk.
