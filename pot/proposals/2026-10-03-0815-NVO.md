# NVO — Novo Nordisk (ADR)

**What it does:** Novo Nordisk is a Danish drug maker. Most of its sales are injectable and oral
GLP-1 medicines for type 2 diabetes (Ozempic, Rybelsus) and obesity (Wegovy), plus insulin and
haemophilia treatments. It sells them to pharmacies, insurers and health systems worldwide, and the
US is its largest market. It earns by manufacturing patented biologics at high margin and selling
them at a net price negotiated with US payers and set by governments elsewhere. NVO is the US
depositary receipt, one ADS for one B share, traded on the NYSE.

## 2. Thesis

Novo turns a patented manufacturing franchise into a **41% operating margin and a 30.6% ROIC**
(FY2025). On 4 August it **raised** its 2026 outlook to adjusted sales and operating profit growth
of 0% to −6% at CER, from −4% to −12%. International Operations grew 10% at CER in Q2, and at its
21 September Capital Markets Day the company guided 2026–30 revenue growth in line with peers and a
broadly stable operating margin. The market prices the ADS at 9.4x reported and about 10.7x
adjusted trailing earnings, with a 5.0% dividend yield. It fell 8% on the Capital Markets Day and
37% over a year. That is a multiple for earnings in long decline, set on a company whose own
guidance is roughly flat this year and whose medium-term plan says it will grow with the industry.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none. T1 to T3 are about AI models, seat software and agent control. None touches a
drug maker's sales.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** Lilly's tirzepatide, and now its oral orforglipron, keep taking
the US incretin market, so Novo's US sales go on falling while the category grows.

**Earliest observable:** Novo's US Operations growth at CER and Wegovy growth on **4 November**,
read against Lilly's Mounjaro plus Zepbound growth in its Q3 release in late October. Both are the
issuers' own quarterly filings, inside two quarters.

**Secondary arguments, put to persuade:**

- **Price, not only volume.** Novo is cutting the US list prices of Wegovy, Ozempic and Rybelsus to
  $675 a month from 1 January 2027 ([Novo announcement](https://www.novonordisk.com/content/nncorp/global/en/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=916504)).
  This run's filing scout dated the announcement to February 2026. The page's id sits just before
  Novo's 7 September releases, so the date is unconfirmed. Government price setting can shrink US revenue even where
  share holds, and that shows only in the 2027 outlook, which Novo gives in February 2027.
- **"In line with peers" is a downgrade.** It sounds safe, but for a company priced at 70x in 2024
  on above-industry growth it is an admission that the growth premium is gone. The market's answer
  on 21 September was −8% in a day.
- **Returns are falling fast.** ROIC went from 56.6% to 30.6% in two years as capacity spending ran
  ahead of sales. If the capacity was built for volumes Lilly now takes, the asset base stays and
  the returns do not come back.
- **13,000 job cuts** read as defence of the margin, not offence.
- **The 340B reversal flatters the reported figure.** On the adjusted base the multiple is about
  10.7x, not 9.4x.
- **Proposed before, not bought.** NVO was proposed on 29 September and 2 October. That is a
  remark, not a reason.
- **Overlap.** Not in the human book, so there is no added exposure there. It adds a European
  healthcare name to a pot that already holds AZN.L and EUZ.DE, and healthcare would then be about 39% of
  contributed capital at today's marks: AZN.L £118.70, EUZ.DE £63.00 and this order's £112.75.

**Why this is not 7532.T again.** 7532.T was bought on a tourist-rebound story without looking at
valuation, forward prospects or currency. Here, the forward prospects are the company's own
guidance, read in full: flat to down this year, growth with peers to 2030. The valuation is 9x to
11x against a 31x median. The currency exposure is explicit: a DKK earner, pegged to the euro,
held in USD. And the risk that matters, losing share to Lilly, has a dated test in both companies'
filings within five weeks.
