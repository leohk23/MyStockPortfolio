## 2. Thesis

R&S sells mission-critical distribution and power transformers into grid modernisation, renewables, data centres and industrial infrastructure, and its FY2025 accounts show 8.6% organic sales growth, a 20.9% EBITDA margin, CHF48.1m free cash flow and leverage reduced to 0.7× EBITDA ([FY2025 results](https://ir.the-rsgroup.com/news/rands-group-reports-solid-ebitda-margin-for-fy2025/e1b9c123-bd25-42ce-978a-8576e31d0be1)).
Why now: H1 sales and EBITDA fell as utility inventories normalised and longer-cycle power-transformer deliveries slipped, yet backlog rose 17% to CHF357.9m, book-to-bill was 1.2× and scheduled deliveries extend into 2027–2028 ([H1 update](https://ir.the-rsgroup.com/news/rands-group-provides-trading-update-for-h1-2026/bb41967c-ac88-4041-be50-200b3f26951b)).
The market may be treating delayed delivery as a transformer down-cycle when signed backlog and new capacity make timing a credible alternative; unlike the 7532.T mistake, this case separates acquisition-led from organic growth, confronts the current sales and margin decline, and makes backlog conversion plus a 19%–21% EBITDA margin—not the cheap multiple—the test.

## 6. The case against

The backlog may be less economically firm than it looks. H1 adjusted order intake fell 10%, sales
fell 13%, EBITDA fell 23%, and FY2026 revenue guidance is roughly flat with FY2025 and below the
mid-term growth range; utility customers have excess inventory and installation bottlenecks, while
the higher power-transformer mix takes longer to produce. A record CHF357.9m backlog can therefore
sit for years, be postponed or consume working capital before becoming revenue and cash
([H1 update](https://ir.the-rsgroup.com/news/rands-group-provides-trading-update-for-h1-2026/bb41967c-ac88-4041-be50-200b3f26951b)).

Much of FY2025's reported growth came from the Kyte acquisition, the 2025 margin already fell from
2024, and 143 public-market weeks have not seen a full transformer cycle. The strongest bear case
is that the market correctly sees peak margins, acquisition-flattered growth and an expensive
capacity build meeting normalized utility demand; if the backlog does not convert at the guided
sales and margin, the 12.44× recurring multiple is a value trap rather than an answer.
