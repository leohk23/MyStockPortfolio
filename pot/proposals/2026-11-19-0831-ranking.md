QQ (Value-added Services, 47% of Q3 revenue). It sells advertising across those apps (Marketing
Services, 21%), and payments, wealth and cloud (FinTech and Business Services, 31%).

**The engine, on filed years** (`earnings.json`, `prices.json` `capital`):

- Revenue grew from RMB554.6bn in FY2022 to RMB751.8bn in FY2025, **+10.7% a year**.

**Why the cheap P/E is not the opportunity it looks like.** Three things sit under the 11.5x:

1. **Cash commitment has risen much faster than current profit.** The combined balance-sheet line
   is prepayments, deposits **and other assets**; its increase cannot all be assigned to compute.
   Future service expense and depreciation are risks, but their timing, existing expense and
   corresponding revenue are not quantified. The second reader therefore removes the claim that
   trailing earnings carry almost none of these costs. Cash actually spent remains in cash flow.
2. **New AI Products reduce non-IFRS operating profit by about RMB15.23bn in Q3.** The issuer places
   both figures on the same non-IFRS basis. This is a **net operating-profit drag**, not gross
   spending: 83.3 − 68.073 = 15.227, or **18.3%** of profit excluding those products, not 22%.
   Tencent also reports cloud revenue growth of **35%**; the missing fact is the return on the
   incremental investment, not the existence of commercial demand. See Second reader sources.
3. **Reported EPS and underlying operating performance diverge.** The RMB8.78bn investee-gain
   adjustment remains evidence only, before separately disclosed tax effects. The full earnings
   bridge also contains compensation, amortisation, impairment and other adjustments; the gain
   alone is not a complete explanation of the EPS change. No after-tax EPS deduction is invented.

**Rule 5, against T1.** T1 says Chinese open-weight models reach rough parity with US frontier models
at a fraction of the price, so *inference volume grows and the price per unit collapses*. Tencent is
buying compute to build its own model family (Hy) and agents, in exactly the market where T1 says the
models become cheap and interchangeable. However, T1 also identifies non-US clouds and applications
as potential beneficiaries. **Second-reader correction:** the release does quantify cloud growth,
and describes compute serving internal products and external customers. It does not establish that
the core has no use for the contracts. The investment question is whether that growing business
earns an adequate return after the cash commitments and AI losses. I still do not have a supported
rebuttal to that discount under Rule 5; the actual deterioration in cash and operating profit is
the business reason for EXCLUDE, rather than an assertion that AI has no revenue.

The case for the other side, written to persuade. The core earned RMB83bn in one quarter. On the
reported quarterly-EPS sensitivity above, the shares cost about 11.5x trailing earnings, while Tencent still buys back
about HK$7bn of its own stock a quarter. The prepayments are an asset on the balance sheet, not a
write-off. The release quantifies cloud revenue growth; it does not separately quantify the cash
returns or profitability of the incremental compute. That distinction is why the countercase
deserves weight without establishing a buy today.

**This is the 7532.T mistake if bought now:** a strong core story, read without the cash flow, while
the denominator under the multiple is about to change.
