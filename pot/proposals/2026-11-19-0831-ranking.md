## Tencent, read

**What the business is.** Tencent sells games and digital content, plus subscriptions on WeChat and
QQ (Value-added Services, 47% of Q3 revenue). It sells advertising across those apps (Marketing
Services, 21%), and payments, wealth and cloud (FinTech and Business Services, 31%).

**The engine, on filed years** (`earnings.json`, `prices.json` `capital`):

- Revenue grew from RMB554.6bn in FY2022 to RMB751.8bn in FY2025, **+10.7% a year**.
- Operating margin rose every year: 20.5%, 27.2%, 31.6% and 33.1%.
- FY2025 ROIC was **15.1%**, gross profit over assets 0.21, and asset turnover 0.37.
- FY2025 operating cash flow was RMB303.1bn, up from 146.1bn in FY2022.

None of the §2.4b triggers fires. FY2025 operating profit rose 19%. Tencent gives no guide to a fall.
Three-year revenue growth is well above 1% a year, and volume is not disclosed.

**Local check.** Q3 is not yet in `earnings.json`. The stored quarters end at June 2026: revenue
RMB204,785m, IFRS EPS 6.104. The stored Q3 2025 EPS of 6.779 matches the release's prior-year figure,
so they agree. The quote's trailing EPS of HK$30.01 is those four quarters to June (RMB25.461) at the
bot's 1.179 HKD per RMB. Swapping Q3 2025 for Q3 2026 gives RMB25.629, or HK$30.21: a P/E of **11.48x**
at HK$346.80 (my arithmetic, a sensitivity). Today's second reader logged a RMB960m gap on June
operating profit (local 68,236 against the announcement's 67,276). This run did not re-open it.

**Why the cheap P/E is not the opportunity it looks like.** Three things sit under the 11.5x:

1. **The cost of the build-out has not reached the income statement yet.** The prepayments balance
   rose by RMB187.6bn in nine months, three-quarters of FY2025's operating profit. RMB138bn of that
   rise sits in non-current prepayments, which is compute contracted for years ahead. Quarterly capex
   is now six times what it was a year ago. As that compute is used, it lands in cost of revenue as
   service cost or depreciation. The trailing earnings carry almost none of it.
2. **The AI products already cost about RMB15bn a quarter.** Operating profit excluding New AI
   Products was RMB83.3bn, against RMB68.1bn non-IFRS for the group. That gap is my arithmetic, and it
   assumes the excluded figure is on the non-IFRS basis, which the scout's reading did not state. If
   so, the AI products absorbed about 22% of the core's operating profit in one quarter, and nothing
   in the release shows them earning revenue.
3. **Q3's reported EPS rose only because of an investee gain.** The RMB8.78bn gain, before tax
   effects, is recorded in `pot/adjustments.json` as evidence. On the company's own adjusted basis,
   EPS fell 4.6%.

**Rule 5, against T1.** T1 says Chinese open-weight models reach rough parity with US frontier models
at a fraction of the price, so *inference volume grows and the price per unit collapses*. Tencent is
buying compute to build its own model family (Hy) and agents, in exactly the market where T1 says the
models become cheap and interchangeable. The market's discount is a fair reading of that. The core is
growing 8% at a 40% margin, but the cash is being spent on a layer whose unit price is falling, and
on contracts the core does not need. To argue the discount is wrong, Tencent would have to show the
prepaid compute turning into revenue: cloud or AI revenue, or AI loss narrowing as a share of the
core. The release gives neither a utilisation figure nor an AI revenue line. **I cannot argue the
discount is wrong on what has been published, so under Rule 5 no proposal can be written.**

The case for the other side, written to persuade. The core earned RMB83bn in one quarter. On a
company-adjusted basis the shares cost about 12x trailing earnings, while Tencent still buys back
about HK$7bn of its own stock a quarter. The prepayments are an asset on the balance sheet, not a
write-off. And the release says part of the compute is to "meet external demand for our cloud
services", which is paying demand. That case becomes arguable the day a filing quantifies it.

**This is the 7532.T mistake if bought now:** a strong core story, read without the cash flow, while
the denominator under the multiple is about to change.

**What would reopen it:**

- The FY2026 annual results, with Q4 operating cash flow, the prepayments balance, and whether New
  AI Products' drag on the core narrows.
- A TCEHY quote in `prices.json`.

The results date is not announced, and the HKEX board-meeting notice (`hk-board.json`) should carry
it some weeks ahead. **No reopening is booked.** A `date` booking needs the day results are due, and a guessed one
would fire before anything is published. The wishlist asks for the date.
