## 2. Thesis

Tradeweb can earn more from institutional trading moving onto electronic venues without needing to lend the principal traded, and its latest reported quarter shows revenue and operating profit growing together. The business opportunity is greater use of its transaction network, with existing infrastructure serving additional flow; August's fully electronic US credit volumes support that adoption case, while lower revenue per unit makes its durability a live test. My inference is that the discount overstates the risk that automation destroys aggregate trading economics: unlike a disappearing software seat, an executed transaction still generates a fee, but P3 must establish whether adoption actually outweighs fee compression. [Quarterly results](https://investors.tradeweb.com/news-releases/news-release-details/tradeweb-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results), [August activity](https://www.tradeweb.com/newsroom/media-center/news-releases/tradeweb-reports-august-2026-total-trading-volume-of-%2461.2-trillion-and-average-daily-volume-of-%242.8-trillion).

**Why this is not the 7532.T mistake:** the case rests on filed operating profit, fee-paying activity and observable market share, not a macro trading-volume rebound or a low P/E alone. A high margin is not proof of a moat. I infer that connecting both sides of institutional trades and embedding execution into their workflows can preserve some economics; the fee and share tests below explicitly allow that inference to fail. The reported multiple is about **55.72% below** its local historical median, but gains in investments contaminate that comparison.
