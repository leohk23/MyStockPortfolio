## 2. Thesis

Eckert & Ziegler sells the isotope inputs and manufacturing work needed by medical customers, with an earning engine that also includes industrial sources. The Medical segment's H1 adjusted EBIT growth and the last three years of improving filed group margins support a small investment in delivered operating earnings rather than an unpriced industry demand forecast. The business opportunity is to turn that medical production into durable shareholder cash, and today's allocation buys limited exposure while the next cash-flow statements test whether that conversion actually occurs.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none.

**Dominant risk (`financial`):** Medical expansion and associated capital deployment fail to turn accounting profit into cash available to shareholders.

**Earliest observable:** Nine-month operating cash, capex, principal leases and attributable profit, in the issuer's **12 November 2026** results; P3 measures the conversion directly. The H1 warning baseline is already weak.

The medical-isotope discovery case remains plausible, but the local accounts still include industrial exposure and licensing/perimeter complications. Adjusted EBIT growth is not free cash flow. Cash committed to a spun-off company's convertible is a real allocation choice; an accounting presentation change would not recover it. The BaFin process and old licence economics make the historical comparison less reliable, while FX can move both the GBP ticket and reported group earnings.
