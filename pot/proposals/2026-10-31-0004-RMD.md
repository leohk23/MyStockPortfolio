## 2. Thesis

**The business:** ResMed sells breathing devices and replacement masks to people treated for sleep apnoea, through healthcare providers and distributors. It also sells software used in care delivery. Device placements create a continuing market for consumables, but future retention must be demonstrated.

**The engine:** Filed operating margins were 28.17%, 32.75% and 33.37% in FY2024–26; revenue grew from $4.685bn to $5.653bn. The capital indicators below support an established earning business, while the latest quarterly operating margin shows that improvement is not uninterrupted. Sources: [2025 proxy](https://investor.resmed.com/sec-filings/content/0000943819-25-000079/rmd-20251001.htm), [2026 proxy](https://investor.resmed.com/sec-filings/content/0000943819-26-000080/rmd-20261001.htm) and local annual revenue.

**Why now:** The September-quarter filing supplies a fresh test of device and mask demand after the portfolio changes. The opportunity is to buy an operating business after reading that test, rather than extrapolate the headline EPS increase.

**What the market is missing:** My different view is that the installed treatment base and repeat purchases deserve more weight than a single quarter's capital-return headline. The evidence is existing cash generation and the filing's attribution of sleep-product growth to units; it does not establish that drug substitution is harmless.

**What has to happen:** Treatment starts and resupply must remain resilient through the next two quarters, with operating profit becoming cash after investment. Price increases alone will not satisfy the case. P3 tests the treatment economics separately from EPS.

## 3. Falsifier

The following thresholds are analyst decisions, not company guidance.

**Warning:** at the next quarterly result, checked by **5 February 2027**, re-read if the filing describes flat/falling sleep-device or mask unit demand; if Americas sleep-device constant-currency revenue growth is below **3%** (latest **6%**); or if the comparable GLP-1/CPAP cohort loses its positive initiation or one-year resupply advantage. If the company ceases to attribute growth to units and gives no replacement volume evidence, keep the warning active as **cannot tell**, even if revenue rises. These tests can move before operating profit falls.

An independent warning is an FDA approval of AD109 for OSA, checked immediately on publication and by **1 March 2027**, after Apnimed's **28 February** target decision date. Read the actual label's eligible population and treatment setting. Approval is a new competing option, not itself proof that ResMed's thesis has broken. [Apnimed notice](https://ir.apnimed.com/news-releases/news-release-details/apnimed-present-five-abstracts-including-two-late-breakers-chest); [FDA's existing Zepbound approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-medication-obstructive-sleep-apnea).

**Break:** by **7 May 2027**, recommend exit under §5.1 if either (a) two consecutive post-entry quarterly filings explicitly report falling sleep-device or mask unit demand, regardless of revenue/price growth, or (b) two comparable updates show CPAP initiation or one-year mask resupply worse for the drug-treated cohort than CPAP-only patients. Independently, two consecutive quarters of declining constant-currency Americas sleep-device revenue **and** mask revenue break the repeat-demand case. Compare like-for-like definitions, excluding discontinued life-support products.

A financial break is trailing twelve-month cash from operations less PP&E purchases becoming non-positive. Retain all operating restructuring cash and exclude disposal proceeds. Observe quarterly; it is a separate route, not an extra condition required for a demand break.

The cohort series is company-produced, observational and not guaranteed quarterly. Missing data cannot establish a pass or a numerical break: require a specific re-review and maintain the warning. The external FDA label and Apnimed's quarterly launch/adoption disclosures supply an independent check on the substitute; no OSA-specific prescription count was verified, and total obesity-drug sales would not substitute for one.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** T2 bears on the residual care-software business, where workflow automation can reduce seat value; T1 may lower its inference costs but does not prove pricing power. Neither explains the core device valuation. No separately evidenced T3 revenue is assumed.

**Dominant risk (competitive):** drug treatments reduce CPAP initiation and mask replacement before aggregate revenue reveals it.

The [FDA approved Zepbound](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-medication-obstructive-sleep-apnea) for moderate-to-severe OSA in adults with obesity in December 2024. [Apnimed's AD109](https://ir.apnimed.com/news-releases/news-release-details/apnimed-present-five-abstracts-including-two-late-breakers-chest) is a further, non-weight-loss mechanism with a dated decision pending. These are actual treatment alternatives, not a generic technology worry.

**Earliest observable:** next quarter's unit-demand commentary and comparable initiation/resupply evidence, by 5 February, then the February FDA decision. The [29 October call](https://investor.resmed.com/_assets/_0963557030a6bf65e868c5a046e196ae/resmed/db/2598/48783/webcast_transcript/RMD_1Q27_Earnings_Transcript_29OCT26.pdf) reports a 3.3m-patient cohort with initiation over 11% higher and one-year resupply over 3% higher in combined GLP-1/CPAP patients.
