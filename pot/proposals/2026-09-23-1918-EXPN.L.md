retailers and others, mainly in North America, the UK and Brazil. It also runs a consumer business
that sells credit monitoring and earns referral fees when consumers take out financial products.

## 2. Thesis

Experian earns a recurring toll each time a lender pulls data or runs a decision. That toll grew
**12.3%** in FY2026 on a statutory operating margin held at **23.8% to 24.2%** for three years. It
left **$1.465bn** of cash after intangibles, capex and lease payments, and still grew **9%
organically in B2B** in Q1 FY27. The market has cut its multiple from a five-year median of
**36.4x** to **22.0x**, a de-rating of about **40%**. At an unchanged multiple, that would be the
same as permanently losing about 40% of the earnings. The price is therefore discounting a large,
lasting loss of the toll to direct score licensing and agent-driven underwriting (T2).

My claim is only that the discount is larger than the evidence so far supports, because paid B2B
activity is still growing. Whether the toll is shrinking beneath that growth is exactly what P3
tests. I do not assume the data franchise is safe.

**Break:** propose a sale under §5.1, at any review and at the latest by **31 May 2027** after

- **Volume or price:** a comparable paid-volume or net-price series falls **more than 10%** year
  on year in two consecutive observations, attributed to such substitution.

Separately, FY27 operating cash less intangibles, capex and lease principal at zero or below
breaks the cash part of P2. It does not certify that the substitution risk has passed.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:**

- **T2 bears directly.** Agents and direct score licensing can move decision work, and its fees,
  away from the bureau's paid reports.
- **T3 is a possible tailwind** through identity and permissions, but no material revenue from it
  is evidenced, so none is counted.
- **T1 cuts both ways.** Cheaper models lower Experian's own costs, and also the cost of
  competing analytics.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** Lenders, resellers and platforms capture the scoring and
decision economics, so fewer paid reports are needed or the reseller margin disappears.

**Earliest observable:** FICO's and Plaid's partner and production announcements (event-driven,
re-read by 23 October), then Experian's H1 disclosure on 18 November. Paid-unit data is undated
and may never be published, so Rule 4 halves the order.

Secondary arguments:

- **The warning is already on.** Signed coverage of 73.1% is past my line on day one. Buying under
  an active warning needs the reduced size and the dated re-read; neither makes the risk smaller.
- **Revenue can hide the loss.** Price rises, acquisitions and consumer services can keep group
  growth positive while the core toll shrinks. That is T2's early shape in Leo's own words, and
  group growth is therefore not used as a pass anywhere in P3.
- **The earnings quality depends on the basis.** Benchmark EPS excludes acquisition amortisation
  and fair-value items, and the $101m financing gain sits inside statutory profit. The 19.9x and
  21.8x figures are close, but neither has an adjusted history behind it.
- **The combined exposure is mostly the US.** EXPN.L is 0% of the human book, but most of its
  profit is US credit activity. A London quote does not diversify away from the book's 67% US
  weight.
