### Why the four fresh names do not get the cash this week

**Stevanato** was this run's first pick, and the order was drafted at ten shares before the issuer's own release was read in full. The release reversed it, and these are the facts that did so, all from the [Q3 2026 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001849853/000119312526445320/stvn-ex99_1.htm) of 3 November:

- Revenue was €326.1m, +8% reported and organic. Adjusted EPS was €0.16 against €0.14. FY2026 guidance is maintained at €1.260–1.280bn revenue and €0.60–0.62 adjusted EPS, which is growth on FY2025's €1,186m and €0.54.
- But "other containment and delivery solutions" grew 22% partly on "variable compensation tied to lower GLP-1 volumes". Some of the quarter's growth is a customer paying for volume it did not take, and the release does not say how much.
- High-value solutions, the margin engine, grew 1% to €149.4m (46% of revenue).
- On 2027 the company says "we remain confident in our ability to grow low-single digits next year, based on what we know today". GLP-1s were about 17–18% of Q3 revenue (scout reading of the same release).
- Nine-month operating cash flow was €165.0m against €192.0m a year earlier, while the plants in Italy and Indiana are still being built (Q3 capex €44.8m).

On those facts, the price is 24.3x company-adjusted trailing EPS of $0.6507 (`researchEps`) for a business that guides low-single-digit growth next year, earns 8.1% on its capital and is spending cash faster than its operations bring it in. The percentile is cheap because the window's median is 44x, struck in 2023 when Stevanato was priced as a GLP-1 growth stock. §2.4 asks for margin, consistency, growth and return on capital. The growth now guided and the 8.1% ROIC do not give a balance of growth and valuation at 24x. The test that would change this is dated: FY2026 results (early March is the usual timing, not yet announced), with the size of the compensation, the formal 2027 range, and cash after capex for the full year. The stock fell from $19.74 to $14.10 on 4 Nov (−28.6%, `history.json`) and recovered to $15.80 by Friday, +10.0% on the 13th. This run did not establish why it rose on Friday.

**lululemon** is a shrinking business by its own guide: revenue down 5–7% this year, a wider decline than the 0–1% fall guided in June, and EPS guided at $9.48–9.73 against $14.64 for FY2024. Its ROIC of 36.5% and GP/A of 74% are the best capital returns on this page, so it is worth a fresh look once the Q3 figures show whether the US decline has stopped widening. Proposing it now would be the 7532.T mistake: buying a rebound before the accounts show one.

**H.B. Fuller** has price without volume, −3.0% in Q3, and is taking on 7.625% debt for an acquisition whose effect on leverage is not yet disclosed. FY2026 results and the FY2027 guide in January answer both questions.

**Haleon** cut its organic growth outlook on 29 October and has published nothing since. Its two earlier risks are both read at FY2026 results.

Holding cash is a position under §4.4. The next full run after 17–19 November, when NVIDIA, Experian, Alstom, Tencent, Intuit and Trip.com have all reported, will have more to choose from than this one.
