## 2. Thesis

**Second-reader qualification:** rent growth has not translated into growing cash available to fund expansion. The latest cash statement and the weaker Q3 leasing/delivery evidence in P5 make the discount less clearly an opportunity. The business still grows, but the draft understates its dependence on borrowing and timely development completions. This is a business judgement, not a veto for an uncertified earnings basis (§7.2a).

**The business:** CTP builds and owns warehouses and light-industrial buildings in Central and Eastern Europe, on business parks it develops on its own land. It entered Italy in Q4 2025 and has agreed to enter Vietnam. It earns rent from manufacturers, logistics firms and retailers, and about 63% of 2026's leases were signed with existing tenants ([Q3 release](https://ctp.eu/news/ctp-n-v-q3-2026-results/)). Its founder, Remon Vos, holds 73.3% of the shares ([shareholder structure](https://ctp.eu/investors/shareholder-structure/)).

**The engine:** filed revenue grew **18.2% a year** over 2022–25, from €589.1m to €972.4m, and operating margin rose from 58.5% to 65.7% to 67.5% (2022, 2024, 2025). The 2026 quarters run at 67.1%, 69.5% and 69.8% (`earnings.json`). Like-for-like rent grew **4.8%** in nine months, and occupancy held at 93%. New buildings are delivered at a **10.0% yield on cost** against a 6.5% gross yield on the standing portfolio ([Q3 release](https://ctp.eu/news/ctp-n-v-q3-2026-results/)). D104 ROIC is only **3.1%** (FY2025): a landlord's operating return on fair-valued capital is thin, and what it adds for owners comes as development margin, through NTA. That rose from €18.08 to €20.78 a share between end-2024 and September 2026.

**Why now:** the shares are €12.36, down 31% in a year, with no profit warning. Guidance of €1.01–1.03 company-specific adjusted EPRA EPS was reiterated in October. The company is buying back up to €100m: 1.43m shares at an average €12.76 by 20 November ([buyback](https://ctp.eu/news/ctp-announces-share-buyback-programme-of-up-to-e100-million/)).

**What the market is missing:** my reading is that the price treats a growing rent roll as if it were a shrinking one. At 40.5% below NTA, the market values the portfolio at roughly 22% below CTP's own valuation (approximate, P5). Yet gross rental income rose 12.5% and like-for-like rent 4.8% in nine months, and the company is buying its own shares below NTA.

**What has to happen:** adjusted EPRA EPS keeps growing after the cheap 2021 debt is refinanced, while leverage moves back toward the 40–45% target. P3 tests both.

## 6. The case against

**Second-reader addition:** the dominant financial risk already has a cash manifestation, not merely a future refinancing scenario. Working capital and interest consumed the improvement in operating earnings. Separately, the quarterly leasing decline and delivery-range narrowing increase completion and letting risk. These are two distinct weakens: cash conversion and delivery execution. The restated EPS uncertainty is already disclosed and is not counted again. The company can slow development, so negative cash after development alone is not a thesis break. The question is whether growth and leverage can improve together; the reopening tests make that observable.

**Theses:** none. CTP rents warehouses and sells no seat-priced software, models or AI control layer; T1, T2 and T3 do not bear on it.

**Dominant risk (`financial`):** CTP stays above its own leverage target while it buys a ~US$500m Vietnam portfolio, buys back €100m of shares and refinances 0.75% debt at about 3.6%, so adjusted EPRA EPS growth stalls and the NTA the discount is measured against falls.

**Earliest observable:** the FY2026 results, expected in Q1 2027 on last year's timing (26 February 2026), with the leverage ratio, NTA, interest coverage and 2027 guidance. Within two quarters. The Vietnam completion notice may come earlier, and CTP's refinancing of the 18 February 2027 bond will be visible in its debt page or a bond release.

The capital allocation is the part I find hardest to defend. Leverage was 46.1% at end-2025 "due to the acquisition and market entry in Italy in Q4-2025" (FY2025 release), and rose to 46.6% by September. Yet in October and November CTP launched a buyback and agreed a US$500m purchase in Vietnam, where the standing assets yield about 4.6% (US$23m NOI on ~US$500m). That is below the 6.5% gross yield at home and the 10% yield on cost of its own developments, and it is barely above the 3.4–3.6% its new bonds cost. The consideration also includes development sites and the platform, so 4.6% understates the eventual yield, but on today's income the deal is thin. CTP has not said how it will pay. A company that was a pure Central European nearshoring story a year ago is becoming an Asian logistics developer, and the shareholder who decides that holds 73.3% of the shares. Minority holders have no say.

Second, the discount to NTA may be right rather than an opportunity. NTA rests on CTP's own valuations. H1 already took a −€83.0m revaluation, "mainly" a "slight valuation decrease in Romania" ([H1 release](https://ctp.eu/news/ctp-n-v-h1-2026-results/)). With the US 10-year at 4.9% and CTP's own new debt costing 3.4–3.6%, a 6.5% gross yield leaves a narrower spread than the years of 1% bonds did. Third, the earnings measure has moved: from 2026 CTP capitalises development interest, which raised adjusted EPRA EPS and its restated comparatives. The guided "9–11% growth" is against a restated 2025, roughly €0.93 by my arithmetic from the guidance sentence, not the €0.85 first reported. On the first-reported basis it would look like 20%. Fourth, with 73.3% held by the founder, the free float is about 27%. That makes the shares less liquid, though three shares do not need much.
