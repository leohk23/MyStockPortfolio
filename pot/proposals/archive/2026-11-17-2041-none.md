## 2. Thesis to test after results

**The business:** NVIDIA sells processors, networking and computing systems, with software used to train and run AI models. Cloud operators and other computing customers buy that capacity; revenue depends on equipment demand rather than a subscription to a single model.

**What the market is missing:** a possible opportunity is that lower model costs expand useful inference faster than compute prices fall. This run does not certify a durable mispricing before the new figures.

**What has to happen:** operating growth must turn into customer cash without progressively greater NVIDIA-funded support, and the new outlook must survive the regulatory change. The balance-sheet checks below test that proposition; revenue growth alone cannot.

### What I examined

The draft's verdict (WAIT under D109, results tonight) is correct as a matter of rule, so I did not spend effort re-deriving it. I looked for the question nobody had asked, the Halma failure this lane exists for. Three stood out:

1. **Why the price fell.** P2 says what the market may be missing but never says what it is discounting, which is Rule 5's test. A 29% fall in a month has a cause, and the cause decides whether the 0th-percentile P/E is an opportunity or the risk, priced.
2. **What the CEO change actually is.** P6 files it as a "secondary change" and a "transition". A founder-CEO who leaves effective immediately, with no reason given, is not a routine succession.
3. **Where the revenue comes from.** P6's dominant risk is accounting, which is fair, but the draft quantifies no customer or geographic concentration at all. That is the exact gap Halma fell through.

**The CEO resigned, and the circumstances are an export-control matter.** The [8-K of 11 November](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvda-20261111.htm) says only that the Board "accepted the resignation of Jen-Hsun Huang ... effective immediately". The press accounts, which are narrative and not figures, tie the resignation to a relationship with a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore data-centre firm that [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/why-did-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resign-and-who-is-colette-kress) describes as NVIDIA's primary partner in Southeast Asia. It names neither company, but it is aimed at exactly that channel. The [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm) discloses no government investigation of NVIDIA itself. It predates all of this.

**Receivables are concentrated, and the concentration is widening.** The 10-Q: "Five direct customers accounted for 22%, 14%, 13%, 11%, and 10% of our accounts receivable balance" at 26 July, that is 70%. At year end it was three customers at 25%, 18% and 13%. On the draft's own proxy, receivable days rose from **51.38** at year end ($38.466bn against Q4 FY2026 revenue of $68.127bn, `earnings.json`) to **59.64** at Q2. The receivables build of $24.593bn in the half is real working capital. The 10-Q does not give revenue concentration by customer.

**The cheapness survives stripping the investment gains.** This is my own arithmetic, a sensitivity and not a certified basis. I take Q1's pre-tax equity gains of $15.936bn and Q2's $7.771bn (the draft's issuer figures), tax them at 17%, the midpoint of the guided rate, and divide by each quarter's implied diluted shares (net income ÷ EPS). Q1 FY2027 EPS falls from $2.39 to about $1.85 and Q2 from $2.46 to about $2.19, close to the issuer's non-GAAP $1.87 and $2.22. Trailing EPS becomes about **$7.10**, a P/E of about **21.6×** at $153.66. That is still far under the 5-year 10th percentile of 34.30× (`peCeiling` $271.289 ÷ $7.91). P/OCF, at the 2.68th percentile, carries no investment gains anyway. On the same adjustment, H1 cash conversion is about 75.7% rather than 63.1%. So roughly half the shortfall is non-cash gains and the rest is the receivables build. The decile verdict is not an artefact of gains.
