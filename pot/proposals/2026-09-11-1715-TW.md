## 6. The case against

**Theses:** **T2** — automated workflows can compress research/access pricing and execution fees;
**T1** — cheaper models lower the cost of building competing trading interfaces. Both are hypotheses
about economics, not proof that transaction-based execution is equivalent to seat software.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** execution becomes easier to substitute and lower-fee protocols
capture the growth, so activity expands without enough profit for shareholders.

**Earliest observable:** Tradeweb's monthly share/protocol mix (next 12 October) and MarketAxess's
quarterly credit fee per million (by 15 November), per P3. The August release already flags portfolio
trading as lower-fee; both observables are within two quarters, so **Rule 4 imposes no extra
haircut**. T1 has no separately attributable adoption series for TW — it is a possible cause of the
same pricing pressure, not a claimed measured tailwind, and Rule 2 keeps that answer in P3 rather
than in the thesis.

Secondary: LSEG control, investment gains obscuring ordinary earnings, and a weakly observed return
on the acquired asset base (ROIC and GPA blank locally). A trading network may defend margins through
counterparty access and integrations — a hypothesis to keep testing, not a guarantee.
