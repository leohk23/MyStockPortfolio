# EXPN.L — Experian plc

Ranked second in the [run report for this cycle](2026-09-13-1823-ranking.md), which accounts for all
93 watchlist names.

**What it does:** Experian keeps credit files on consumers and businesses — repayment histories,
balances, defaults, company records — collected from the lenders who contribute them, in the United
States, the United Kingdom, Brazil and about thirty other countries. It sells access to those files
and the decisions built on top of them: credit scores, affordability and fraud checks, identity
verification, and the software lenders use to run those checks inside an application. Banks,
credit-card issuers, motor and telecoms lenders and insurers are the paying customers, and a smaller
consumer arm sells memberships directly to individuals who want to see and improve their own file.

## 2. Thesis

Experian sells the same underlying asset to a broad set of lenders year after year, and the accounts
show that repeating steadily: revenue grew **12.26% to $8,445m** in the year to March 2026, and the
filed operating margin has sat at **23.87%, 23.83% and 24.21%** across the three filed years, which
is consistency rather than an elevated margin needing a story to defend it. Why now is the price:
the multiple has roughly halved across two fiscal years, from a 35.88–60.10× band in FY2025 to
26.73–43.72× in FY2026 and **21.73–30.65× so far in FY2027**, putting today's 22.99× at the 1.81st
percentile of 221 weeks against a 37.20× median. What the market appears to be pricing is a fade in
B2B decisioning economics as lending workflows automate — and the disclosed direction of that exact
segment has not faded but firmed: group organic growth was 8% in FY2026, and in the June 2026 quarter
group organic was **7%**, B2B **9%** and **North America B2B 11%**, so the de-rating is steeper than
anything Experian has yet reported.

That last sentence is the whole of the Rule 5 answer and it is deliberately narrow. It says the
price has moved much further than the disclosure has. It does **not** say the disclosure can see the
risk — P6 says it cannot, and P1 sizes for that.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:**
[T2](../theses.md#t2--ai-agents-compress-the-value-of-seat-priced-workflow-software) bears directly —
Experian sells B2B decisioning whose unit of work (pull a file, score it, return an answer) is
precisely the kind an agent performs end-to-end, and part of its B2B revenue is subscription rather
than per-transaction.
[T3](../theses.md#t3--ai-agents-create-a-control-layer-and-regulation-makes-it-compulsory) bears on
the tailwind side — its Activate and Agent Trust work is the non-human-identity and permissions
layer T3 describes — but T3's own caution applies: the launch release disclosed no adoption and no
revenue, so this is **optionality, not the thesis arriving**, and it receives no credit in P2 or in
the sizing.
[T1](../theses.md#t1--chinese-open-weight-models-reach-rough-parity-with-us-frontier-models-at-a-fraction-of-the-price)
does not bear.

**Dominant risk (`secular`):** AI agents reduce the number of paid credit decisions and data pulls
Experian is asked to serve, or move buyers from subscription to outcome pricing, and **Experian's
own disclosure cannot show it** — the company publishes organic revenue growth percentages and no
customer, unit or transaction counts at all, verified in the Q1 FY27 update for this proposal.

**Earliest observable:** North America B2B organic growth at the half-year results, locally dated
**18 November 2026** — two months away, so the *date* is close. But it is a revenue measure standing
in for a volume risk, and the volume measure is published nowhere on any cadence. Rule 4 applies on
that limb and **P1 takes half the allocation**. The nearest thing to a direct observable is external
and imperfect: Equifax's USIS Diversified Markets organic growth, quarterly.

Then the secondary arguments:

- **The thesis rests on a metric its own dominant risk says is blind.** P2 argues the de-rating
  outruns the disclosed deceleration. If units are already falling and price is carrying revenue,
  P2's evidence would look exactly as it does now. That is the strongest single objection to this
  proposal and it is answered with size rather than with prose.
- **Not the geographic diversification the London listing suggests.** Roughly 67% of group revenue is
  North American, per the FY26 results as read by the [08:13 Sweep](../sweeps/2026-09-13-0813.md).
  Leo's book is already 66.89% US ([signals.json](../../signals.json) `book.byGeography`), so this
  adds US exposure wearing a UK ticker.
- **Consumer Services grew 2%** in the June quarter against B2B's 9%. Nearly a third of the
  business is growing at barely the rate of inflation, and it is the half most exposed to a direct
  consumer relationship that an aggregator or a bank app can intermediate.
- **$792m of FY26 acquisitions to integrate**, per the FY26 results as read by the Sweep. Acquired
  growth is not organic growth, and the filed operating margin has to absorb the amortisation that
  the company's own Benchmark EPS removes.
- **The capital measures are entirely missing.** Leo's §2.4 extension asks what the capital earns and
  how hard the assets are worked, and for this name the repo answers nothing at all. The 17.2% ROCE
  quoted is Experian's own definition on Experian's own numbers. Blank is an answer and it is not
  replaced here by the 24.21% margin.
- **US consumer-credit cycle.** A large part of B2B volume moves with lending activity, which is
  cyclical and currently facing 4.79% five-year yields. A volume decline from the cycle and one from
  T2 would look identical in the only figures Experian publishes.
- **Combined exposure with the human book: none.** `holdings.json` holds no Experian and no credit
  bureau, and RELX — the nearest information-services comparator — is watchlist only, not held. This
  adds a new exposure rather than concentrating one.

**Why this is not 7532.T again.** The regret was buying a top-line rebound without the fundamentals,
the valuation, the forward outlook or the currency. Here the case is three years of filed operating
margin within 40 basis points of itself, 12.26% filed revenue growth, and a dated forward
expectation the company has left unchanged; the multiple is a filter, quoted with its real 221-week
span and with the flattering-median caveat stated; and the currency is a GBP ticket on a GBp line
with no conversion. What is missing — every local capital measure, and any disclosure of paid
volume — is named in P1 and in the risk above, and paid for with half a position rather than
explained away.
