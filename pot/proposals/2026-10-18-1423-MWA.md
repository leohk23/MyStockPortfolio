## 2. Thesis

Repair and measurement of installed water networks provide recurring equipment demand, and Mueller's business case is the conversion of that demand into manufacturing profit and cash.
Its local annual operating-margin sequence improved from 10.8% to 16.3% to 18.8% through FY2025, while the latest filing still shows positive cash after reinvestment ([earnings](../../earnings.json), [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1350593/000135059326000036/mwa-20260630.htm)).
My inference is that the market may undervalue the manufacturing improvements, but a small order is justified only while physical demand and cash productivity remain testable rather than assumed.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none.

**Dominant risk (financial):** Weak physical demand leaves added capacity and inventory consuming cash while pricing and temporary benefits flatter earnings.

**Earliest observable:** The issuer's quarterly volume commentary and cash-flow statement, at FY2026 results reviewed by 10 November, then the next quarter by 28 February 2027. The risk is observable within two quarters, so Rule 4 requires no additional haircut.

The [Q3 release](https://ir.muellerwaterproducts.com/press-releases/2026/08-05-2026-211118748) attributes gains partly to prices, tariff refunds and lower expenses while consolidated volume fell. A high gross margin therefore cannot establish durable pricing power. Municipal replacement demand does not immunise the business from construction weakness, distributor inventories or unsuccessful foundry investment.

**Why this is not 7532.T again:** filed profit and positive reinvestment cash support the business case; volume, inventory and after-charge margins test the story directly. The order is halved because these observations do not isolate all temporary earnings benefits.
