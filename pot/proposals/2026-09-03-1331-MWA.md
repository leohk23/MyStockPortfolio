## 2. Thesis

Mueller sells mission-critical valves, hydrants, metering and leak-detection equipment into North
American water networks, where the installed base, qualification burden and cost of failure support
replacement demand and help margins resist competition. Why now: four locally filed years show
revenue rising every year, GAAP operating margin widening from 10.1% to 18.8% and FY2025 ROIC
reaching 13.1%, while Q3 FY2026 still delivered 4.1% sales growth and a 20.4% GAAP operating margin
([earnings.json](../../earnings.json), [prices.json](../../prices.json),
[Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1350593/000135059326000034/exhibit991pressreleasefy26.htm)).
The market appears to treat the gain as temporary pricing and tariff help; what it may be missing
is that foundry investment, portfolio work and the faster-growing Water Management business have
reset normalized economics upward while the replacement need remains.

## 6. The case against

The margin expansion may be a peak, not a reset. Q3 gross profit explicitly benefited from higher
pricing and non-recurring tariff refunds while volumes were slightly lower; SG&A benefited from
lower incentive compensation, Water Flow Solutions sales fell 0.6%, and inventory absorbed
$59.9m of cash in the first nine months. If those aids reverse while inflation, foundry spending
and municipal or residential demand soften, a low-looking P/E is capitalising temporarily elevated
earnings and both earnings and the multiple can contract together
([Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1350593/000135059326000034/exhibit991pressreleasefy26.htm),
[Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1350593/000135059326000036/mwa-20260630.htm)).
