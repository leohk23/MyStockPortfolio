## 2. Thesis

**The business:** Cranswick processes pork and poultry, and makes cooked meats, convenience foods and pet food. It sells mainly to UK supermarkets (its three largest customers take 24%, 16% and 12% of revenue) and to food-service customers. It earns a processing margin on product made through its own farms, feed mills and factories, with input costs passed through customer pricing models.

**The engine:** filed operating margins were **6.41%, 7.01%, 7.06% and 7.81%** in FY2023–26, and operating profit rose every year, from £148.9m to £232.8m. Reported revenue grew **8.69% a year**, from £2,323.0m to £2,982.5m ([earnings.json](../../earnings.json), agreeing with the [issuer's five-year record](https://cranswick.plc.uk/investors/financial-highlights/5-year-history)). The filed return on invested capital was **13.13%, 12.90% and 13.44%** in FY2024–26 (`prices.json` `capital`, D104 basis). The margin is thin and steady, not high. What can erode it is the price retailers pay and the plants running below capacity.

**Why now:** the 24 November interim shows volume **+6.9%**. Operating profit before the pig-herd revaluation rose **7.4%**, from £112.0m to £120.3m (interim p.11 and Note 17). The statutory figure fell 2.5%, all of it the IAS 41 swing, a £6.1m loss against a £5.1m gain. The share then fell **5.9% on 25 November** to 5,060p, and is 9.4% below its 16 November close of 5,630p (`history.json`). Press coverage put that down to unchanged full-year guidance and second-half capex acceleration rather than an upgrade ([news summary](https://www.newsminimalist.com/articles/cranswick-shares-slip-35percent-as-half-year-results-miss-upgrade-hopes-4ee792b3), narrative only). That is no new fact about the business. The same figures were read and tested yesterday.

**What the market is missing:** my hypothesis is that the new capacity earns adequate incremental returns once it is full, and that volume growth now running above like-for-like revenue growth (+6.9% against +3.1%) is the plants filling, not discounting. This is a hypothesis. The capex still to come is what tests it.

**What has to happen:** FY2027 operating profit before the IAS 41 line must grow on FY2026's £235.0m, and expansion must still leave positive cash after all capital spending, leases and interest. P3 tests both.

**The spending is still ahead.** £376m of named projects compares with £75.8m of FY2026 residual cash. **The market's read.** The share fell 5.9% on the day after the interim, and 9.4% from 16 November. A business growing volume 7% and operating profit 7% at a below-median multiple can be fully priced if the next leg of capex earns less. A P/OCF ranking low in its own range does not say the coming capex will earn its keep.

**The pig market.** The GB standard pig price was at its 2026 low, 176.12p/kg in the week to 14 November, with a 59p gap over the EU reference ([FarmingUK citing AHDB, 21 Nov](https://www.farminguk.com/news/british-pig-prices-hit-2026-low-as-farm-losses-mount_69518.html)).
