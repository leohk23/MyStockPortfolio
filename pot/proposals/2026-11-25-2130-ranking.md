## Why the recovery does not earn an order

**§2.4b applies: FY2025 operating profit fell 5.8% in the local annual series**, from €157.1m to €148.0m, and revenue fell 2.2%. Local operating margins across 2022–25 were 11.90%, 7.83%, 11.66% and 11.23%. That is neither a steadily expanding margin nor evidence of a high-return compounder. The Q4 2025 report's rounded FY2024 EBIT comparator is €156m, rather than the local €157.1m; its implied decline is about 5.1%. Either comparison is a decline. I retain the explicitly local 5.8% in the screening history rather than silently switching comparators.

The case for buying deserves its strongest version. Nine-month sales were €1,105m against €937m, operating profit €94m against €87m, and operating cash flow €210m against €147m. Thus sales and operating profit have already resumed growth in 2026. Reported EPS, however, stayed €0.17; adjusted EPS rose to €0.20 from €0.16. The Q3 report supplies these comparisons (pp6,13–14). Local quarter revenues sum to the same €1,105m; local quarter operating income sums to €93m, a €1m difference from the rounded issuer year-to-date statement. For the year-to-date comparison I use the issuer's €94m.

**Growth:** the local 2022–25 reported EUR revenue CAGR is **+4.37%**, using €1,159.618m and €1,318.3m. Q3 reported group growth was +20%; a complete group organic price/volume split was **not disclosed** in the sources read. Collection's +19% existing-market growth includes acquisition and one-off contributions, so it is not a clean organic-volume number.

**Theses:** none. T1–T3 do not supply the case for these physical machines.

**Dominant risk (competitive):** the rollout buys lower-priced equipment from several suppliers, so unit growth fails to produce the assumed earnings and return on capital.

The outside check supports taking that risk seriously. [Envipco's 11 November Q3 presentation, pp14–15](https://storage.mfn.se/61437b47-92b8-4ff4-baa6-933158d7ba6b/envipco-q3-26-results-presentation.pdf) lists approximately **7,100 UK machine appointments across five retailers**, with a mixture of signed contracts and appointments. These are the rival's own claims, not independently audited market shares, and I do not add them to TOMRA's figures to manufacture a market-share denominator. The quarterly metric to follow is each company's signed/appointed UK machines and their conversion into revenue and margin.

The UK launch itself is real: [regulation 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/67/regulation/1/made?view=plain) and the [scheme operator's transition notice](https://exchangeforchange.co.uk/news/deposit-return-scheme-update-on-transition-arrangements/) support 1 October 2027 for England, Northern Ireland and Scotland. A statutory deadline establishes demand for collection, not the supplier's pricing power.

**Why this is not 7532.T again:** I do not buy the policy event and assume its spending becomes shareholder profit. The observable accounts show improving operations but flat reported nine-month EPS, while the event has weakened the growth assumptions. Cheapness against the old history may be compensation for that change. I cannot substantiate that the discount is wrong.

A future investment case should show FY2026 reported operating profit and EPS above FY2025, Recycling's Q4 expenses meeting its approximately €19m checkpoint, and evidence that UK wins are not sacrificing margin. A miss in the first two is an early warning; a second annual failure to grow reported per-share earnings despite the rollout would undermine the recovery case. These are research checkpoints, **not** an executable proposal's P3 or a sell instruction.

Next results are **12 February 2027**, confirmed by [TOMRA's 5 November calendar](https://news.cision.com/tomra-systems-asa/r/financial-calendar,c4420241). There is no new D127 booking: this verdict is EXCLUDE on the economic case, not WAIT solely for an unpublished document.
