## 6. The case against

**Theses:** **none**. Netflix sells consumer entertainment access, not task-priced workflow seats under T2 or frontier-model access under T1. Cheap generated video remains a relevant competitive scenario even though neither standing thesis names it.

**Dominant risk (competitive):** Other entertainment, including creator and generated video, takes viewing time while price rises and advertising temporarily conceal the loss in revenue.

**Earliest observable:** **Nielsen's monthly US-TV viewing shares**, next checked **9 October**, with publication lag explicitly retained. Netflix's global view-hours series moves to annual publication in Q1 2027, with no exact date established; the smaller allocation also pays for this slower and incomplete global observable.

[Nielsen's June release](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2026/fox-and-nbcuniversal-score-with-fifa-world-cup-2026-success-in-nielsens-june-gauge-reports/) places YouTube at 13.8% of viewing and shows competing sports and streaming services capturing attention. It also says Netflix viewing increased month on month; neither fact proves lasting displacement or immunity. A US panel will miss an international or mobile-led deterioration.

The company can increase content commitments ahead of cash receipts, and the exceptional payment inflates the impression of cash generation as well as earnings. Strong historical ROIC does not guarantee the next content dollar earns the same return. The warning and break deliberately remain able to fire while P2's revenue and margin figures still look healthy.
