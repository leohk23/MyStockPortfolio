## Research behind the PRU.L verdict

**What Prudential is.** It sells life and health insurance and savings policies in Hong Kong, mainland China (CITIC Prudential, a joint venture), Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia and other Asian markets. Bancassurance and agents do the selling. It also owns the asset manager Eastspring. It earns the margin on policies written and the spread on the assets held behind them.

**The oneOff flag first.** The Scan's 9.15x divides 969.4p by trailing EPS of 106p, which is IFRS. Prudential's own figures, checked against both releases:

| Basis | FY2025 | HY2025 | HY2026 | Trailing 12 months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IFRS basic EPS | 154.2c | 49.2c | 37.9c | 142.9c, about 108p (local 106p, agrees) |
| EPS on adjusted operating profit after tax | 101.4c | 49.3c | 58.4c | **110.5c, about 83.8p** |

Sources: [FY2025 results](https://www.prudentialplc.com/en/newsroom/company-news/2026/prudential-plc-2025-full-year-results/), [HY2026 results](https://www.prudentialplc.com/en/newsroom/company-news/2026/prudential-plc-2026-half-year-results/). Each figure was fetched by a scout and then re-read on the company page by this lane.

On the **approximate** operating-EPS roll-forward the multiple is **11.6x**, against 9.1x reported. Annual EPS less first-half EPS is not an exact second-half EPS when weighted shares change. Yahoo's recurring basis (`normEps` 72.3p) gives 13.4x, at the **10.9th percentile** of 138 weeks from 2024-04-05. No operating-basis percentile exists. **Second-reader correction:** the IFRS-to-operating gap is reconciled below. The reported screen still passes; the vendor recurring rank does not replace it or impose an extra filter.

**Local reconciliation, completed by the second reader.** `earnings.json` agrees with the issuer's attributable profit and **diluted** EPS. The summary's EPS is **basic**, not a rounding variant. The exact bridge and source are below; local figures remain unchanged.

**The engine is slowing where it starts.** For a life insurer, new-business profit (NBP) and sales (APE) lead operating profit by years. From the [Q3 update](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001116578/000165495426009534/a8645b.htm), 5 Nov:

- Q3 NBP was $709m, +1%, on APE −6% at constant FX. Over nine months NBP was $2,093m, +6%, with APE flat.
- H1 had NBP +8% and APE +3% CER (HY2026 release). FY2025 had NBP +12% and APE +6% CER.
- Hong Kong: "repricing actions supported double-digit growth in new business profit in Q3". That growth came from price and margin, with sales falling.
- Mainland China: NBP "declined significantly in the third quarter as sales volumes were constrained by recent changes to expense regulations in the bancassurance channel."
- **Second-reader correction:** the qualification concerns group NBP guidance specifically; the company retained its wider targets. Its mainland outlook and recovery expectation are recorded below from the issuer-hosted Q3 release.

Operating EPS grew 12% in FY2025 and 17% in H1 2026, which buybacks help ($1.5bn planned for 2026, per the Sweep's reading of the release). It reports the business written in earlier years, while current sales have weakened. §2.4b applies: the issuer itself describes APE as an indicative volume measure. **Second-reader correction:** management does identify a mainland recovery period, Q4. That supports a testable recovery case, but is not evidence that group sales have already recovered.

**The 7532.T test, answered: partly that mistake.** Visitor conversion in Hong Kong and bancassurance regulation in mainland China are separate risks. **Second-reader correction:** the latest Insurance Authority release checked does not supply the proposed visitor split. Use the issuer's comparable APE recovery test below; do not substitute total Hong Kong premiums or visitor arrivals for insurance conversion.

This is a judgement about the recovery, not a claim that §2.4b forbids all recovery purchases. Theses: none of T1 to T3 bears on this case.
