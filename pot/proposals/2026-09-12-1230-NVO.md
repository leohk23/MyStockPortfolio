# NVO — Novo Nordisk A/S (New York-listed ADR)

**What it does:** Novo Nordisk discovers, manufactures and sells prescription medicines, almost all of
them injectable or oral peptides. Two families carry the company: GLP-1 medicines for type 2 diabetes
and for obesity — Ozempic, Rybelsus, Wegovy and an oral Wegovy tablet — and insulins. It sells to
national health systems and hospital groups, and in the United States to insurers and pharmacy benefit
managers at rebated list prices, increasingly also direct to patients for cash. It makes the active
ingredient and the injection devices in its own plants in Denmark, the United States and France.
Revenue is patented drug sales; the cost is a very large and still-growing factory estate.

## 2. Thesis

Novo Nordisk earned an operating margin of **42.3%, 44.2%, 44.2% and 41.3%** in the four filed years to
December 2025, on revenue compounding from DKK 176,954m to DKK 309,064m — **20.4% a year** — and it
earned that on capital rather than only on sales: **ROIC 30.6%, gross profit over total assets 46.1%,
asset turnover 0.569×** in FY2025, after four years in which total assets more than doubled to
DKK 542,902m to build the capacity this category needs. Why now: the company has raised its own 2026
outlook twice this year, most recently on **4 August 2026** from −4%/−12% to **0% to −6% at constant
exchange rates** on both adjusted sales and adjusted operating profit, and Q2 adjusted operating profit
grew **+11% CER** after Q1's −6%.

What the market may be missing is arithmetic rather than opinion. The de-rating from a 31.0× median to
12.3–12.9× prices the loss of this category's economics as something already decided, and **the filed
accounts do not contain it yet**: FY2025's operating margin is one point below FY2022's, and the decline
the company itself guides for 2026 is nought to six per cent, not a collapse. The two most recent
quarters are distorted in **opposite** directions — a DKK 26,800m non-cash 340B provision reversal
inflates Q1 2026, DKK 6,300m of pipeline impairments depress Q2 2026 — so the screen that flagged this
name and the headlines written about it are reading two different companies, and neither is the one
filing the accounts. P5 corrects both, and the correction leaves the name in the cheapest part of its
own range rather than removing the reason to look.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** `none`.
[T1](../theses.md#t1--chinese-open-weight-models-reach-rough-parity-with-us-frontier-models-at-a-fraction-of-the-price)
concerns the price of frontier-model inference and the margin of whoever sells it; Novo neither sells nor
resells models, and buys no material AI input whose price is the question.
[T2](../theses.md#t2--ai-agents-compress-the-value-of-seat-priced-workflow-software) concerns software
priced per seat where the seat maps to a task; Novo sells a manufactured molecule under patent to health
systems, with no seat and no per-user pricing anywhere in the model. Neither bears — and because neither
bears, **Rule 5 does not bind**: this cheapness is not a declared thesis showing up as a discount. I have
argued the discount anyway in P2, because a de-rating from 31× to 12.5× deserves an explanation whichever
rule is in force.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** Eli Lilly's orally dosed orforglipron and its next-generation
injectable take the marginal obesity patient, and Novo defends on price — so US prescription volumes hold
or grow while realised net price per script falls faster, contracting revenue and the 41% operating
margin together.

**Earliest observable:** Novo's own Q3 2026 result, which gives North America Operations obesity-care
sales at CER together with the company's volume-versus-price split — locally dated **4 November 2026**
([prices.json](../../prices.json), `estimate: true`, so my deadline is 6 November). Eli Lilly's Q3 2026
release, carrying US Zepbound and Mounjaro revenue, arrives in late October 2026. **Both are inside two
quarters, so Rule 4 does not size this down**; the half allocation in P1 comes from §7.2a alone.

Then the secondary arguments, written to persuade rather than to dismiss:

- **The de-rating may be right and early rather than wrong.** Novo's ROIC has fallen in a straight line
  for three years — 56.6% → 41.1% → 30.6% — while total assets more than doubled. A 41% margin invites
  competition by definition, and this is what the first stage of that looks like on a balance sheet. The
  P2 case rests on returns that are, on the record, in decline. "Still 30%" is a comfort, not a trend.
- **US pricing is under attack from more directions than Lilly.** The 340B reversal in P5 is itself
  evidence of how much of Novo's US revenue is a negotiated rebate rather than a price; most-favoured-
  nation pricing proposals and direct-to-consumer cash channels push the same way. A company whose
  reported sales can move DKK 26,800m on a provision estimate is a company whose realised price is not a
  fact you can read off a label.
- **Patent expiry is dated and known.** Semaglutide's protection lapses first outside the US — Canada and
  China from 2026 — before the US cliff early in the next decade. Generic entry in large non-US markets
  is not a risk to discover; it is a schedule.
- **The pipeline write-off.** DKK 4,000m of the Q2 impairment was monlunabant, a compound the company was
  presenting as a pipeline asset a year ago. Writing off your own next thing is the most expensive
  sentence in a results release.
- **What this does to Leo's total exposure — the §3.3 disclosure.** Novo Nordisk appears **nowhere** in
  the human book: not in `signals.json` → `book.names`, which prices 60 of 60 positions totalling
  £128,687, nor in `byCompany`. So this **adds** an exposure rather than concentrating one — the opposite
  of NVDA's case, which sits at 7.15% of the book and has been proposed seven times. On geography the
  book is **66.88% US**; a Danish issuer bought through a US depositary receipt is a European economic
  exposure in a US-listed wrapper. Healthcare appears nowhere in the book at a weight worth naming. This
  is a disclosure and not an argument — diversification is not a thesis either, and the name still has to
  win on the numbers in P2 and P3.
