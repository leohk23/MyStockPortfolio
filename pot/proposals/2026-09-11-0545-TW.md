## 6. The case against

**Theses:** T2 bears on institutional trading workflows as a potential change in how clients execute; Tradeweb primarily charges for transactions rather than user seats. T1 bears indirectly through cheaper automation for customers and rivals. Neither establishes that Tradeweb keeps the resulting economics.

**Dominant risk (`competitive`):** competing execution channels and lower-fee protocols capture the economics of growing electronic volumes, leaving Tradeweb with weaker customer share or lower revenue per trade.

**Earliest observable:** September's monthly high-grade share and Q3 total-credit revenue/ADV plus the cash-credit fee table, reviewed on 9 and 30 October; the source and thresholds are in P3. MarketAxess's quarterly protocol data supplies the outside comparison. These are within two quarters, so Rule 4 does not independently require another reduction.

A high margin can attract cheaper substitutes; existing connections are a reason to investigate persistence, not proof of it. MarketAxess's portfolio-trading growth and the announced ICE deal strengthen that objection. T1/T2 could enlarge volumes while reducing what each venue collects, exactly the combination P3(b) tests. The current data do not show that every protocol shares one economic model.

Secondary risks are non-operating crypto volatility, the related-party data relationship with LSEG, acquisition accounting and USD/GBP movement. A low relative multiple may partly price a different earnings mix rather than undervalued execution profits.
