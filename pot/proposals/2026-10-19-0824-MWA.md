## 2. Thesis

Municipal repair and replacement provides repeat demand for physical water-network equipment without requiring a housing boom. The FY2025 reported operating margin improved from 13.8% to 18.2%, and the latest quarter still earned a 20.4% after-charge margin while cash remained positive after investment. My inference is that the market gives too little credit to the improved manufacturing economics, but the investment case requires those economics to persist as temporary benefits fade.

## 6. The case against

**Theses:** none.

**Dominant risk (financial):** Pricing, tariff refunds and a tax benefit conceal weakening physical demand and an unsustainable improvement in operating economics.

**Earliest observable:** The FY2026 volume bridge, after-charge margin and cash-flow statement, reviewed by 10 November 2026. This is within two quarters; Rule 4 adds no further reduction.

Utility specifications and an installed base may support repeat purchases, but do not eliminate competition or municipal budget delays. Housing weakness can affect the same factories; inventory absorption and charges can consume the gains. The current cash multiple is not in its cheapest decile, and the earnings-only signal must not be sold as confirmation from both measures.
