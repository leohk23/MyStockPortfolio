### CSL Limited — `CSL.AX`, ASX — new

**Why:** a plasma-therapy business (Healthcare, 1.00% of the book; Australia, 0%) that the market priced as broken. The provisional Yahoo monthly close was A$96.6 in April 2026. It is A$183.84 today, still down 15.9% on the year. FY26 was a declared "reset": underlying NPATA US$3.1b, down 2% at constant currency. A reported net loss of US$2.6b came after US$7.1b of pre-tax impairments. Cash flow from operations was US$3.5b, the dividend was maintained, and a further A$1.1b buy-back was announced. Ig sales were flat at US$6.2b, which the company puts down to its own US channel-inventory normalisation, Medicare Part D changes and earlier tender losses ([CSL FY2026 results, 18 Aug 2026](https://investors.csl.com/pdf/80148cbc-d117-4b5c-9213-628b0d1de681/Platform/ListPage/CSL-FY2026-Results.pdf)).

**What would have to be true:** the FY27 outlook holds. That outlook is revenue in line with FY26, underlying NPAT growth of about 5%, and Behring revenue up mid-single digits with Ig up mid-to-high single digits (same source). If it does, FY26's flat Ig was a one-off inventory correction, not lost share. Vifor is guided to fall about 25% and Seqirus faces declining US immunisation rates, so Behring must carry the whole thing. **7532.T check:** the price has already nearly doubled off the low, so this is not early. The recovery is a *guided* one, and the falsifier is plain: Ig growth in the 1H FY27 result (February). The CEO is still interim (Gordon Naylor). Impairments of this size suggest past capital (Vifor) earned below its cost, so ROIC on the remaining capital is the question the Deep dive must answer. Price and multiples above are provisional until the watchlist fetch carries them.

### Diageo — `DGE.L`, London — new

**Why:** a consumer brand owner Leo can watch first-hand (UK, Consumer Defensive 3.11%), with the price at 1,639.5p, near its five-year weekly low of 1,391.5p and down 10.2% on the year (provisional Yahoo). The new CEO, Sir Dave Lewis, is restructuring. FY26 organic net sales **fell** 2.0% (volume −0.4%, price/mix −1.6%), but organic operating profit rose 2.0% and free cash flow rose $463m to $3.2b. Pre-exceptional EPS was 165.3c (+0.7%), reported EPS 78.1c (−26.3%). Net debt is $20.5b, 3.1x adjusted EBITDA. The dividend was reset to 50c under a policy announced in February, and a new operating framework targets about $850m of savings over two years from FY27 ([Diageo preliminary results](https://www.diageo.com/en/news-and-media/press-releases/2026/2026-preliminary-results-year-ended-30-june-2026)).

**What would have to be true:** this is a **shrinking business** (§2.4b). FY27 guides organic net sales **broadly flat**, with North America **down** mid-single digit, and organic operating profit up low to mid-single digit. The medium term (FY27–29) guides low-single-digit sales growth and about $8b of cumulative free cash flow, with leverage at the midpoint of 2.5–3.0x by end-FY27, assuming the EABL and RCB disposals complete ([Capital Markets Day RNS, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/diageo--dge/diageo-capital-markets-day-and-outlook/9708741)). Growth resumes, if it does, in FY28 sales. The first observable is the Q1 FY27 trading statement on 5 November (date from the scout's read of Diageo's calendar, not verified). **7532.T check:** this is the closest of the two to buying a turnaround on story. What separates it is that the savings and cash are disclosed, dated and measurable, while the volume decline (spirits demand, GLP-1, US tariffs) is not explained away. The case fails if North American depletions do not stop falling.
