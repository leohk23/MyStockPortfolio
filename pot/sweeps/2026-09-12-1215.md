## 1. Where the world is

**Observation — the thing our data does not carry.** Japan had **3,442,100** foreign visitors in July,
a record July and **+0.1%** on a year earlier. That headline is false comfort. Inside it, arrivals from
**China were 428,200, −56.1%**; for January–July, China was **2,486,500 against 5,693,104, −56.3%**,
and the national total is **24,527,200, −1.7% — Japan's inbound count is shrinking**
([JNTO, 19 Aug 2026](https://www.jnto.go.jp/en/news/20260819.pdf), preliminary). The hole was filled by
Korea (+20.3% ytd), Taiwan (+21.8%) and Hong Kong (+8.6%) — lower-spend, shorter-stay, repeat visitors
standing in for the highest-spending cohort in Asian retail. The cause is dated and political: PM
Takaichi's Taiwan remarks in November 2025 drew a Chinese travel advisory, and arrivals have not
recovered since
([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/11/15/china-issues-travel-warning-for-japan-over-threats-to-intervene-in-taiwan),
[Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2026/02/19/japan/visitors-january-japan/) — press, for
narrative; the counts above are JNTO's own).

**Observation — two more things land on the same businesses in the same quarter.** From **1 November
2026** Japan's tax-free system stops being an exemption at the till and becomes a **refund claimed at
departure after Japan Customs confirms export**; the general/consumable split, the sealed-bag rule and
the ¥500,000 daily cap all go, and the export window becomes 90 days (2025 Tax Reform amendments to
the Consumption Tax Act, per
[Grant Thornton Japan](https://www.grantthornton.jp/en/insights/news-letter/tax-bulletin/202512/) —
professional commentary, see §4 for what I could not reach). And the currency has turned: **USD/JPY
153.554, −3.68% over a month and −4.00% over three** ([prices.json](../../prices.json)), with the BoJ
meeting **17–18 September**, its policy rate at **1.0% since 16 June** on a 7–1 vote
([BoJ](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/state_2026/index.htm)), July nominal cash earnings
**+4.7%** and real wages **+2.4%**, a seventh straight rise. The FOMC sits **15–16 September** with a
Summary of Economic Projections ([Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm)),
still at 3.50–3.75% after three July dissents to raise.

**Inference.** The interesting claim is not "Japanese tourism is weak" — it is that **the tourist-retail
trade and the tourist-volume trade have come apart, and only one of them is in a price.** Chinese
outbound travel is at records; it has been *redirected*. Thailand's and Korea's beneficiaries have
noticed — Airports of Thailand is **THB 61.50, +62.9% over a year** — while the Korean name most
levered to Chinese arrivals sits at a 52-week low (§3). Meanwhile a Japanese retailer whose tax-free
economics are about to change mechanically on a known date is guiding profit to *flat*. Two
second-order notes that cut against the obvious reads. First, a strengthening yen is a **cost tailwind**
to Japanese importer-retailers at the same time as it is a revenue headwind on tax-free — the two are
not the same sign, and the market has been pricing only the second. Second, I checked the crowded
version of the BoJ trade and it is gone: **Dai-ichi Life ¥1,883, +53.4% over a year and within 4% of
its 52-week high**, Japanese life insurers having already taken the 30-year JGB story. Naming a lifer
here would have been the 7532.T mistake in a new suit — a true macro story bought after the discount
closed. Same reason no Thai name appears below.

Our own block is unchanged from Friday and I will not re-list it, except for one figure worth holding
against the war narrative: **gold $4,390, −12.09% over six months** while WTI is +74.44% ytd and the
5-year yield is +28.14% ([prices.json](../../prices.json)). Real yields, not conflict, are setting the
gold price right now.
