## 1. Where the world is

**Observation — two central banks tightened inside 48 hours.** The FOMC raised the target range to
**3¾ to 4 percent** on 16 September on a **12–0** vote, saying "Inflation remains elevated" and
describing activity as expanding at a solid pace, with "productivity growth is strong, and capital
investment is robust"
([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)).
The Bank of Japan changed its **Guideline for Money Market Operations** this morning; the decision
document is dated 18 September 2026 on the BOJ's own news list
([BOJ](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/whatsnew/index.htm),
[statement PDF](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2026/k260918a.pdf)).

**I am not quoting the new Japanese rate.** The BOJ publishes both decision documents as CID-keyed
PDFs; the repo's own `pdfText()` recovers the header "September 18, 2026 Bank of Japan" and nothing
else, and `WebFetch` cannot read a PDF either. The press reports 1.25% on a 7–2 vote, the highest
since 1995, and under §7.3 that is narrative rather than a figure I may write down. The Fed's case
went the other way only because federalreserve.gov posts the statement as HTML.

**Observation — policy is not moving the currency.** USD/JPY is **157.313**, up **6.33%** over a
year and **2.53%** in the past week; the dollar index is **100.277**, up only **2.70%** over a
year; WTI is **$101.05**, up **76.29% year to date** and **15.05%** over the past month
([prices.json](../../prices.json)). That is after a coordinated US-Japan yen-buying intervention in
early August, the first joint purchase of yen since 1998
([Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/08/03/markets/japan-us-joint-yen-intervention/)).

**Inference.** The equity market read the Fed's hike as credibility rather than restraint: the S&P
500 rose **1.14%** and the Nasdaq **1.69%** on the day, while the 10-year yield fell to **4.947%**
from the 5.006% the last sweep recorded ([prices.json](../../prices.json)). Japan is the more
interesting half. Two hikes and a joint intervention have not stopped the yen weakening, which
points at the import bill rather than the rate gap — Japan buys essentially all of its oil, and oil
is up 76% this year. That is a live cost and translation risk sitting on 8.34% of the book, and
most sharply on its energy-intensive domestic manufacturers.

**Observation — past our seventeen series.** Hormuz has a second casualty that is not oil, and it
is metal. Iranian strikes in March hit Emirates Global Aluminium's Al Taweelah in Abu Dhabi and
Aluminium Bahrain's smelter. Both companies have since published what that costs them:

| Company, own disclosure | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Alba**, Q2 and H1 2026, 4 August ([albasmelter.com](https://www.albasmelter.com/en/article/alba-discloses-its-financial-results-for-the-second-quarter-and-h1-of-2026)) | Net finished production **155,469 MT, −61% year on year**. Sales volume **280,799 MT, −32%**, "reflecting the impact of regional shipping and logistics disruptions". Profit **US$172.5m, +164%**. LME averaged **US$3,576/t** in the quarter. |
| **EGA**, 2 July ([media.ega.ae](https://media.ega.ae/ega-reports-strong-early-progress-in-al-taweelah-restart-and-updates-on-business-performance/)) | **89 of 1,262 reduction cells** restarted. Hot metal "could take up to a year to reach pre-incident levels". "A return to pre-crisis shipment levels is expected, based on current conditions, to require the re-opening of the Strait." |

**Inference.** Production is down by more than half at two of the Gulf's largest smelters, the
operators' own timetable runs into 2027, and the recovery is conditional on a strait that has not
reopened. Norsk Hydro's Q2 report, dated 22 July, attributed easing supply concerns to "the U.S.
and Iran ceasefire, and subsequently partly opening of the Straight of Hormuz"
([Hydro](https://www.hydro.com/en/global/media/news/2026/hydros-second-quarter-2026-operational-strength-delivering-solid-results/)).
Tanker strikes resumed through August and intensified on 7–8 September
([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/7/oil-prices-surge-as-us-iran-strikes-intensify-in-strait-of-hormuz),
narrative only), and our own file has WTI up 15% on the month. So the physical constraint came
back and the metal did not: COMEX aluminium is **$3,463.25/t** (provisional, Yahoo `ALI=F`,
2026-09-18 05:30 UTC), and that contract carries the US tariff premium, so the LME price behind it
is lower still — below the $3,576 average Alba banked in Q2. Loud, then quiet, with the price
unresolved. That is the shape this lane is supposed to look for.

**Is this the 7532.T mistake again?** The resemblance is real: a shock-driven rebound story in a
cyclical, at spot prices near the high end of the range. Three things differ. The shock is a supply
outage with published restart timetables rather than a demand rebound anyone can imagine; the
share price has *not* responded, which is the opposite of the tourist trade; and the currency runs
the right way rather than the wrong way. But the trap is the same trap, so a Deep dive on this has
to price it on mid-cycle metal, not on $3,576/t.
