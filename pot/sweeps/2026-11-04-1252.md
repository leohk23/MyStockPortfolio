- **Indonesia.** On 15 October MSCI launched a consultation on moving Indonesia from Emerging Market to Standalone status. It cites "persistent opacity in shareholding structures and suspected coordinated trading behavior". It says it "expects to proceed with the proposed reclassification unless the consultation identifies clear evidence of material improvements". Feedback closes on 30 April 2027 and the decision comes on or before 30 June 2027. Until then MSCI will not implement increases to foreign inclusion factors or share counts, additions to the Indonesia IMI, or upward size migrations ([MSCI release](https://ir.msci.com/news-releases/news-release-details/msci-launches-consultation-proposal-reclassify-indonesia), which refuses scripts; read as [republished in full on Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/world-indices/articles/msci-launches-consultation-proposal-reclassify-214700173.html)). Bank Central Asia, already watched, is down 19.2% over the month and 43.3% over the year. The rupiah is at 18,155 to the dollar (`prices.json`).

The World block puts both markets among the furthest from their highs: in dollars, Indonesia's ETF needs +278% to regain its 2013 peak and Brazil's +222% to regain 2008's (`signals.json` `world`).

**Inference.** A passive reclassification is the cleanest kind of forced selling there is. The seller is an index rule, not a view on the business, and the timetable is published. It is also the kind that does not resolve quickly. The decision is eight months away, MSCI's stated default is to proceed, and the actual index deletion comes after that. So the selling may not be over, and anyone buying now is betting that the business is worth more than the price over years, not that the flows reverse soon. Brazil is less clean. An election is a fiscal question, and a fiscal question reaches a bank's earnings through rates and credit costs, so it bears on the business. The Indonesian case is the one taken forward.

### BBCA.JK — Indonesia Stock Exchange — Bank Central Asia — re-look (watched since 1 September)

- **Why.** It is priced as a forced sale while the business keeps growing. BCA's [nine-month release](https://www.bca.co.id/en/tentang-bca/media-riset/pressroom/siaran-pers/2026/10/28/03/29/kredit-tumbuh-126-bca-konsisten-dukung-perekonomian-nasional) of 28 October has net profit up 4.2% to Rp45.2tn, loans up 12.6% to Rp1,063tn, CASA up 10.4% to Rp1,103tn, deposits up 8% to Rp1,301tn, an NPL ratio of 1.8% and loan-at-risk of 4.6%. Locally it trades at 10.5x trailing EPS of Rp469, at the 0th percentile of its P/E record, with a 7.7% trailing yield over four payments (`prices.json`). The 08:57 ranking gives that record as 189 weeks from March 2023. It adds Indonesia, where the book has nothing (`signals.json` `book`), and adds to Financial Services (8.36%) rather than to US technology. This speaks directly to the 08:57 ranking, which holds BBCA at **WAIT** pending deposit costs, loan quality and a venue by 6 November. The release settles loan quality as reported and shows the deposit franchise growing. It does not give deposit cost, so that question stays open.
- **What would have to be true.** The fall has to be the index and not the bank. The release's own figures show one thing that is about the bank: profit up 4.2% on loans up 12.6% means each rupiah lent is earning less. The release gives no margin to explain it, and that has to be read in the full financial report before any order. **The 7532.T test:** Leo named the currency in that regret, and it applies here. The rupiah is at 18,155 to the dollar, and a 7.7% yield in rupiah is not 7.7% in pounds if the currency keeps sliding. Unlike 7532.T, though, this is not a narrative bought at its peak. It is the opposite: a sound bank sold by a rule, with a published date for the next forced step (30 June 2027). The case is 1-2 years of holding through further index-driven selling, not a quick re-rating.
