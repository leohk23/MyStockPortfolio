# Sweep — 4 September 2026

## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** The oil shock remains an economic constraint rather than a one-day headline. On
4 September the US Treasury widened its campaign against Iranian oil-finance networks by
designating a Turkish bank and its subsidiaries. The repo's 21:46 UTC snapshot has WTI at $91.22,
up 6.4% over seven days and 47.4% over one year. The EIA's latest weekly observation has US retail
diesel at $5.599 a gallon on 31 August, versus $3.734 a year earlier. **Inference.** Another crude
producer is the obvious and probably well-owned expression. The quieter question is which
transport networks can preserve economics through fuel surcharges and gain freight where road
haulage becomes less competitive. Sources: [US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/treasury-severs-iranian-regime-s-financial-lifelines-turkiye/),
[local markets](../../prices.json) and [US EIA diesel series](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/pet_pri_gnd_a_epd2d_pte_dpgal_w.htm).

**Observation.** North American rail traffic is already growing, although the data do not prove
that fuel is the cause. In the week to 29 August, US total traffic rose 4.1% from a year earlier
and intermodal units rose 5.7%; year-to-date total traffic was up 3.3%. Canadian intermodal units
rose 7.7% for the week, but Canadian total traffic was only 1.3% higher year to date. **Inference.**
That is enough to investigate a modal-shift beneficiary, not enough to assume one. The candidate
must show company-level volume, surcharge recovery, efficiency and cash conversion, because a
railway also consumes fuel and a stronger week can reverse. Source: [Association of American
Railroads](https://www.aar.org/news/aar-reports-weekly-rail-traffic-for-the-week-ending-august-29-2026/).

I went beyond the local macro block for retail diesel, sanctions enforcement and rail traffic by
mode. The repo supplied the current market backdrop and was not used to set the scope.

## 3. Candidates

### CNR.TO — Canadian National Railway — Toronto Stock Exchange

**Why it deserves work:** This is a second-order energy-dislocation watch, not a bet that dear
diesel automatically sends freight to rail. CN's second quarter had revenue ton miles up 5%,
revenue up 11% and first-half free cash flow up 19%; locomotive fuel efficiency improved 3%.
The counter-evidence belongs in the same sentence: intermodal revenue rose 8% while intermodal
revenue ton miles fell 1% and carloads fell 5%, the operating ratio worsened by 80 basis points,
and operating expense per gross ton mile rose 9%. CN's published
intermodal surcharge also moves with the EIA diesel price, giving the transmission mechanism a
company source rather than an assumption. Sources: [CN second-quarter
results](https://www.cn.ca/en/news/2026/07/cn-delivers-on-commitments-with-strong-second-quarter-results-an/)
and [CN fuel surcharge schedule](https://www.cn.ca/en/customer-centre/prices-tariffs-transit-times/fuel-surcharge/).

**What would have to be true:** Intermodal volume must turn up and total revenue-ton-mile growth
must persist after separating grain and energy commodities; surcharges and efficiency must protect
rather than merely inflate revenue; the operating ratio and cash return on the C$2.8bn capital
programme must improve. The
provisional Yahoo snapshot puts the shares up 26.2% over one year and on 21.5 times trailing
earnings, so the independent repo fetch must show that earnings growth or a later price makes the
valuation filter defensible. This adds Canadian geography, but its North American freight exposure
only modestly offsets a book already 66.3% US. Source: [provisional Yahoo price](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CNR.TO/)
and [local book](../../signals.json).

This is a candidate, not an order. The recent share-price gain and weaker cost ratio are reasons to
wait for independently fetched fundamentals, not details to wave away.
