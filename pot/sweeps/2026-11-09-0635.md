**Inference.** China has gone from exporting deflation to paying more for its inputs. But it cannot pass the cost to its own shoppers: purchaser prices are up 7.9% while consumer goods are down 0.1%. Chinese downstream manufacturers are absorbing the squeeze. A rise of this size is the kind of shift this lane exists to trace. The obvious beneficiaries are Chinese miners, and section 3 shows why the biggest of them did not benefit. For Nvidia, the investigation is a question about the chief executive and export controls, not about demand. The repo's P/E floor was struck on trailing earnings two days before the news. The Review, not this lane, should weigh whether the floor still means what it did, after the 17 November results and the 18 November briefing.

### Screened and not carried

- **China Coal Energy (1898.HK).** Coal prices are up 36.6% on the year, but the company's output fell. Its own filing for the nine months to September ([HKEX, 23 October](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/1023/2026102300458.pdf), read with the repo's `pdfText`):

  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | Attributable profit, RMB m | 4,599.5 | 4,782.1 | −3.8% | 12,748.8 | +2.1% |
  | Commercial coal produced, Mt | | | | 93.38 | −8.1% |
  | Coal bought in and traded, Mt | | | | 70.07 | −20.2% |

  The price is high because supply is being held back, by safety inspections after a deadly mine accident in Shanxi in May ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/china-september-coal-imports-fall-6-on-high-inventories-indonesia-supply-constraints-2026-10-14), narrative). The producer sells less, and the price covers it with nothing to spare. The share is HK$10.17, 30% below its weekly high of HK$14.62 in the week of 26 April (Yahoo, provisional). That fall looks earned, not mispriced. China Shenhua (1088.HK), the integrated coal and power group, is flat over six months at HK$43.84 (Yahoo, provisional). It was not read further.
- **Coal exporters to China.** If Chinese mines are held back, imports should fill the gap. They have not. Customs data put September coal imports 5.7% lower than a year earlier, and up only 2.2% for the nine months. Indonesia's own production quotas and low river levels limited what it could ship. Mongolia's shipments rose 40% over the nine months ([SteelOrbis](https://www.steelorbis.com/steel-news/latest-news/chinas-coal-imports-increase-by-22-percent-in-january-september-2026-1483749.htm), [Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/china-september-coal-imports-fall-6-on-high-inventories-indonesia-supply-constraints-2026-10-14), both narrative citing China's customs agency, not checked at [GACC](http://english.customs.gov.cn/Statics/6192bee0-d609-4238-94f9-9f901428b1ef.html)). Adaro (AADI.JK, +35.3% on the year) and Bukit Asam (PTBA.JK, +28.4%) have already moved (Yahoo, provisional). October's customs figures come out tomorrow. Not carried. On the 7532.T test, this is a supply shock from one accident and one policy, and buying a miner on it is a bet on the rebound.
- **FMC (FMC).** Beijing has told pesticide makers to stop selling below cost. The China Crop Protection Industry Association issued guidance on 10 February, and the NDRC and the market regulator followed with a joint notice on 3 September. Generic Chinese chlorantraniliprole rose from about $32.76/kg in mid-year to $41.69/kg on 18 October ([Global Agriculture, 30 October](https://www.global-agriculture.com/crop-protection/chinas-pesticide-technical-prices-turn-higher-as-regulators-crack-down-on-below-cost-selling/), trade press, not checked at source). That molecule is FMC's Rynaxypyr, and cheap Chinese copies of it are what broke FMC. If the Chinese floor is lifting, FMC's price war should ease, which makes it a second-order beneficiary. But it arrives too late to matter. FMC's own [third-quarter release](https://www.fmc.com/en/articles/fmc-corporation-reports-third-quarter-2026-results-and-revised-fourth-quarter-outlook) gives revenue of $106m against $542m a year earlier. That figure is after $639m of price and rebate cuts to clear stock held by distributors in the Americas, and includes a $288m impairment on Rynaxypyr. The press puts the loss at $2.07bn. The company is already exploring a sale ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fmc-corporation-sets-2026-priorities-and-announces-exploration-of-strategic-options-including-but-not-limited-to-the-sale-of-the-company-302679488.html)). The share is $5.63, against $138.40 in April 2022 (Yahoo, provisional). With no earnings to value, it cannot pass the valuation filter Leo uses. Not carried. Leo can revisit the idea if a sale is announced.
