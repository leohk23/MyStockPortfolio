The cause of this morning's Tokyo fall, which the 11:26 sweep could not find, is now reported. Its [final prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm) prices 980,000,000 Class A shares at **$83**, which comes to **$81.3bn** (my arithmetic). Disclosure: Anthropic makes the model writing this sweep. It is not a candidate, and I take nothing from the listing except its size.

**Inference.** The 11:26 sweep read the sell-off as a repricing of the cost of money, because yields and the dollar were rising alongside it. Today that stopped. The 10-year fell about 12bp while equities fell, which is the older pattern: bonds hedging a growth or risk scare. Gold and bitcoin falling with everything else looks like deleveraging rather than a flight to safety. The selling is aimed at **whoever is paid by AI capital spending** (chips, memory, Japanese and Taiwanese suppliers), not at whoever spends it. That makes the second-order questions sharper. First, who is paid for the build-out by a regulator rather than by the AI return? Second, who buys AI rather than selling it (T1's tailwind)? The two candidates below take one question each. My inference about the IPO is weaker and goes no further than this: $81bn of new AI equity in one session has to be paid for by selling something, and the obvious something is the rest of the AI trade. I have no flow data to show that. This section went beyond the macro block for the Jiji report and the prospectus.

### NG.L, London Stock Exchange: National Grid, new

- **Why:** it is paid for building the grid that AI and electrification need, and the payment comes from regulators on its asset base, not from anyone's AI return. Its [half-year results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1004315/000165495426009528/a8672b.htm) (5 Nov, the 6-K copy of the RNS) report:
  - underlying EPS **36.5p, up 22%**, statutory EPS **26.9p**
  - capital investment **£6,707m, up 33%**, with FY27 guided at about **£13.5bn**
  - FY27 underlying EPS guided **up 15–16%** from the FY26 baseline of **78.0p**, and **8–10% a year** to 2030/31
  - interest-rate exposure is small: a sustained **100bp** rise above its forward assumptions "would impact 2030/31 EPS by around 1 pence", because debt costs are recovered through regulation and about 10% of net debt is rate-sensitive
  - interim dividend **16.97p** (35% of last year's full dividend), with growth in line with UK CPIH

  The price is **1,227.5p** (Yahoo, **provisional**), ex that dividend. A year ago it was 1,138.5p, and its 52-week range on weekly closes is 1,118.5p to 1,391p. On the 78.0p baseline that is **15.7x** (my arithmetic, not on the local basis).
- **What would have to be true:** EPS has to grow 8–10% a year after the dilution that funds it. The guidance assumes **25% scrip take-up**, and net debt was **£48.6bn**, rising about **£4bn** more by March, with regulatory gearing heading to the high 60s. The next tests are Ofgem's RIIO-T3 reopener decisions, which it will file every six months, and the New York rate filings.

**The case against, and the 7532.T test.** H1 is flattered. The release itself names "one-off fair value gains in our NGP portfolio" among the drivers of the 22%, and statutory profit includes £333m of revaluation and divestment effects year on year. So the 15–16% for this year is not the run rate, and the run rate is the 8–10%. It is also less insulated from AI than it looks. It has committed **$1.75bn to Joulent**, which develops contracted power for US large-load customers, including a 2.7 GW plant in West Texas serving a Microsoft data-centre campus. And Micron's New York fab is set to become its largest single electric customer there. That part is unregulated and depends on exactly the capital spending being sold off today. This is not the 7532.T mistake: there is no tourist boom and no currency tailwind, and the release says a five-cent move in sterling-dollar is hedged around 70% at the asset level. The risk is a regulator, not a fashion. On exposure it adds to **Utilities (1.2%)** and the UK and offsets the technology concentration, which is the diversification this lane is told to prefer. No thesis drove it, although T3's point that a regulator makes something compulsory applies here, to the grid itself.

### INFY, NYSE (ADR of Infosys, also INFY.NS): Infosys, new

- **Why:** a **buyer** of AI, and the market has priced it as a victim. The [Q2 FY27 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1067491/000106749126000042/exv99w02.htm) (23 Oct, 6-K) reports:
  - revenue **$5,145m**, **+1.5%** year on year in constant currency
  - operating margin **21.0%**, stable
  - basic EPS **$0.20**, down 0.3%
  - free cash flow **$928m**, down 15.7%
  - large-deal TCV **$5.2bn**, 62% net new, including one deal over $2bn
  - AI revenue **10.5%** of the total, up 29% on the quarter
  - FY27 guidance **revised to 1–2%** constant-currency growth, margin 20–22%

  The price is **₹1,098** (Yahoo, **provisional**) against ₹1,560 a year ago (−30%). Its five-year low was ₹1,000.2 in the week of 20 September. TCS fell from ₹3,137.5 to ₹2,376 over the same year (−24%), so this is a sector de-rating, not one company. The story was loud and went quiet without resolving.
- **What would have to be true:** cheaper models (T1) have to make Infosys's delivery cheaper faster than clients cut what they pay for it. The evidence would be AI revenue keeping its growth while total revenue at least holds, and the $5.2bn of TCV turning into revenue at the same margin. The next test is Q3 results in January. I did not find the guidance range this revision replaced, so I cannot say how far it was cut.

**The case against, and the 7532.T test.** Infosys bills largely for people's time, which is close to the seat pricing T2 attacks, and its clients say so. In the release's own testimonials Crocs signs a ten-year deal to "reduce costs", and Metsä Group is simplifying its IT sourcing "to meet the efficiency demands". Every AI deal can shrink the bill it replaces. The currency trap behind 7532.T is here too, in mirror image. EPS grew **8.7% in rupees** and **fell 0.3% in dollars**, so the rupee growth was the currency, not the business. Judge it in dollars. On exposure it adds to **Technology (38.1%)**, so as a sector it does not diversify. What it changes is which side of the AI trade Leo holds: everything he owns there sells to the build-out, and this would be the first name that buys from it. It adds India, where he has no named exposure. It is a plain Indian company with no VIE. T1 drove it, and T2 is the case against.
