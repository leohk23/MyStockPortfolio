attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure, Yanbu among them

Qatar's Ras Laffan LNG plant is reported to have lost about 17% of capacity in March, with
repairs put at three to five years. That comes from a press summary
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis)) and I have not checked it
against QatarEnergy, so treat it as narrative.

**Inside the book, this is results week.** The repo carries Visa on 27 October, Alphabet and
Microsoft on 28, Amazon and BYD on 29, and AstraZeneca, which the pot holds, on 30 October
([prices.json](../../prices.json) `earnings`). Alphabet is down 6.7% over seven days and Amazon
5.5%. The Hang Seng is down 12.3% this year.

**Inference.** The money is made in the gap between an oil-priced world and US gas. Whoever buys
gas at Henry Hub and sells into prices set by oil, such as US ethane crackers and LNG exporters,
gets a windfall. Whoever burns diesel or bunker fuel is paying close to double. The obvious winners
(producers, tankers, refiners) are already on the watchlist: Woodside, Neste, Scorpio, BW LPG. The
two candidates below are less direct. One is a chemical maker that cut its dividend days before the
windfall arrived. The other is a newly listed Norwegian maritime supplier, whose products save fuel
on ships, at a time when fuel has rarely cost more.

## 3. Candidates

### KMAR.OL — Oslo Børs — Kongsberg Maritime — new

**Why watch it.** It was demerged from Kongsberg Gruppen and listed separately in April 2026
([press report](https://smartmaritimenetwork.com/2026/01/23/kongsberg-maritime-prepares-for-listing-as-stand-alone-company/)).
A six-month-old spin-off without a defence label tends to get less coverage than its parent. In Q2
it reported revenue of MNOK 6,651, EBITDA of MNOK 830 (a 12.5% margin), order intake of MNOK 7,231
(book-to-bill 1.09) and a record backlog of MNOK 28,406. Newbuild book-to-bill was 1.25 for the
half year
([company release, 13 July](https://www.kongsbergmaritime.com/news-and-events/news-archive/2026/kongsberg-maritime-q2-2026-record-high-backlog-and-strong-newbuild-execution/)).
It adds to European Industrials, has no AI-capex dependence (the reason Halma was rejected), and
works against the book's US weight.

**What has to be true.** Diesel at $5.27 a gallon should make fuel-saving retrofits pay back faster.
Yet the CEO said aftermarket "customer decisions remain slower, especially for larger upgrade
projects" (same release). That gap is the test: aftermarket orders have to pick up while fuel stays
dear, or the efficiency argument isn't reaching the accounts. The release gives no year-earlier
margin, so the full Q2 report has to be read before anyone calls 12.5% stable or falling. Q3 is due
at the end of October; I have not confirmed the date at source. **7532.T check:** a
newbuild-heavy backlog at a shipbuilding peak is exactly the rebound trap. The defence against it
is an aftermarket share that holds up when yards slow, not the size of the backlog.

### LYB — NYSE — LyondellBasell Industries — new

**Why watch it.** It is the plainest second-order winner from the oil and gas gap. It runs ethane
crackers on US gas and sells polymers priced against oil. On 20 February 2026, just before the war,
it cut its quarterly dividend from $1.37 to $0.69 because "markets [are] expected to remain
challenged"
([company release](https://investors.lyondellbasell.com/news/news-details/2026/LyondellBasell-recalibrates-quarterly-dividend/default.aspx)).
In Q2 it earned diluted EPS of $1.71, or $4.30 excluding identified items, with EBITDA of $2.1bn
before those items and operating cash flow of $752m. It also sold four European assets and said
Middle East supply recovery is "likely extending into 2027"
([Q2 release, 31 July](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001489393/000148939326000057/a2026q2ex991_pressrelease.htm)).
It adds Basic Materials, which the book barely holds, but it adds to the US weight rather than
reducing it.

**What has to be true.** The earnings have to be judged on what is left once the windfall passes,
and the valuation filter has to be read against 2024–25 earnings, not 2026's. The gap between
$1.71 reported and $4.30 adjusted is large. The Q3 release on 30 October
([company notice](https://www.lyondellbasell.com/en/who-we-are/updates-events/corporate--financial-news/lyb-to-discuss-third-quarter-results-friday-oct.-30-2026))
needs to explain the identified items and show cash conversion catching up with adjusted profit.
**7532.T check:** this is the closest to that mistake on the list. It is a cyclical whose 2026
earnings come from a war, and its own board was cutting for a downturn eight months ago. Watch it
for the rules to judge, not because the story is good.
