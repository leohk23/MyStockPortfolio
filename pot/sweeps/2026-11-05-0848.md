## 1. Where the world is

**Observed: the oil shock has reversed quickly, but not completely.** WTI is $87.90, down 15.6% over seven days but still up 53.4% year to date. Gold is $4,131.80, up 6.0% over seven days, and the US ten-year yield is 4.894% (`prices.json` `macro`). The Bank of England's next policy decision is due today at 12:00 UTC ([Bank of England calendar](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/events/upcoming-events)). Press expectations of a rate rise are a forecast, not a result; this sweep does not treat them as fact. The fast oil fall means the market can move from pricing an energy shortage to pricing relief before either company costs or monetary policy have caught up.

**Observed: the book still has little room for another technology beneficiary.** Technology is 39.12% and Communication Services 17.04% of the book, 56.16% combined. Healthcare is 1.15% and Europe 8.04% (`signals.json` `book`). The Nasdaq is down 6.75% over the month while the S&P 500 rose 1.58% yesterday. A cheaper technology share is not automatically diversification.

**Qualcomm, held, reported after yesterday's close.** Its [FY2026 release](https://investor.qualcomm.com/files/content_files/FY2026-4th-Quarter-Earnings-Release.pdf) reports $43.1bn of annual revenue, GAAP diluted EPS of $12.70 and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $10.54. QCT Automotive had its first $2bn quarter, up 89% year on year, and Automotive plus IoT revenue grew 36%. Qualcomm also renewed long-term licensing agreements with Apple, Huawei and Transsion. That is meaningful diversification inside Qualcomm; it does not diversify Leo's book away from technology.

**Nintendo, held, reported this morning.** Its [six-month release](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2026/261105e.pdf) reports sales of ¥966.0bn, down 12.1%, and operating profit of ¥216.5bn, up 49.1%. Switch 2 sold 6.49m units and digital sales rose 60.5%. The profit comparison was helped by a higher software mix and a first-quarter refund of US tariffs previously charged to cost of sales; Nintendo left its full-year forecast unchanged. This is better operating leverage than the sales decline suggests, but neither the tariff refund nor a mix step-up should be projected as recurring growth.

**Inference.** The useful second-order question is not “which stock rises when oil falls?” It is which business has a cost base that improves when fuel contracts and hedges reset, yet can survive if the reversal fails. The other useful direction is earnings that do not depend on the oil or AI cycle at all. That produced one deliberately conditional airline watch and one healthcare equipment watch. Neither is an order.

## 3. Candidates

### SHL.DE — Xetra — Siemens Healthineers

- **Why.** Today's [FY2026 results](https://www.siemens-healthineers.com/press/releases/2026q4) show comparable revenue growth of 4.0%, an adjusted EBIT margin of 16.2%, adjusted basic EPS of €2.42, free cash flow of about €2.4bn and equipment book-to-bill of 1.14. Q4 Imaging revenue grew 10.0% with a 21.9% adjusted EBIT margin. It adds a European healthcare earnings stream to a book with only 1.15% in Healthcare, and the evidence is orders and cash rather than an AI narrative.
- **What would have to be true.** The FY2027 guide of 4%–5% comparable revenue growth has to turn into per-share growth after the planned Diagnostics carve-out. Management guides adjusted EPS of €2.20–€2.40, whose €2.30 midpoint is below FY2026's €2.42, while Q4 Diagnostics revenue fell 4.2% and its margin was only 5.4%. **The 7532.T test:** the completed year grew, but the forward EPS guide does not. A Deep dive would have to reconcile the carve-out, debt and stranded costs before calling this a growing business. Add it to the watchlist for the local valuation; do not chase today's result.

### RYA.IR — Euronext Dublin — Ryanair Holdings

- **Why.** Ryanair is a clean test of whether the oil reversal can create a future margin tailwind in a structurally low-cost operator. Its [FY2027 half-year release](https://corporate.ryanair.com/news/ryanair-h1-pat-falls-15-to-e2-15bn-as-unhedged-oil-doubles-traffic-grows-6-at-lower-fares/) reports traffic up 6% to 125.8m and revenue up 3% to €10.08bn. It would add European transport exposure rather than another US technology name, and 80% of FY2027 fuel is hedged at about $67 a barrel, so it has time rather than an immediate need to bet on spot oil.
- **What would have to be true.** The cost relief must last into the FY2028 hedge window, when only 25% of fuel is hedged at about $95, and unit revenue must stabilise. In H1 average fares fell 4%, operating costs rose 10% and profit after tax fell 15% to €2.15bn. **The 7532.T test:** traffic growth is not business growth when profit and fares are shrinking. This is a watch for a possible earnings turn, not evidence that one has happened.
