## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** The WTO raised its forecast for 2026 merchandise-trade volume growth to 3.9% from 1.9%. The resilience is narrow: AI-enabling goods rose 67% year on year in the first half and supplied 47% of the growth in merchandise trade, while container throughput rose 3.9% through July despite the Middle East disruption. The same release cut expected services-trade growth to 3.3% from 4.8% ([WTO, 8 October](https://www.wto.org/english/news_e/news26_e/stat_08oct26_510_e.htm)). **Inference.** “World trade is resilient” and “the cycle is broad” are not the same statement. Nearly half of the growth came from the same AI capital cycle that already dominates Leo's book. A fresh semiconductor or cloud-infrastructure candidate would increase the concentration, so I looked at the physical trade layer instead. The two operators checked below did not clear the valuation and organic-growth questions.

**Observation.** US payrolls rose by only 29,000 in September, versus an average 45,000 a month over the prior year, while unemployment stayed at 4.2% and hourly earnings were 3.0% above a year earlier ([BLS, 2 October](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.htm)). At the same time, [local market data](../../prices.json) has the US 10-year yield at 5.244% and WTI at $91.85, up 54.4% over one year. **Inference.** That is weak labour demand without cheap money or cheap energy. It raises the value of repeat-purchase businesses with visible cash generation, but it does not waive the valuation filter. That combination, plus the book's thin defensive and healthcare exposures, is why Haleon is a candidate rather than an order.

## 3. Candidates

### Haleon — `HLN.L`, London Stock Exchange — new

**Why:** a familiar, repeat-purchase consumer-health business that adds to two thin parts of the book rather than to its 59.74% technology and communication-services concentration. H1 organic revenue grew 2.6%, with Q2 improving to 3.1% and splitting between 1.7% price and 1.4% volume/mix. Free cash flow was £769m and net debt was 2.5x adjusted EBITDA. The warning is visible: reported operating profit fell 2.6% while adjusted operating profit rose 8.2%, because restructuring costs sit outside the adjusted result ([Haleon H1 2026 results](https://www.haleon.com/news/press-releases/financial/2026/2026-half-year-results)).

**What would have to be true:** the 29 October Q3 update must keep FY2026 organic growth within the stated 3–5% range with volume/mix still positive, not merely price, and the full-year accounts must narrow the reported-versus-adjusted profit gap while leverage does not rise above the company's roughly 2.5x target. **7532.T check:** this is not a price rebound being mistaken for business recovery; volume and cash already exist. The risk is instead accepting a permanently “adjusted” margin while restructuring charges recur. Price and valuation remain unavailable locally until the watchlist fetch runs, so no buy case is being made.

That is pricing and new concessions doing more work than organic boxes. Until core volumes improve and new-terminal margins converge, the headline is too close to the 7532.T mistake in infrastructure form.
