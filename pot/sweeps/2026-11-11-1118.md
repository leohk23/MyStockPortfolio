### NESN.SW — SIX Swiss Exchange — Nestlé (new)

- **Why:** a loud story that has gone quiet while the volumes have come back. In Q3 2026 organic growth was **4.0%**, of which real internal growth (volume) was **2.8%** and pricing **1.1%**. For the nine months, organic growth was **3.7%** (RIG **1.9%**, pricing **1.8%**). Reported nine-month sales were **CHF 65,865m**, flat against **CHF 65,869m**, because currency took **3.7%** off. The 2026 guidance was kept: organic growth of 3% to 4% with RIG accelerating, an improving underlying trading operating profit margin, and free cash flow above **CHF 9bn**. Russia was deconsolidated from the end of September (about 2% of sales), with a non-cash charge of about **CHF 1.4bn** ([Nestlé, 22 October](https://www.nestle.com/media/pressreleases/allpressreleases/nine-month-sales-2026)). The shares are **CHF 86.32**, up **9.6%** this year and just under their one-year high of 87.23 (provisional, Yahoo). The recovery is therefore partly priced, and the pipeline's P/E history has to show how much.
- **What would have to be true:** volume has to keep leading pricing. RIG has to stay above 2% a quarter at the full-year results, with the margin improving, so that the growth is not a price increase being paid back. If FY2026 RIG falls back towards 2025's level, this is the old Nestlé at a recovered price.

**Exposure and thesis:** adds Consumer Defensive outside the US. It does not hedge megacap tech, but its earnings do not depend on it. No thesis drove it. **For the Deep dive:** the CHF 1.4bn Russia charge is a one-off that will depress 2026 reported EPS. Value it on the result before that charge, and say which basis. **7532.T test:** the risk is currency, in reverse. The franc took 3.7% off reported sales, so organic growth is the honest measure and reported growth understates it. The trap would be paying a full multiple for one strong volume quarter after a 10% rise. Leo may well buy its products, but shopping for them would not show whether RIG holds up. The filing would.

### EQNR.OL — Oslo Børs — Equinor (new)

- **Why:** it is the cleanest hedge in Europe for the chain of events now hurting the book: Hormuz keeps oil and gas high, that keeps inflation and yields up, and that de-rates long-duration tech. Equinor does not ship through Hormuz and sells pipeline gas into Europe. In Q3 2026 it realised a European gas price of **$20.0/mmbtu** and a liquids price of **$92.3/bbl**. Adjusted operating income was **$12.35bn** and net income **$2.54bn**; the gap is mostly Norwegian petroleum tax, which takes most of upstream profit. Production rose **2%** to 2,166 mboe/d, and the 2026 growth guidance went up from 3% to about 4%. The buyback for 2026 is up to **$3bn**, and the net debt ratio fell to **3.8%** ([Equinor, Q3 2026](https://www.equinor.com/news/equinor-third-quarter-2026-results)). The shares are **NOK 394.9**, up **66.6%** this year and **13.0%** below its highest weekly close, NOK 453.8 in the week ending 23 October (provisional, Yahoo).
- **What would have to be true:** the case has to work at normal gas prices, not at today's. Earnings that rest on $20 gas cannot be capitalised. The question for the Deep dive is what Equinor earns and distributes at a gas price near pre-war levels, and whether the shares fall far enough in a truce to be cheap on that basis. Watching it now is the point: when the spike reverses, the pipeline will already carry its history.

**Exposure and thesis:** adds Energy, a sector the book barely holds, and its earnings move against the part of the book that is falling. No thesis drove it. **7532.T test: this is the 7532.T mistake if it is bought now.** 7532.T was a rebound bought at its peak. A record quarter on a geopolitical price is the same set-up. The candidate is for later, not for today, and the Deep dive should not take it up while the realised gas price stays far above its pre-war level.
