## 1. Where the world is

**Observation:** cheaper crude has not restored normal energy conditions. The [IEA's November Oil Market Report](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-november-2026), published 13 November, forecasts a return to surplus in Q4, but says October's inventory increase was oil afloat while on-land stocks continued falling. Its forecast remains conditional on maritime security. I could read the agency's highlights directly, resolving the access problem in yesterday's sweep. Locally, WTI is **$82.49, down 15.1% over a month**, but still **up 39.5% over a year** (`prices.json`, `macro.CL=F`, 07:36 UTC). **Inference:** falling crude is a possible input-cost benefit, not proof that transport and working-capital costs have normalised. Look for businesses that can retain customers and earn cash through both directions of the shock.

**Observation:** yesterday's [ONS October inflation release](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/consumerpriceinflation/october2026) puts UK CPI at **3.6%, from 3.5%**; motor fuels made the largest upward contribution to the change. Core CPIH was **3.1%, little changed**. The local US ten-year yield is **5.066%** (`prices.json`, `macro.^TNX`). **Inference:** the household squeeze and expensive capital remain relevant even as oil falls. Procurement scale and outsourcing may help customers contain costs; insurers can reinvest at better yields. Neither mechanism guarantees shareholder returns: caterers can lose the saving to contract repricing, and insurers can lose it to claims or bond marks. These are the mechanisms behind the new candidates below, not a forecast of rate cuts.

**Observation:** AI demand and AI investment returns are different questions. [NVIDIA's 17 November release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx) reports October-quarter revenue of **$115.8bn, up 103% year on year**. Yet the local Nasdaq is **down 7.6% over a month** (`prices.json`, `macro.^IXIC`). The local NVDA quote is **$150.96**, while its `mrq` still says **26 July** and `earnings.json` ends its quarterly accounts in July. **Inference:** the business is growing while the market questions what that growth is worth. A freshly fetched price does not certify freshly updated earnings. T1's pressure on model economics remains a hypothesis to test, not evidence that chip demand has collapsed. Tencent's cash spending below is a concrete test of who ultimately pays. ONS inflation composition, the IEA physical balance and issuer releases all go beyond our macro block.

- **Why:** a different earnings driver from the tech core, with a measurable reinvestment benefit: Q3 reinvestment yield **4.7%** versus running yield **4.0%**, while regular investment income rose to **€2,314m from €2,092m**. [Issuer Q3 statement, 12 November](https://www.munichre.com/en/company/media-relations/media-information-and-corporate-news/media-information/2026/quarterly-statement-3-2026.integratortemplatesecure.html).
- **What would have to be true:** underwriting must earn an adequate return with normal catastrophe losses and less favourable renewals, rather than require another benign claims year. Check the FY2026 report and January renewal disclosures for risk-adjusted pricing, loss assumptions and reserve development.

**The case against and the 7532.T test:** Q3 net profit **fell to €1,742m from €1,997m**; major-loss expenditure was still below budget, **14.9% versus an expected 18%**. The raised **€6.6bn** annual target is guidance, not normalised earnings. Buying a low multiple on unusually light claims would repeat the mistake of buying the story without the economics. Adds **Financial Services** and a German issuer, but global insured risks remain global; it also overlaps the held Tokio Marine and watched insurers. No T1–T3 thesis drove it. [Yahoo listing](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MUV2.DE/): **€582, provisional**. Local P/E and cash-history checks remain outstanding.

### CPG.L, London Stock Exchange: Compass Group, new

The [21 July Q3 update](https://www.compass-group.com/en/media/news/2026/compass-group-third-quarter-trading-update-2026.html) reports **7.1% organic revenue growth**, **96% retention**, and half of new business wins from first-time outsourcing. That is customer evidence rather than an inflation-only revenue story.
- **What would have to be true:** new contracts must retain cash returns after mobilisation spending and food/labour repricing. At the issuer-scheduled [24 November full-year result](https://www.compass-group.com/en/investors/financial-calendar.html?y=2026), check net new business, cash conversion, acquisition spending and returns on the capital committed.

**The case against and the 7532.T test:** Q3 capex was running around **3.5% of sales**, with a higher full-year proportion expected; year-to-date net M&A spending was **$2.4bn**. ### 0700.HK, Hong Kong Stock Exchange: Tencent, re-look, already watched

- **Why:** yesterday's [Q3 release](https://www.tencent.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Tencent-Announces-2026-Third-Quarter-Results.pdf) tests T1 directly: revenue **grew 8%**, but non-IFRS operating profit **fell 6%**; reported IFRS operating profit **fell 7%**. - **What would have to be true:** compute prepayments must convert into paying usage and cash, with AI losses narrowing rather than being perpetually excluded from the success measure.
