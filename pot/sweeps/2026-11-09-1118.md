## 3. Candidates

### HDFCBANK.NS — National Stock Exchange of India — HDFC Bank (new)

Also listed in New York as `HDB`, one ADR for three shares. On Friday's closes the two were at parity: $25.14 × 93.93 ÷ 3 = ₹787, against ₹788.3 in Mumbai (Yahoo, provisional, this lane's arithmetic).

- **Why.** India's largest private bank is cheaper than it has been in a long time, while the business is still growing. From its results for the quarter to 30 September, filed with the SEC on 19 October ([6-K exhibit 99](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144967/000119312526424680/d166042dex99.htm)), standalone:

  | Quarter to 30 September 2026 | Figure | Change on a year |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | Net interest income | ₹346.2bn | +9.7% |
  | Profit after tax | ₹195.1bn | +4.7% |
  | Profit after tax, excluding treasury gains | | about +12.6% |
  | Treasury trading gains | ₹7.8bn | ₹23.9bn a year earlier |
  | Deposits at period end | ₹33,274bn | +18.8% |
  | Gross advances | ₹32,195bn | +16.3% |
  | Net interest margin, on total assets | 3.16% | |
  | Gross NPAs | 1.11% | 1.24% a year earlier |
  | Common equity tier 1 | 17.1% | |

  The fall in profit growth is a fall in trading gains, which do not recur. On the bank's own figure, the rest of the profit grew about 12.6%. Deposits are growing faster than loans, and the ratio of loans to deposits is now 96.8% (this lane's arithmetic, from the two rows above). That ratio is what the 2023 merger with HDFC Ltd broke.

  **The price.** Trailing diluted EPS, standalone, is ₹49.50: ₹48.40 for FY2026, less ₹23.88 for its first half, plus ₹24.98 for this one. At ₹798.15 that is 16.1x. On the consolidated figures it is ₹51.88 and 15.4x. That figure includes a ₹6,949.27 crore pre-tax gain on selling shares in its lending subsidiary HDB Financial, which note 8 books in the half year to 30 September 2026. So the recurring consolidated multiple is higher than 15.4x. Price to book is 2.1x on March's consolidated book of ₹378 a share. All of this is this lane's arithmetic on the filing, with the price from Yahoo. The pipeline will strike its own P/E and P/E Low once the ticker is fetched, and those win.

  **What it does to the book.** It adds Financial Services (8.4% of the book) and offsets Technology and Communication Services (55.9%) (`signals.json` `book.bySector`). It adds a country the book does not hold. `book.byGeography` has no India line, against 63.2% in the US. It diversifies more than anything in recent sweeps. No thesis drove it.

- **What would have to be true.** Three things, each checkable at the next results in January.
  - **The margin stops falling.** Time deposits grew 22.8% and current and savings deposits 10.8%, so CASA is now 31.6% of deposits. The bank is paying up for the funding it needs to bring the loan ratio down. If the margin on total assets falls below 3.16% again, the cheapness is earned.
  - **The new management is not a reset.** Anup Bagchi took over as chief executive on 27 October ([6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144967/000119312526434998/d158742dex99.htm)). The deputy managing director, Kaizad Bharucha, named by the board as a principal architect of its credit framework, leaves on 31 December ([6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144967/000119312526424682/d124031dex99.htm)). The head of business banking retired on 31 October ([6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144967/000119312526443068/d170465dex99.htm)). That is three senior departures in a quarter. A new chief executive often clears the decks in their first set of results, and January's provisions will show whether this one does.
  - **The rupee does not take the return.** Leo's 7532.T regret names yen depreciation. A sterling holder of an Indian bank carries the rupee too, and the rupee is the currency most exposed to Hormuz.

  **The 7532.T test.** Half of this passes and half does not. The franchise case is not a rebound story: the de-rating is a year old, and the filing shows growth without credit trouble. But the last fortnight is a rebound. The board granted options at ₹692.80 on 24 October ([6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144967/000119312526431019/d176059dex99.htm)), and the share has risen 17.3% since its ₹680.20 close on 27 October (Yahoo, provisional). Most of that came with the oil fall. Buying it this week would be buying the oil hope as much as the bank. **Familiarity:** Leo has no first-hand view of an Indian bank, which is the basis §2 says he buys on. This is a name to understand from its 20-F before anything else, and the 20-F is on [EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144967/000119312526322004/d88195d20f.htm). It is a candidate, not a case.
