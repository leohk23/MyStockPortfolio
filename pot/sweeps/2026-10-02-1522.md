## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** Manufacturing demand has broadened, but it is not a clean disinflationary recovery. The global manufacturing PMI reached 53.0 in September, its highest since February 2022, with new orders at the strongest pace since that month; the compiler also says conflict-related inventory building contributed to demand. In the US survey, new orders were 55.3 and order backlogs 56.4, while customers' inventories remained "too low" at 41.6. Sources: [S&P Global's September manufacturing survey](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2026/10/global-manufacturing-pmi-hits-highest-since-february-2022-but-price-growth-also-accelerates), [Institute for Supply Management's September report](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/pmi/september/). **Inference.** Real production is improving, but some orders are insurance against shortages and prices rather than final demand. Backlog and revenue growth therefore need cash conversion and end-customer evidence before they count as a durable cycle.

**Observation.** The cost side tightened at the same time. [Local market data](../../prices.json), updated at 11:20 UTC, has WTI at $89.21, up 46.53% over one year, and the US ten-year yield at 5.237%. Eurostat's September flash estimate put euro-area inflation at 3.8%, up from 3.2% in August, with energy inflation at 18.8%; the US manufacturing prices index rose to 77.9, and no surveyed industry reported lower raw-material prices. Sources: [Eurostat's September flash HICP](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/web/products-euro-indicators/w/2-02102026-ap), [ISM](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/pmi/september/). **Inference.** The useful second-order question is who can preserve returns on capital while customers restock and inputs rise. A business merely exposed to factories can report growth and still lose economics through lower margin, more working capital or expensive capacity.

## 3. Candidates

### Midea Group — `0300.HK`, Hong Kong Stock Exchange — new

**Why watch it.** Midea is a PRC-incorporated A/H-share company rather than a Cayman VIE, adding a directly listed Chinese consumer-and-industrial operator. Its first-half filing shows the tension worth tracking: other-country revenue grew 5.5% while China's domestic appliance retail market contracted 9.9%; Building Technology and Robotics & Automation revenue grew 10.8% and 10.3%, respectively. Group revenue grew only 3.5%, however, profit attributable to owners grew 1.7%, gross margin slipped to 25.0% from 25.5%, and Industrial Technology revenue fell 12.7%. Source: [Midea's 2026 interim report filed with HKEX](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0828/2026082802074.pdf).

**What would have to be true.** Overseas own-brand growth and the building/robotics businesses must become large enough to offset weak Chinese appliance demand without purchasing growth through margin or working capital. First-half operating cash flow of RMB37.6bn was sound, but trade and note payables rose RMB27.2bn while receivables rose RMB18.6bn; the next filing must show that cash conversion is repeatable and that the current manufacturing upswing restores Industrial Technology rather than merely raising input costs. The 7532.T mistake would be buying a broad China/manufacturing narrative while ignoring the filing's slower profit, lower margin and segment decline. Source: [Midea's cash-flow reconciliation](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0828/2026082802074.pdf).

No standing thesis drove it. It reduces reliance on US Technology and Communication Services, although it adds to existing China and Consumer Cyclical exposure rather than filling an empty geography or sector. `0300.HK` was added to [the watchlist](../../watchlist.json); valuation and price are deliberately left to the repository's independent fetch.
