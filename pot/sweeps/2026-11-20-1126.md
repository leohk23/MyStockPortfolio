### 1929.HK, Hong Kong Stock Exchange: Chow Tai Fook Jewellery, new

- **Why:** the most direct test of what cheaper gold does to demand. In the quarter to 30 September, Mainland same-store sales rose **2.2%** while same-store **volume fell 9.7%**. Mainland RSV fell **5.3%** and is **83.3%** of the group. The group RSV figure was **−0.5%** ([HKEXnews operational data, 22 Oct](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/1022/2026102200383.pdf)). Customers have been buying fewer grams at higher prices. Gold is down 23.6% in three months, and the share price is **HK$10.92**, close to its 52-week low of HK$10.63 on weekly closes (Yahoo, **provisional**).
- **What would have to be true:** Mainland volume has to turn positive as the price per gram falls, by more than the gross margin gives back. Stock bought at higher gold prices now sells against a lower spot price. The test is the interim results, due to be approved on **Tuesday 24 November** ([board-meeting notice](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/1109/2026110900213.pdf)): gross margin, the size of the gold-loan gain, and any comment on October and November trading.

**The case against, and the 7532.T test.** The headline profit is not the earnings. The [positive profit alert](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/1022/2026102200335.pdf) guides H1 net profit up **90–100%**, "mainly attributable to" a fair-value gain on gold-loan contracts against a loss a year earlier. That is a hedge revaluation, and it will reverse when gold turns. Any P/E struck on it is wrong.

The growth that does exist is in Hong Kong and Macao: RSV **+33.2%**, same-store sales **+29.6%**, weight-based gold same-store sales **+43.5%**. That is Mainland visitors buying across the border, which is close to the tourist-rebound mistake behind 7532.T itself. Treat it as the weak part of the case, not the strong part.

On exposure, it offsets technology but adds to Consumer Cyclical and to China, so it does less for diversification than its geography label suggests. Check the corporate structure in the annual report rather than assume it is a clean non-VIE listing. No thesis drove it.

### AJB.L, London Stock Exchange: AJ Bell, new

- **Why:** a business holding customer cash while money is expensive, and growing fast. The [year-end trading update](https://www.ajbell.co.uk/group/investor-relations/market-announcements/year-end-trading-update-6) (22 Oct, year to 30 Sep) reports:
  - platform AUA **£126.0bn, up 22%**
  - customers **796,000, up 24%**, of which direct-to-consumer **602,000, up 30%**
  - net inflows **£9.7bn, up 37%** (FY25: £7.1bn)

  The share price has not followed. It is **538p**, against 504.5p a year ago and a 52-week high of 643p (Yahoo, weekly closes, **provisional**). A story that went quiet without resolving is what this lane is told to look for.
- **What would have to be true:** revenue and margin have to grow with the customer count. The trading update **says nothing** about interest income on customer cash, pricing or competition, and those are what decide it. The test is the full-year results on **3 December**: revenue per £ of AUA, the share of revenue from interest on cash, and any change to its charges.

**The case against, and the 7532.T test.** Customer counts are the easy number. Buying them without the unit economics would be the same mistake as buying footfall. The competition is apps charging little or nothing, and Leo's own pot broker, Trading 212, is one of them (§10). Leo can judge that first-hand, which is the familiarity §2 asks for. It also cuts against the name: if the low-cost apps are winning, AJ Bell's price is the margin that gives. If UK rates fall, the cash income falls with them. UK CPI was 3.6% in October ([ONS, cited 19 Nov](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/consumerpriceinflation/october2026)), so a fall is not imminent, but it is not something to bank on either. On exposure it adds to **Financial Services (8.4%)** and to the UK, and offsets the US technology concentration. No thesis drove it.

### PNDORA.CO, Nasdaq Copenhagen: Pandora, new, watch only

- **Why:** the obvious winner from falling silver and gold, which is why it is a watch and not a case. The price has already moved: **DKK 914**, against a 52-week low of DKK 440.9 and a high of DKK 933 (Yahoo, weekly closes, **provisional**). The [Q3 release](https://www.pandoragroup.com/news/27971) (4 Nov) reports:
  - organic growth **6%**, of which like-for-like **4%**
  - FY2026 guidance narrowed to **2–3% organic** and a **22.5–23% EBIT margin**
  - Q3 margins include a one-off partial refund of a US IEEPA tariff claim
- **What would have to be true:** cheaper metal has to reach the cost base, and when depends on the hedge book. The release page says nothing about metals or hedging. It sets a **2027 EBIT margin target of 14–15% including one-offs, 15.5–16.5% excluding** (previously above 12% and above 14%), far below the 22.5–23% guided for 2026, and the page does not explain the gap. Reconcile that from the FY2026 annual report's hedging note before treating cheaper silver as a tailwind for 2027. It was added to the watchlist because I cited its price (`sources.md` rule 3). It is not yet an idea.

**Considered and left out: BOC Hong Kong (2388.HK).** My hypothesis was that the HKD peg imports US rates into Hong Kong banks' margins. [BOCHK's own Q3 review](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/1030/2026103000626.pdf) (30 Oct) contradicts it for now:

- Q3 net interest margin **narrowed 3bp quarter on quarter to 1.52%**, "owing to increased pressure on funding costs as the yield curve steepened and market competition in deposits intensified"
- Q3 profit after tax **fell 9.2% quarter on quarter**
- over nine months, profit was **up 9.0%** and impairment charges fell **HK$998m**, against a base inflated by real-estate downgrades

Expensive money is not reaching the bank's shareholders. It is going to depositors. Not added.
