# Sweep — 4 September 2026

## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** The US employment report released after the previous sweep recorded 162,000
additional nonfarm jobs in August and an unchanged 4.1% unemployment rate. That is much stronger
than the 31,000 average monthly gain over the preceding year, but 59,000 jobs came from food
services and 42,000 from local-government education, where the increase largely reversed July.
Information lost 23,000 jobs. **Inference.** This is not the broad labour collapse that would make
an imminent rate rescue the investment case, nor is it broad strength: the composition favours
businesses paid for necessary physical services or measurable workflow savings over a general
consumer or software bet. Source: [BLS August employment report](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm).

**Observation.** Manufacturing added 16,000 jobs, including 6,000 each in machinery and fabricated
metals, while home health care added 11,000. The repo's 20:18 UTC snapshot still has the US 10-year
yield at 4.784%, the S&P 500 up 19.1% over one year and WTI up 47.8%. **Inference.** The new payroll
number strengthens the physical-economy thread from the earlier factory-orders release, but high
rates, energy costs and a strong equity market leave little room for a merely plausible story.
Candidates need company-level volume, cash conversion and balance-sheet evidence; sector hiring
alone is not evidence that a particular operator is winning. Sources: [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm)
and [local markets](../../prices.json).

## 3. Candidates

### LULU — lululemon athletica — Nasdaq

**Why it deserves work:** This is a fallen-compounder watch, not a reflexive dip buy. Second-quarter
revenue fell 4%, comparable sales fell 9% and Americas comparable sales fell 12%; a $134.5m tariff
refund lifted operating margin by 560 basis points, making the reported result materially better
than the underlying quarter. The filing now guides to a 5%–7% full-year revenue decline. Source:
[Lululemon's filed second-quarter results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1397187/000139718726000126/lulu-20260802xex991.htm).

**What would have to be true:** Comparable sales must stabilise because product relevance and the
new leadership are repairing demand, not because more stores or promotions obscure a damaged
brand. Gross and operating economics must recover without tariff refunds, and the independently
fetched valuation must compensate for a reset that may be structural rather than cyclical.

### ADUS — Addus HomeCare — Nasdaq

**Why it deserves work:** Necessary care has company-level evidence behind the employment signal:
second-quarter revenue rose 8.0%, personal-care organic revenue rose 6.8% and hospice organic
revenue rose 11.1%. Six-month operating cash flow was $92.4m and revolving debt fell by $60.0m.
The counter-evidence is visible too: home-health revenue declined as patient volumes fell. Sources:
[Addus results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1468328/000143774926025448/ex_997368.htm)
and [Addus 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1468328/000143774926025678/adus20260630_10q.htm).

**What would have to be true:** Growth in client hours and census must persist after separating
acquisitions and reimbursement increases; state and federal payors must keep rates aligned with
caregiver wages; and cash conversion must survive normal receivable timing. The Illinois waiver
renewal and the company's large exposure to one state payor make this a reimbursement-underwriting
exercise, not a simple ageing-population thesis.

### GWRE — Guidewire Software — New York Stock Exchange

**Why it deserves work:** Against broad information-sector job losses, this insurer-specific
workflow platform reported 19% constant-currency ARR growth, $389.7m of operating cash flow and
$149.9m of GAAP operating income for fiscal 2026, up from $41.1m. The narrower vertical and core
system role are more interesting than a generic AI label. Source: [Guidewire's SEC-filed annual
results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1528396/000152839626000039/gwreex991earningsrelease73.htm).

**What would have to be true:** Subscription growth must convert into per-share GAAP cash earnings
after the $181.8m of stock compensation, not merely into a non-GAAP margin. The $606.3m of buybacks
must create value rather than offset dilution, and valuation must allow for slower cloud migrations
or insurer budgets without requiring the current ARR rate forever.

These are candidates, not orders. LULU is explicitly a watch for proof of repair, not a conclusion
that a lower share price has fixed deteriorating fundamentals.
