**Observation.** Outside the local macro block, the OECD's new medical-supply-chain study identifies a less crowded resilience problem. Although **84 companies** sell injectable corticosteroids in the EU, national markets commonly depend on only **one or two suppliers per molecule**. Of 20 identified API certificate holders, six are in the EU, two in the US, nine in China, two in India and one in Malaysia; surveyed manufacturers named sterile fill-and-finish and internationally sourced APIs and intermediates as critical bottlenecks ([OECD report](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/strengthening-the-eu-s-medical-supply-chains_cc235a9c-en.html)). This goes beyond the repo, which has no medical-product concentration or shortage map.

**Inference.** Another AI-capital-cycle name has to clear a high bar when almost three-fifths of the book is Technology plus Communication Services. The medical evidence instead points toward the infrastructure around injectable products and outsourced manufacturing. That produced two Healthcare candidates, but it does not make either cheap: Stevanato must turn new capacity into cash, and Oxford Biomedica must turn contracted work into positive earnings. No standing thesis in [`theses.md`](../theses.md) directly drives either name.
