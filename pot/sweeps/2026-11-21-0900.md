Outside the AI trade, the week's largest regulatory event was in US healthcare. On 16 November CMS

extrapolate the findings **from payment year 2018**, and save "nearly $90 billion" over 2027–2036.
Comments close after 60 days, so in mid-January. On Yahoo's weekly closes (**provisional**) UNH fell
from $407.46 to **$339.85 (−16.6%)** that week, and Cigna from $297.00 to **$270.90 (−8.8%)**.

**Inference.** Two things are now being sold at once and should be kept apart. The first is the AI
supply chain, where Nvidia has an unquantified governance and accounting question on top of a
capex worry. That is the Review's business on a 4.5% position, not this lane's. The second is
everything else that sold with it. Gold and bitcoin fell with equities for a second day while bonds
rallied, which still reads as forced selling rather than a view. A forced seller does not tell
apart a company that is exposed from one that merely sits next to it. Both candidates below are the
second kind: one is a Japanese retailer that fell with Tokyo for no reason of its own, and one is
an insurer that fell with a rule aimed at a business it sold 20 months ago. Beyond the macro block,
this section used the Bloomberg/Reuters report, CNBC, TrendForce and CMS.

### 3382.T, Tokyo Stock Exchange: Seven & i Holdings, new

- **Why:** a loud story that went quiet without resolving. In March 2025 the company promised
  about **¥2 trillion** of buybacks by FY2030, funded by the superstore sale to Bain and an IPO of
  7-Eleven Inc. "by the second half of 2026" ([company release, 6 March
  2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/seven--i-holdings-announces-plan-to-unlock-shareholder-value-through-leadership-changes-and-transformational-capital-and-business-initiatives-302394438.html)).
  In April it pushed the IPO to April 2027 at the earliest ([CSP Daily
  News](https://www.cspdailynews.com/company-news/seven-i-holdings-delays-north-american-ipo-fiscal-year-2027),
  narrative only). The price is **¥2,153** (Yahoo, **provisional**) against ¥2,144 a year ago. Its
  52-week range on weekly closes is ¥1,811 to ¥2,417. The company's [own
  figures](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/financial/highlight.html) and
  [forecast](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/financial/forecasts.html), updated 8 October:

  | | H1 to Aug 2026 | H1 to Aug 2025 | Change |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Revenue | ¥5,460.3bn | ¥5,616.6bn | −2.8% |
  | Operating income | ¥232.3bn | ¥208.4bn | +11.5% |
  | Net income to owners | ¥124.4bn | ¥121.8bn | +2.2% |

  | | FY to Feb 2026 (actual) | FY to Feb 2027 (forecast) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Operating income | ¥423.0bn | ¥425.0bn |
  | Net income to owners | ¥292.8bn | ¥278.0bn |
  | EPS | ¥118.81 | ¥121.90 |
  | EPS before goodwill amortisation | | ¥169.81 |

  That puts it at **17.7x** forecast EPS and **12.7x** before goodwill amortisation (my
  arithmetic). Japanese GAAP amortises goodwill and IFRS does not, so the second figure is the one
  comparable with a European or US retailer. EPS rises while net income falls because the share
  count falls: the two years imply about **2,464m** and **2,281m** average shares, **7.4%** fewer
  (my arithmetic). The buyback is happening.
- **What would have to be true:** the North American stores have to recover enough to list. Until
  then the buyback is funded by one sale rather than two. The test is the Q3 results in early
  January, and the IPO decision from April.

**The case against, and the 7532.T test.** The forecast is weaker than the half. H1 earned ¥232.3bn
of operating income against ¥425.0bn for the year, so the forecast implies about **¥192.7bn** for
H2, against ¥214.6bn last year, about **10% lower** (my arithmetic). The company does not explain
it on the pages I read. Revenue includes US fuel, so it moves with the oil price, and the IPO delay
was blamed on weaker fuel demand and thinner store traffic. This is closer to 7532.T than anything
else today, and should be judged on that. Both are Japanese retailers, and the yen is the trap, in
reverse. 7532.T's error was buying a tourist rebound that a weak yen had inflated. Here most of the
profit is earned in dollars and reported in yen at **158.5** (`prices.json`), so a stronger yen
would shrink it. Judge the US business in dollars. There is no tourism case here. On exposure it
adds to **Consumer Defensive (3.6%)** and Japan, and it offsets the technology concentration. No
thesis drove it.

### CI, New York Stock Exchange: The Cigna Group, new

- **Why:** it fell **8.8%** in the week CMS proposed the Medicare Advantage audit rule, and it no
  longer sells Medicare Advantage. It sold that business, with its Part D plans, to HCSC on 19
  March 2025 (Note 1 of its [Q2 2026
  10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1739940/000173994026000065/ci-20260630.htm)). Its
  [Q3 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001739940/000114036126042030/ef20084818_ex99-1.htm)
  (5 November) reports:
  - revenue **$73.7bn**, **+6%**
  - adjusted income from operations **$7.98** a share against $7.83, and shareholders' net income
    **$5.47** against $6.98
  - 2026 adjusted EPS outlook raised to **at least $30.60**, from $30.45
  - pre-tax adjusted earnings of **$1.83bn** at Evernorth (pharmacy benefits and specialty
    pharmacy) and **$1.19bn** at Cigna Healthcare (employer medical)
  - 18.5m medical customers, +2% since year-end

  At **$270.90** (Yahoo, **provisional**) that is **8.9x** the adjusted outlook (my arithmetic).
  Its 52-week range on weekly closes is $257.85 to $318.05. UNH, which is still the largest
  Medicare Advantage insurer and fell twice as far, is at **16.6x** its own adjusted outlook of at
  least $20.50 ([Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000731766/000073176626000255/earningsrelease3q26unh.htm), my arithmetic).
- **What would have to be true:** Cigna has to carry little or none of the audit exposure for
  payment years **2018 to 2024**, when it still owned the plans. That is the open question, and I
  could not answer it. HCSC bought the legal entities, and the audit liability could have gone with
  them, or Cigna could have indemnified it. EDGAR's full-text search finds "risk adjustment" and
  "HCSC" together in the FY2025 10-K and the Q2 2026 10-Q, but the passage did not render through
  the fetch. A scout found nothing either. This is the first thing a Deep dive must read, in the
  10-K's contingencies note and the March 2025 purchase agreement. Cigna paid **$172m** in 2023 to
  settle a False Claims Act case on its Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment practices ([Cigna
  newsroom, 29 September 2023](https://newsroom.thecignagroup.com/2023-09-29-The-Cigna-Group-Affirms-Commitment-to-Medicare-Advantage-Customers-Resolves-Allegations-Related-to-Industry-Risk-Adjustment-Practices)),
  so the history is not clean. The comment period closes in mid-January.

**The case against, and the 7532.T test.** The market may be right for a different reason. Evernorth
is a pharmacy benefit manager, and PBM reform is the policy risk the midterm result makes likelier.
The press reports Senator Warren calling for the large health conglomerates, Cigna among them, to
be broken up. That is press only, and I did not find her statement. The release also mentions
"uncertainties surrounding participation in government-sponsored programs". The gap between
adjusted and reported EPS is large: $7.98 against $5.47 in the quarter, falling year on year while
the adjusted figure rose. A CPA should want that bridge before trusting 8.9x, and I did not
reconcile it. Not the 7532.T mistake: no boom, no currency. On exposure it adds to **Healthcare
(1.3%)**, the smallest sector in the book, and it offsets technology. It is still a US name, so it
diversifies by sector and not by country. No thesis drove it.
