## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** Europe is directing capital towards physical bottlenecks, but the evidence is at
three different stages. On 14 September the European Commission opened applications for the next
electricity, storage and smart-grid Projects of Common or Mutual Interest list; successful projects
will not reach the final Union list until late 2027. On 10 September it launched the European
Radioisotope Valley Initiative after identifying ageing facilities and dependence on foreign
suppliers, particularly Russia, in a network serving more than **10 million medical procedures a
year**. These are policy frameworks, not company orders
([grid call](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/call-applications-candidate-energy-infrastructure-projects-common-and-mutual-interest-electricity-2026-09-14_en),
[radioisotope framework](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/nuclear-medicine-new-eu-framework-secure-europes-supply-medical-radioisotopes-and-reduce-dependence-2026-09-10_en)).

**Observation.** The UK rail announcement is already enforceable business: Alstom signed contracts
worth more than **€1.2bn**, comprising about **€930m** for 29 battery-electric trains and **€230m**
of maintenance, with the order to be booked in fiscal Q2 2026/27. Delivery starts in 2032. That sits
against Alstom's June backlog of **€102.8bn**, Q1 sales growth of **4.9%**, a weak **0.5×**
book-to-bill ratio and guidance for positive full-year free cash flow
([contract](https://www.alstom.com/press-releases-news/2026/9/alstom-and-transpennine-express-sign-eur12-billion-contracts-britains-first-mainline-battery-electric-trains),
[Q1 release](https://www.alstom.com/press-releases-news/2026/7/alstoms-first-quarter-202627)).

**Inference.** The investable question is not whether Europe wants resilient infrastructure; it is
which suppliers turn long-dated policy and backlog into cash without destroying the return on the
capital needed to deliver it. This points towards non-US Industrials and Healthcare, both useful
counterweights to Leo's book, but it also recreates the 7532.T trap if an order headline is mistaken
for profitable execution. Alstom and Eckert & Ziegler therefore merit tracking; the grid call alone
does not justify adding another cable or electrical-equipment name to an already crowded watchlist.
