### EL.PA — Euronext Paris — EssilorLuxottica (new)

- **Why:** it makes Varilux and Stellest lenses and Ray-Ban and Oakley frames, and it builds
  Meta's AI glasses. The shares are **€182.35** (13 November close), against **€306** a year ago,
  which is **−40%**. Its highest weekly close was **€318.90** on 9 November 2025, its low **€139**
  on 13 September 2026, and it is +12% over three months (Yahoo chart endpoint, provisional).
  Over the same year, by the company's own account:
  - **H1 2026** ([release, 28 July](https://ml-eu.globenewswire.com/Resource/Download/6e24c0f8-175d-4eb7-97f0-637cfd94c0d8)):
    revenue **€14,818m, +9.7%** at constant currency (+5.7% reported). Adjusted operating margin
    **18.6%**, up 80bp at constant currency to 18.9%. Adjusted operating profit **+15%** at
    constant currency, reported operating profit €2,296m (+14.7%). Diluted EPS **€3.37**
    against €3.00. Free cash flow €1.07bn against €0.96bn.
  - **Net debt rose** from €10.85bn at December to **€13.39bn** at June, including €3.91bn of
    leases (same release). The release attributes Asia-Pacific growth partly to consolidating
    Top Charoen in Thailand, "almost 2k stores added" since April.
  - **Q3 2026** ([release, 20 October](https://www.essilorluxottica.com/api/getCapContent/?download=false&id=307258)):
    revenue **€7,486m, +7.2%** at constant currency, nine months **€22,304m, +8.9%**. The
    traditional Eyecare and Medtech business grew more than 6% and the myopia portfolio 24%.
    Wearables grew 20%, but in Professional Solutions they grew only "mid-single digit ...
    against an increasingly challenging comparison base".
  - **Its own outlook** over five years is "a solid growth of its total revenue and a broadly
    aligned growth of the adjusted operating profit". That is no promised margin expansion.
- **What would have to be true:** the earnings rose while the price fell, so the whole fall is
  the multiple. My inference is that the 2025 peak priced AI glasses, so the case has to stand on
  the business underneath: lenses, myopia control and retail growing above 6% without the
  wearables. With margins guided flat, the return has to come from growth alone. The jump in net
  debt has to be the Thai acquisition and not a trend, and the next annual results will show
  which.

**Exposure:** adds to **Europe** and to healthcare-and-eyewear, and offsets the US and megacap
tech. Its sector label arrives with the pipeline's fetch, so I do not assert one. One overlap: the
wearables tie part of the story to META, which Leo holds at 3.3%. **Thesis:** T1's tailwind side,
second-order. The company sells the device and Meta supplies the model, so cheaper models improve
the product without EssilorLuxottica paying for them.

**7532.T test:** this is the trap. Buying it for AI glasses would be buying the story that set
the 2025 peak. Only the traditional business makes it a different decision.

### 4568.T — Tokyo — Daiichi Sankyo (new)

- **Why:** it developed the cancer drugs Enhertu, co-commercialised with AstraZeneca, and
  Datroway. The shares are **¥2,867** (16 November, 06:25 UTC), against **¥3,700** a year ago
  (**−22.5%**), a highest weekly close of **¥6,108** on 25 August 2024 (**−53%**), and **¥2,995**
  five years ago (Yahoo chart endpoint, provisional). Its results release of 30 October
  ([company text via BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/daiichi-sankyo-reports-second-quarter-fiscal-year-2026-financial-results))
  gives:
  - revenue **+19.7% to ¥1.167tn**, and operating profit **+34.4% to ¥193.8bn**. Set against a
    full-year guide of ¥2.34tn, these are first-half figures (my reading).
  - Enhertu **¥500.0bn, +51.4%**. Datroway **¥54.4bn, +161.6%**.
  - full-year guidance **unchanged** at ¥2.34tn revenue and ¥320.0bn operating profit.
- **Why it fell, as reported:** the press says the July first-quarter results carried an
  accounting error and manufacturing losses, and that local Chinese ADCs are taking share from
  Enhertu in China ([Fierce Pharma headline](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/daiichi-sankyo-flags-accounting-error-shares-tank-despite-revenue-beat),
  narrative only; the page refused the fetch). The company's own July release confirms the
  error and its direction. After announcing FY2025 results on 11 May, it "identified an error in
  the aggregation process for selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses", and FY2025
  operating profit was revised **up, from ¥229.1bn to ¥258.0bn**. The same release has Q1
  operating profit **−12.0% to ¥85.1bn**, "primarily due to EU Specialty Business restructuring
  expenses", on revenue +21.1%
  ([Q1 release, 31 July](https://www.daiichisankyo.com/files/news/pressrelease/pdf/202607/20260731_E4.pdf),
  decoded locally). A scout reports that the Q1 presentation names local Chinese ADCs as
  intensifying competition. I did not open that document myself.
- **What would have to be true:** Enhertu's growth outside China has to carry the guide. The
  accounting error ran in the flattering direction, since profit went up, but it was about
  **11%** of the year's operating profit (¥28.9bn of ¥258.0bn) and was found only after
  publication. That is a control failure. The next annual report's internal-control statement has
  to show it fixed, and for Leo as a CPA that is the first thing to read.

**Exposure:** adds to **Japan** and to **Healthcare**, and offsets the US and megacap tech. One
overlap: Leo holds AZN.L, which shares in Enhertu, so this is partly the same drug.
**Thesis, against it:** Chinese ADCs undercutting a Western-priced drug is T1's pattern in
pharma, cheap fast-follow eroding the incumbent's margin. If it spreads beyond China through
out-licensing, it is a headwind to this name, not a tailwind.

**7532.T test:** partly. It is a yen reporter at ¥159.4 to the dollar, so a sterling holder's
return also turns on the yen. The price is back at its level of five years ago, so the premium
has gone. The question is whether the reasons for the fall are temporary.
