## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** The Fed, ECB and Bank of Japan all tightened policy within eight days. The ECB raised each key rate by 25 basis points on 10 September, taking the deposit rate to **2.50%**, and projected 2026 inflation of **3.0%** ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp260910~314e508016.en.html)). The Fed then set its target range at **3.75%–4.00%** on 16 September ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)), and the BOJ changed its money-market guideline on 18 September ([BOJ](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/whatsnew/)). Markets have not treated this as recession: the S&P 500 is **14.80%** higher over one year and the Nasdaq **17.19%** higher, even with the US 10-year yield at **4.998%** ([prices.json](../../prices.json)). WTI fell **6.32%** on Friday but remains **52.31%** higher over one year. Observation, not forecast: this is simultaneous policy tightening against an energy shock, with risk assets still near their highs.

**Inference.** That combination raises the price of weak balance sheets and long-duration promises. It does not by itself create a candidate. The more useful new evidence is one layer down from the loud energy and defence trades: Exosens raised 2026 guidance on 17 September after a faster production ramp, and separately said nuclear-instrumentation demand remained strong ([Exosens](https://www.exosens.com/news/exosens-raises-2026-guidance)). Mirion's filing shows the same demand in a different company and geography: its Nuclear & Safety segment supplied **70.9%** of first-half revenue, and total backlog rose to **$1,138.6m** at 30 June from **$1,104.3m** at year-end ([10-Q](https://ir.mirion.com/filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001628280-26-050604/mir-20260630.htm)). What I take from it is narrower than “buy nuclear”: measurement, dosimetry and safety recur across construction, operation and decommissioning, so they may capture the spending without requiring a reactor design to win.
