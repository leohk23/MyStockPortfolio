## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** The loud energy shock has eased at the edge without resolving. Local WTI is **$94.02**, down **11.16%** over seven days but still **50.10%** higher over one year; the US 10-year yield is **4.998%** ([prices.json](../../prices.json)). That combination still makes energy-intensive operations and debt-funded projects harder to underwrite, even though the S&P 500 remains **14.29%** higher over one year.

**Observation.** A quieter physical constraint became measurable last week. The World Meteorological Organization's 17 September report says global terrestrial water storage has declined persistently since 2014–2016, every major glaciated region lost ice for a fourth consecutive year in 2025, and 2025 was one of the driest years for river discharge in 35 years. The WMO explicitly calls for more observation networks, early warnings, resilient infrastructure and better water management ([WMO launch statement](https://public.wmo.int/content/launch-of-global-water-report-2025)). This goes beyond the local macro block, which carries market prices but no hydrology or observation coverage.

**Inference.** “Water scarcity” is too broad to be an investable conclusion. The second-order layer is measurement and reuse: customers have to know where water is, what quality it is and how a process is consuming it before they can ration or recycle it. With money expensive, the better candidate is a business already converting instruments and data into cash, not a long-duration developer whose return depends on a future project pipeline. That led to Vaisala; Xylem, Veolia and Kurita were checked as counterexamples rather than forced onto the list.
