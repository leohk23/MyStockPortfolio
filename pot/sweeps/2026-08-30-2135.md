# Sweep — 30 August 2026

## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** US nominal strength and real growth are separating. Second-quarter real GDP grew
at a 1.5% annual rate, but real final sales to private domestic purchasers grew 4.2%, the PCE price
index rose 5.3%, and profits from current production increased by $400.9 billion
([Bureau of Economic Analysis](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-second-estimate-and-corporate-profits-2nd-quarter-2026)).
The local five- and ten-year yields are 4.481% and 4.72%, up 19.7% and 10.4% over one year, while
the S&P 500 remains only 1.3% below its high ([prices.json](../../prices.json),
[signals.json](../../signals.json)). Chairman Warsh described the Fed's 2% PCE objective as a
fixed target and short-term rates as its main tool
([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/warsh20260828a.htm)).
**Inference.** Corporate earnings are being supported by nominal demand, but the discount rate is
not offering an easy rescue. That favours businesses with observable pricing power or transaction
activity and makes a rate-cut thesis, by itself, too weak.

**Observation.** Europe is growing without a broad investment surge. Eurostat says industrial and
services production expanded and sentiment improved, while retail trade slipped and construction
confidence did not improve
([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/wdn-20260820-1)). UK real
business investment rose 1.7% in the second quarter but only 0.8% from a year earlier
([Office for National Statistics](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproductgdp/bulletins/businessinvestment/apriltojune2026provisionalresults/pdf)).
Locally, the Euro Stoxx 50 and FTSE 100 are already up 20.8% and 17.7% over one year, and 16 of 43
tracked country markets are within 5% of their highs ([prices.json](../../prices.json),
[signals.json](../../signals.json)). **Inference.** Europe is not a simple cheap-market call.
Physical electricity and data infrastructure can still grow faster than the economy, but each
supplier has to prove its order quality and margins rather than borrow the theme.

## 3. Candidates

### CME — CME Group, Nasdaq

**Why:** the first half was the company's strongest on record, second-quarter market-data revenue
rose 20% to $238 million, and July average daily volume reached a record 27 million contracts, up
23% across a broad set of asset classes
([Q2 results](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2026/7/22/cme_group_inc_reportsstrongfinancialresultsforq22026.html),
[July volume](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2026/8/04/cme_group_july_volumehitsnewrecordof27millioncontractsup23yearov.html)).

**What would have to be true:** participation and market-data demand must remain durable when
volatility normalises, new Treasury clearing and product launches must add rather than displace
revenue, and the independently fetched valuation must compensate for a volume-sensitive business.

### PRY.MI — Prysmian, Milan

**Why:** cables are a physical link between grid investment, data centres and electrification;
Prysmian's second-quarter organic revenue grew 9.4%, adjusted EBITDA rose 20.7%, and growth was
spread across Digital Solutions, Power Grid, Transmission, and Industrial & Construction
([H1 results](https://www.prysmian.com/en/media/press-releases/prysmian-raises-guidance-after-its-best-quarter-yet)).

**What would have to be true:** transmission and digital margins must reflect repeatable capacity
and execution rather than unusually favourable project mix, acquisitions must not dilute cash
returns, and the local valuation history must show that the market has not already capitalised the
whole AI and grid buildout.

No order, price target or position size is implied. `CME` and `PRY.MI` were added to the watchlist
so the normal pipeline can provide price, earnings and valuation history.
