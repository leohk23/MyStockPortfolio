## 1. Where the world is

**Observed.** Intel reported last night. Its client segment fell 5% to $8.1bn while data-centre revenue rose 78%. It guided Q4 revenue to $15.0–16.0bn and said "we see the PC slowdown continuing at least through the first half of next year". The shipment forecasts quoted in the press (PCs down 12% this year and 10% in 2027) came from the call and are not in the release, so they are not verified here. The local file has pre-market moves at 12:36 UTC of −12.4% for Dell, −9.4% for AMD, −5.2% for ASML and −4.4% for Qualcomm, all held. **Inference.** The AI build-out and the consumer device cycle are now pulling apart. The memory and data-centre parts are being bought at the expense of the PC and phone parts. That points at four held names on the losing side (Dell, AMD's client business, Xiaomi and Nintendo, all of which buy memory). That is the Review lane's job, not a reason for a new name.

**Observed.** Amazon's Q3 filing shows AWS up 39% to $46.0bn and operating income of $28.1bn. Trailing twelve-month free cash flow was an *outflow* of $42.4bn against an inflow of $14.8bn a year earlier, because purchases of property and equipment rose $94.8bn. The local file has Amazon −7.3% pre-market. ServiceNow, also held, closed down 26.95% yesterday on the local file. Its Q3 8-K shows subscription revenue up 21% and Q4 subscription growth guided to 22.5–23%. But GAAP operating margin was 9.5% against 32.5% non-GAAP, and total debt was $7.524bn. **Inference.** The market is no longer paying for AI demand on its own. It is pricing the cash it costs. ServiceNow's growth is not slowing, so its fall is about margin and about T2, not about demand. Its release does not disclose the units-against-price evidence T2 asks for, so the thesis can be neither confirmed nor dismissed from it. Under T3, the NOW position must weigh both theses, and the Review should do that.

**Observed.** The Bank of Japan held at 1.25% today, 8–1, with one member voting for 1.5%. It said underlying inflation has reached 2% and will speed up its ETF sales from January 2027. Eurostat's flash estimate has euro-area GDP up 0.4% in Q3 against 0.6% in Q2, and up 1.3% on the year. Both are from the press reports of the releases. The Eurostat page was located but not opened. **Inference.** Japan is now the one large economy tightening on the strength of its own inflation, not because of imported energy. Europe is slowing gently under the oil shock. Neither on its own makes a candidate.

## 3. Candidates

### 0700.HK — Hong Kong Stock Exchange — Tencent Holdings — new

- **Why it might belong.** This is the de-rating Leo's own note on 3067.HK describes, in a single name. In Q2 revenue rose 11% to RMB204.8bn, non-IFRS operating profit rose 9%, and marketing services rose 22%. The company bought back HK$16.9bn of its shares in the quarter. The price is **HK$383.60, provisional** (Yahoo, 30 Oct). That is down 36.0% this year and 41.6% below its one-year high of HK$657.00 (12 Nov 2025). Under T1 it is a builder and buyer of models (its own Hy3) in a market where US frontier APIs are not sold, so cheap open weights help it rather than hurt it. It reduces the US weight (64.9%), but it *adds* to Communication Services (16.8%). It diversifies by geography and currency, not by sector.
- **What would have to be true.** The compute bill has to earn its keep. Q2 capex rose 176% to RMB52.8bn and free cash flow was negative RMB13.8bn (RMB37.6bn excluding compute prepayments). That is the same pattern the market punished at Amazon today. Q3 must show marketing and cloud growth that keeps pace with that spending. It is a VIE, so under Leo's own reading of Burry this is a **sizing** decision before it is a research one. Burry also names the 1260H list as a live risk. **The 7532.T test:** a 36% fall is not a thesis. The case holds only if operating profit keeps growing while the price falls, and Q3 results, due mid-November (date not checked at source), are the test.
