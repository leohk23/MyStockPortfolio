## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** The Strait of Hormuz has been shut to normal commercial traffic since March, and
news reporting this week describes Iran attacking tankers again to break the US-assisted shuttle
that had been moving some Gulf exports ([CNN, 11 Oct](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/11/middleeast/iran-hormuz-oil-analysis-intl),
[Al Jazeera, 12 Oct](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/10/12/iran-sets-crosshairs-on-us-uae-oil-shuttle-out-of-strait-of-hormuz)).
That is narrative, not a figure; we hold no primary transit series. In our own data WTI is $96.03,
up 66.89% over a year, while the US 10-year yield is 5.089% ([prices.json](../../prices.json)).
**Inference.** Oil itself has been in every sweep since August. What has not is everything else the
Gulf exports: sulfur, helium and LNG. Those are second-order, less watched, and they hit different
companies than crude does.

**Observation.** Sulfur is where the damage is clearest in an issuer's own accounts. Mosaic, the
largest US phosphate producer, paid $522 per long ton for sulfur in Q2, has settled Q3 contracts at
$705, has idled its Faustina plant and runs Bartow at 40%. Its phosphate segment made a gross
margin of minus $4 a tonne on DAP selling at $773 ([Mosaic Q2 2026, SEC
8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001285785/000128578526000109/pressreleaseq22026-ex991.htm)).
Meanwhile CF Industries, the nitrogen producer already on the watchlist, is up 44.27% over a year
([prices.json](../../prices.json)). **Inference.** The market has paid the obvious Hormuz
beneficiary (nitrogen, oil) and punished the input-squeezed one. If the strait reopens, the squeeze
ends faster than the phosphate shortage it caused, because curtailed plants do not refill
inventories overnight. That asymmetry is the second-order idea in this sweep.

**Beyond our macro block.** Qatar's Ras Laffan, the world's largest LNG export hub, has been offline
since a strike on 19 March, and QatarEnergy has declared force majeure (reported by
[AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/industry/2026/03/qatari-lng-shutdown-puts-11-of-global-helium-supply-at-risk/)
and [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/19/tech-stocks-qatar-attack-semiconductor-supply-chain-lng-helium.html),
press, not a primary source). Woodside's own half-year report shows the effect on a non-Gulf seller
is smaller than one would guess: realised price up 20% to $74.0/boe, but production down 13% and
operating cash flow down 10% ([Woodside H1 2026, SEC
6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000844551/000119312526364293/d344470dex991.htm)).
**Inference.** The LNG windfall is real but diluted by oil-linked contracts and by its own volume
decline, which means the market may have less of it in the price than the headline suggests.

## 3. Candidates

Two candidates that are deliberately opposite sides of the same event, both outside the book's
sector concentration. Each stands on its own, and neither is an order.

### Woodside Energy: `WDS.AX`, ASX (new)

**Why.** It is Australian and Energy, so it offsets both the 66.59% US weight and an energy weight that
is at most 0.73% of the book. Two dated catalysts are inside the quarter: Scarborough, 98% complete,
targets its first LNG cargo in Q4 2026, which reverses the 13% production fall, and the Q3 report is
due on 21 October. Interim dividend 57 US cents, 80% of underlying profit. Figures from the
[H1 2026 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000844551/000119312526364293/d344470dex991.htm).

**What would have to be true.** Profit must stand without a closed Hormuz: underlying NPAT rose only
7% on a 20% higher price, so the case is Scarborough's volume, not the war premium. Gearing is 20.6%
against a 10–20% target, with 2026 capex guided at $4.0–4.5bn and Louisiana LNG only 28% complete
(first LNG 2029), so the dividend must survive that build without more borrowing. **Is this the
7532.T mistake?** It could be, if bought for the windfall. It is not, only if the Deep dive values it
on a normalised price. Price and multiple are not cited here; the pipeline will fetch them.

### Mosaic: `MOS`, NYSE (new)

**Why.** It is the input-cost loser of the same event, the story that was loud in May and has since
gone quiet. Its potash segment is earning well (gross margin $103 a tonne in Q2), while phosphate is
running at a loss on a sulfur price set by a closed strait. It adds US weight but Basic Materials,
and a Brazilian business (Fertilizantes) that is geographic diversification in substance. Figures
from the [Q2 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001285785/000128578526000109/pressreleaseq22026-ex991.htm).

**What would have to be true.** Sulfur must normalise before the balance sheet forces a worse
decision. Q2 free cash flow was −$153m on $320m capex, it refinanced commercial paper with a $1bn
term loan, Q2 carried $351m of notable items including a $162m mark-to-market loss on its Ma'aden
stake, and it is exploring sale of Brazilian assets. The falsifier is a Q3 stripping margin that is
still negative on DAP at $820–840, or curtailments that turn into closures. **Is this the 7532.T
mistake?** It is a rebound bet, which is exactly what that was, so the Deep dive must price it on
what potash alone earns and treat phosphate recovery as optionality, not the base case.

**Considered and not raised.** Helium: non-Qatar producers are either megacap industrial-gas
companies where helium is immaterial to revenue (a US-heavy addition anyway), or small speculative
explorers. Nothing cleared the bar.

**Thesis.** None of T1–T3 drove either name. Both are a hedge against the book's AI concentration
only in the sense of being uncorrelated to it, not because any thesis argues for them.
