## 1. Where the world is

**Observation — what we hold.** Oil is still the hardest-running series in the macro block: WTI
**$99.99**, +7.48% over 7 days, +23.06% over the month, **+74.44% year to date**. US 10-year
**4.975%** (+18.82% ytd), 5-year **4.791%** (+28.14% ytd). Gold $4,390, −12.09% over six months.
S&P 500 7,656.98, −0.22% on the week. Hang Seng 24,805.63, −6.00% over a year
([prices.json](../../prices.json), macro block).

**Observation — what we do not hold, and it is the bigger number.** The macro block carries WTI and
nothing else in energy. Going outside it: Dutch TTF front-month gas is **€81.50/MWh, +152% over a
year** and within a euro of its 52-week high of €82.05, while US Henry Hub is **$2.82/mmbtu, −3.9%
over a year**; Brent is **$104.42, +57.3%** (all Yahoo, **provisional** — see §4, these are macro
series and belong in the macro block, not in the equity watchlist). The mechanism is not the crude
headline. The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of global LNG trade and Qatari flows are
substantially blocked, with Europe rebuilding storage into winter from a level below its historical
norm ([Kpler](https://www.kpler.com/blog/global-lng-and-natural-gas-prices-surge-as-us-and-iran-resume-hot-war),
[Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/business/2026/08/31/european-gas-prices-spike-above-70-amid-renewed-middle-east-fighting)
— press, cited for narrative only; the storage percentage itself I could not source cleanly, see
§4). Marine war-risk premiums for Hormuz transits have gone from roughly **0.25% of hull value
before the war to 7.5–10%** ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/shipping/072226-middle-east-shipping-insurance-costs-rise-on-hormuz-risks-marsh),
[The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/2026/07/17/war-risk-shipping-premium-surges-again-as-tensions-escalate-at-strait-of-hormuz/)).
The FOMC meets **16 September** with market-implied odds of a *hike* around half to two-thirds
([CME FedWatch as reported](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/fomc-september-2026-odds-rate-201618784.html);
the July decision held at 3.50–3.75% with three dissents in favour of raising,
[Fed minutes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20260729.htm)).

**Inference.** The single widest dislocation in the world right now is not oil, it is the **spread
between European and American gas** — a factor of roughly ten on an energy-equivalent basis, from a
series our seventeen do not contain. Two things follow that the oil headline does not give you.
First, this is a **cost shock localised to Europe**, so it argues *against* buying European
energy-intensive manufacturing on weakness, and that cuts against the standing diversify-out-of-the-US
bias rather than with it — worth saying plainly, because the bias is meant to be a preference and not
a reflex. Second, the beneficiary is the producer sitting on $2.82 gas competing against one paying
€81.50, which is a US position; the book is already 66.88% US, so that idea has to win on merits,
and below I only re-look at one we already carry rather than adding another American name.

On the energy void the last sweep flagged: I researched it and the honest answer is that **the
obvious vehicles have already run.** Saipem is **+82.6%** over a year and Subsea7 **+58.3%**
(NOK 321.6 against a 52-week high of 351) — both near highs (§3).
Buying offshore energy services at $100 spot crude, after an 80% move, on the argument that capex
must follow, is the 7532.T shape almost exactly: a real macro story, a stock that has already
discounted it, and no work done on mid-cycle earnings. So the void stays open this week by choice.
