## 1. Where the world is

**Observed.** US real GDP grew at a 3.4% annual rate in Q3 and private domestic final sales grew 4.9%, while PCE inflation was 2.1% and core PCE 2.5%. The ECB held its deposit rate at 2.5% yesterday and described higher energy prices as an upside risk to inflation and a downside risk to growth. The Bank of Canada raised its policy rate 25 basis points to 2.5% on Wednesday because energy costs and their pass-through had lifted inflation risks. The local file has WTI up 67.3% in real terms, US exposure at 64.95%, and Technology plus Communication Services at 57.67%. **Inference.** Demand has not broken, but the oil shock is keeping policy restrictive. That is a poor reason to add another long-duration growth name and a good reason to test cash-generative diversifiers whose earnings do not require easier money.

**Observed.** The IEA's October report forecasts global oil demand down 2.6 million barrels a day in 2026 and says inventories remain 509 million barrels below end-February. It also records diesel exports from major hubs 700,000 barrels a day lower year on year, with combined Middle East and Russian losses of 1.3 million barrels a day. Refining margins have therefore split sharply: record freight and crude costs compressed Europe and Asia, while US margins remained high. Shell reported $10.8bn of adjusted earnings and $21.0bn of operating cash flow in Q3, explicitly in a quarter of record refining margins and severe energy-system disruption. **Inference.** The useful distinction is no longer “oil up or down”. Product availability, location and refining configuration matter. Shell is worth watching as an operator that can capture dislocation, but the same evidence makes peak-cycle extrapolation the central risk.

**Observed.** MTU Aero Engines' nine-month adjusted revenue rose 13% to €7.116bn. Commercial maintenance revenue rose 19%, free cash flow rose 67% to €466m and cash conversion reached 62%. The weaker side is visible too: total adjusted EBIT margin fell to 14.6% from 15.9%, MRO margin eased to 8.0% from 8.3%, and net capital expenditure rose 82% to €280m. **Inference.** Yesterday's deferral now has enough evidence for a re-look. The service and spare-parts engine is growing and producing cash, but it must pass the 7532.T test: the thesis has to rest on durable shop visits, material content and cash returns, not “air travel recovered”.
