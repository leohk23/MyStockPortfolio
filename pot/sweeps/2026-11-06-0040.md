**Observed: Hong Kong went the other way.** The Hang Seng rose 3.67% on the same day. Tencent (watched) gained 7.2% and Xiaomi's Hong Kong line (held) 8.8% (`prices.json`). The likely cause is support for the stock market announced on 2–3 November: a third round of the central bank's swap facility for institutions buying shares, and the special relending programme for buybacks made permanent at a 1% rate ([Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-11-03/china-moves-to-support-stocks-with-buyback-and-liquidity-measures-102500134.html), press; the PBoC notice itself was not read). Oil also bounced 3.4% to $91.10 a barrel, though it is still down 12.5% over the week.

**Observed: the memory shortage is now a demand problem for hardware, not only a cost one.** Intel said on its 29 October call that customers "began signalling a PC slowdown based on the memory prices and availability", and that it expects PC units to fall about 12% in 2026 and about 10% in 2027 (as reported by [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/dell-fell-nearly-11-intel-133026942.html); Intel's transcript was not read). DELL, held, is still up 148% this year but has fallen 45% in a month. The same squeeze reaches Xiaomi (held, results 24 November) and Nintendo's Switch 2 economics.

**Inference.** Two things fell yesterday: the AI capital-spending story, and the hardware companies that pay for its memory. A candidate that rises when that story is believed adds to what has just hurt the book, however cheap it now looks. The useful search is for businesses whose earnings depend on neither. This run found two, both in Europe and both of the "loud, then sold, then quiet" kind: a defence group whose growth has hit a procurement bottleneck, and a wind developer that has rebuilt its balance sheet since its 2025 crisis.

## 3. Candidates

### RHM.DE — Xetra — Rheinmetall

- **Why.** On 5 November Rheinmetall cut its 2026 outlook for the second time in three months, and the share fell nearly 17% on the day (press: [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/world-indices/articles/ai-fears-weigh-european-markets-190333951.html), narrative only). Its [Q3 statement](https://www.rheinmetall.com/en/media/news-watch/news/2026/11/2026-11-05-rheinmetall-news-quarterly-statement-q3) blames timing, not demand. The German army's Arminius project "cannot be commissioned until next year", and artillery ammunition lot acceptance tests have slipped. Nine-month sales were €8.4bn, up 38%. The operating result was €1,339m, up 69%, at a 16.0% margin, and EPS from continuing operations was €14.44 against €7.83. The backlog is €78.0bn, about six years of the new €13.2bn sales guide (previously €13.7–14.2bn; the margin guide is now about 18.5%, from about 19%). The share is €841.50, provisional from Yahoo, down from a weekly close of €1,962.50 in September 2025 and €1,727 a year ago. It adds Industrials (7.35% of the book) and Europe (8.23%), and nothing in it depends on data centres.
- **What would have to be true.** The delay has to be a contracting delay and not a sign that Germany will buy less, or later, than the backlog implies. Cash is the open question: nine-month operating free cash flow was **−€1,698m**. The statement does not say how much of that is working capital that reverses in the fourth quarter and how much is a lasting change. **The 7532.T test:** this is exactly the shape of that mistake: a narrative stock (European rearmament) bought because a big story is true. A 57% fall does not make it cheap. The repo has no P/E record for it yet, and adding it is what gives one. A Deep dive should only look at it if that record and the fourth-quarter cash flow both support it.

### ORSTED.CO — Nasdaq Copenhagen — Ørsted

- **Why.** This was the loudest European stock story of 2025: US stop-work orders, a rights issue, a collapse. Since then it has gone quiet while the numbers improved. The [nine-month report](https://orsted.com/en/company-announcement-list/2026/11/orsted-delivers-strong-earnings-with-19--ebitda-gr-152708491) of 5 November shows EBITDA excluding new partnerships and cancellation fees up 19% to DKK 20.2bn, and Revolution Wind in commercial operation since October. Gross investment fell to DKK 28.5bn from DKK 39.9bn, and the 2026 investment guide was cut by DKK 5bn to DKK 45–50bn. Net interest-bearing debt is DKK 37.7bn with FFO to adjusted net debt of 37.3%. The full-year EBITDA guide of above DKK 28bn was kept. The share is DKK 147, provisional from Yahoo, against DKK 124 a year ago and a weekly close of DKK 393.60 in November 2024. It adds Utilities (1.18% of the book) and Europe, and its earnings are contracted power, not the AI cycle.
- **What would have to be true.** Interest rates are the risk this report actually shows. Profit fell to DKK 2.6bn from DKK 6.5bn, mostly because of DKK 6.0bn of impairments "linked to interest rate increases on our US projects", and the US ten-year yield is 4.84%, against about 4.09% a year ago (`prices.json`). Sunrise Wind has to finish (all foundations are in), US policy must not take another project, and there must be no more dilution. Note also that the EBITDA growth was helped by "higher wind speeds", which do not recur on demand. **The 7532.T test:** a recovery bought on a rebound is close to that mistake. The difference has to be in the balance sheet and the cash, not the headline growth, and the Deep dive would have to show it.
