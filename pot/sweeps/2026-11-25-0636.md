### CWK.L, London Stock Exchange: Cranswick, new

- **Why:** a British pork, poultry, convenience-food and pet-food producer. Volumes grew in every category while its input prices fell, so the business is not a price-only recovery. It adds to **Consumer Defensive** (3.57%), the second-smallest named sector in the book.
- **What would have to be true:** about £376m of capacity spending already committed (Hull, Eye and poultry) must earn its cost of capital once pig prices turn. Adjusted profit must not keep pulling away from statutory profit.

The [24 November interim results](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/cranswick--cwk/interim-results-/9889890) cover the 26 weeks to 26 September. Investegate's page gave me the summary, not the full RNS:

**Price, provisional:** Yahoo closed it at **5,380p** on 24 November. Its two-year range of weekly closes runs from **4,720p** (January 2025) to **5,690p** (June 2026). It sits in the upper part of that range, so it is not cheap on price alone. The P/E history from our fetch will settle that.

### GRG.L, London Stock Exchange: Greggs, new

- **Why:** a de-rating that has largely reversed, so this asks whether the operating recovery has caught up with the share price. Expansion is self-funded and the brand trades on value at a time when UK households are being squeezed. Yahoo is expected to file it under Consumer Cyclical, already 14.16% of the book; I have not confirmed the classification. Its diversification is by currency and earnings driver, not by sector.
- **What would have to be true:** like-for-like growth must stay above cost inflation through 2027. The company itself flags higher costs that year. Manufacturing consolidation must deliver the promised savings in 2028–29.

The [Q3 trading update](https://s204.q4cdn.com/833746791/files/doc_news/Q3-Trading-Update-2026.pdf) of 30 September, the RNS itself:

| Measure | Q3 (13 weeks) | Year to date (39 weeks) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total sales | +7.7% | +7.4% |
| Company-managed like-for-like sales | +3.4% | +2.6% |

- **Shops:** 2,796 at 26 September (2,157 company-managed, 639 franchised), with 100–110 net openings expected in 2026.
- **Cost inflation:** about **2%** for 2026, with "signs of greater inflationary pressures in 2027". The new Derby and Kettering distribution centres "will increase costs in 2027".
- **Restructuring:** four sites may close, with about £60m of cash costs (about £40m of it capex) for about £20m a year of savings, realised across 2028 and 2029.
- **Outlook:** "a modestly improved outcome for 2026".

The release does not split like-for-like growth into transactions and price. Year-to-date like-for-like of 2.6% against 2% cost inflation leaves little real volume. §2.4b does not formally bite, but a Deep dive must find the transaction count.

**7532.T test:** this is the closer call of the two. Yahoo's weekly closes run from a low of **1,450p** (week of 17 November 2025) to **2,324p** on 24 November, up about 60%. The week of the Q3 update closed at **2,026p**, so about 15% of the rise came after it. Buying because of the rebound would be the mistake Leo named. The case has to rest on 2027 margins after the cost step, which the company has already warned of. All prices here are Yahoo, provisional.
