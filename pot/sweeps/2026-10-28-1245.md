## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** Boeing reported on 27 October. Its release says the 737 programme "continues to
progress toward stabilized production of 47 per month", the 787 stays at 8 a month, and the
commercial backlog is over 6,300 airplanes worth $612bn. It delivered 161 commercial airplanes in the
quarter ([Boeing release](https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2026-10-27-Boeing-Reports-Third-Quarter-Results)).
The press reports that management now expects to reach 47 a month in early 2027 rather than the
summer of 2026, and that it will miss its target of about 500 737 deliveries this year
([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/-boeing-ba-q3-2026-earnings.html)). That timing is the press's
account of the call, not a figure in the release. Boeing's shares closed down 10.7% at $168.18,
provisional from Yahoo.

**Observation.** Four days earlier, on 23 October, Safran reported third-quarter revenue of €9,003m,
up 13.4% organically. Civil engine spare parts grew 17.0% in dollars in the quarter against 23.9% for
the nine months, so the first half ran faster. Services grew 19.0% in the quarter and 32.5% for the
nine months. It kept its full-year outlook: revenue up mid-teens, recurring operating income of
€6.4bn to €6.5bn and free cash flow of €4.7bn to €4.9bn, with 535 LEAP engines delivered in the
quarter ([release text, as republished](https://www.finanzwire.com/press-release/safran-epa-saf-safran-reports-third-quarter-2026-revenue-oZzVpEE6rvr);
Safran's own site refuses scripts). The share closed at €302.00 on 22 October and €271.80 on the 23rd,
a 10.0% fall in one session. It was €272.60 at 12:32 UTC today, 18.6% below the €335.10 of
29 September and 25.6% below its 52-week high of €366.50. All three prices are provisional from
Yahoo.

**Inference.** The market has read these two events separately: Safran fell on the aftermarket
slowing, and did not move on Boeing's delay (€274.40 on the 26th, €274.60 on the 27th). Read
together, they push in opposite directions. Spare parts slowing is the fact. A 737 ramp that slips by
about half a year, against a 6,300-plane backlog, means the older jets these new ones were meant to
replace keep flying longer. The older engines on those jets are where Safran's spare-parts income
comes from, and the release itself credits CFM56 pricing for part of the growth. So the market has priced the first effect and not the second. That second effect is slow and
nobody writes about it, which is the kind of story this lane exists to find. It is still an
inference, not a reported fact. Whether it holds will show up in the spare-parts growth rate over
the next two quarters, not in anything said today.

**Observation, on what Leo already owns.** Kawasaki Heavy (KWHIY) builds the 787's forward fuselage
([Kawasaki](https://global.kawasaki.com/en/corp/newsroom/news/detail/?f=20191216_8615)). Boeing left
the 787 at 8 a month, so yesterday changes nothing for that part of the business. KWHIY is down 13.1%
over a year and reports on 10 November ([prices.json](../prices.json)).

The oil and rates background is unchanged since this morning: WTI $101.76, up 77.5% this year, and

## 3. Candidates

### SAF.PA — Euronext Paris — Safran — new

- **Why:** A high-quality aero-engine franchise fell 10% in one day on a slowdown in spare-parts
  growth, still 17% in dollars, with guidance kept. The market did not re-price it when Boeing,
  four days later, pushed back the new jets that would retire the older engines Safran earns its
  spare-parts income on. It adds to Industrials (7.0% of the book) and Europe (7.8%), and offsets
  the 58% in megacap tech. It touches no thesis in `pot/theses.md`.
- **What would have to be true:** the third-quarter slowdown is a normal return from an exceptional
  first half, not the start of a fall, so civil spare-parts growth in dollars holds in double digits
  through the full-year results in February 2027. And the share must be cheap by its own history,
  which the repo cannot yet say because it does not carry Safran. Adding it is how it will. Until
  that runs, every Safran price above is provisional.
- **Is this the 7532.T mistake again?** It could be, and the test is the same one he named. 7532.T
  was a travel recovery bought on the story. Safran's aftermarket is also travel-driven, and $100 oil
  is a direct threat to airline traffic and finances. The difference has to be shown in the
  accounts: a spare-parts line that stays in double digits while traffic is under pressure. If it
  does not, the reason to own it is gone.
