# Sweep, 27 November 2026, 11:25 UTC

Two new candidates, both copper miners: **Freeport-McMoRan (FCX)** and **Antofagasta (ANTO.L)**.
Each is about 39% below its 52-week high, while copper is about 15% below its September peak.
They add a sector the book holds almost none of. The warning on both is that a miner looks cheapest

## 1. Where the world is

**Observation, China, released today.** The [NBS January–October industrial-profit release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202611/t20261127_1965949.html)
puts profit at large industrial firms **up 16.0%**, with October alone **up 20.1%**, on revenue up
**6.7%**. The operating margin is **5.69%**, up 0.45 points. The spread across industries is very
wide:

|---|---:|

| Nonferrous smelting and processing | +71.7% |
| Coal mining | +61.4% |

Receivables grew **10.5%**, faster than revenue, and the average collection period lengthened by
1.4 days to **72.2**. Finished-goods inventory rose **12.0%**. The [October activity release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease//202611/t20261116_1965843.html)
gives the setting: retail sales **+0.4%**, fixed-asset investment **−8.1%** for the ten months, and
property investment **−21.6%**. PPI is **+5.7%**, and industrial purchasing prices are **+7.9%**.

**Inference.** China's profit growth is coming from upstream industries and from AI hardware. Its
downstream manufacturers are paying input prices that rise faster than their own selling prices,
and autos are the clearest loser. **This matters to BYD (1211.HK), Leo's largest non-US holding at
4.04% of the book** (`signals.json`). It already carries a §6.1 signal: P/E **15.2×** at the 0th
percentile of five years, and P/OCF at the **80th** percentile, so it is flagged `denominatorDrift`
(`signals.json`, 08:25 UTC). The industry figure is a reason to read that flag as a warning and
not as noise. An industry losing a quarter of its profit while BYD's P/E sits at its floor means
the earnings in the denominator are probably still falling. The cash multiple already says so.
This is context for the Deep dive, not a new name. The auto-profit split was last raised on
[30 August](../data-wishlist.md), and it has worsened since.

**Observation, copper.** The local macro block holds no metals except gold. On Yahoo, COMEX copper
(`HG=F`, provisional) is **$5.68/lb**, against a weekly peak of **$6.70** in the week of 21
September, which is **−15%**. Freeport realised **$6.54/lb** in Q3 ([10-Q exhibit, 27 October](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/831259/000083125926000045/a3q2026exhibit991.htm)).
The miners have fallen much further than the metal, by about 39% from their 52-week highs (section
3). Trade press ties the November slide to the AI sell-off and to Chinese weakness ([SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/HPSJFB5W7OIYMM4YPU4ZEOJU7MTVWGRUBNTCQ2A-lme-copper-hits-four-month-low-as-ai-selloff-and-china-weakness-weigh)),
which is narrative only. Hormuz remains heavily restricted ([Al Jazeera, 24 November](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/24/hormuz-oil-traffic-is-down-40-percent-are-irans-tanker-attacks-working)),
and that reaches miners through their consumables. Antofagasta names **diesel and sulphuric acid**
as the reason its nine-month cash costs rose 20%. European gas (`TTF=F`, provisional) is
**€85.4/MWh**, against €28.8 a year ago.

**Inference.** The market is treating copper miners as a leveraged AI trade. Some of that is fair:
Antofagasta's own outlook cites "digitalisation". But copper's demand is mostly grids, construction
and China, and supply is constrained by outages that have dated recovery schedules. A 15% fall in
the metal and a 39% fall in the equities is the gap worth looking into. It may simply be operating
leverage plus cost inflation, and the candidates below are framed to test exactly that.

`prices.json` (11:07 UTC) for context: S&P 500 +3.94% over seven days and −7.25% over the month;
Nasdaq −11.16% over the month; VIX 24.6; WTI $85.38, +45.8% over the year; gold $3,707, +4.84% on
the day but −17.3% over three months.

**The §6.1 trap, before anything else.** A miner's P/E is lowest when the metal price is at its
peak and earnings are at their peak. Both companies' trailing earnings are struck on copper well
above today's price: Freeport realised $6.54/lb in Q3, against $5.68 now. A 0th-percentile P/E on
either name would be the 7532.T mistake in mining form, a rebound read as cheapness. Any Deep dive
must value them on mid-cycle copper or on P/OCF across a full cycle, and say which.

### FCX, NYSE: Freeport-McMoRan, new

- **Why:** the Grasberg recovery is a company-specific earnings driver with dated milestones, and it
  does not depend on the copper price. The block cave mined about 70,000 tonnes a day in Q3, up from
  53,000 in Q2. PTFI expects **80% of capacity by mid-2027** and to **approach full capacity by
  year-end 2027**, and Production Block 1S is targeted to restart by mid-2027 ([Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/831259/000083125926000045/a3q2026exhibit991.htm)).
- **What would have to be true:** those dates hold, and unit net cash costs come back from Q3's
  **$2.07/lb** (against $1.40 a year earlier) as volume returns, so that the recovery adds earnings
  even at today's copper price.

| Measure, from Freeport's filings | Figure |
|---|---|
| Q3 2026 revenue | $7,546m (Q3 2025: $6,972m) |
| Q3 net income to common, diluted EPS | $1,174m, $0.81 (Q3 2025: $674m, $0.46) |
| Q3 copper production, sales | 832m lb, 749m lb (Q3 2025: 912m, 977m) |
| 9M operating cash flow, capex | $5,524m, $3,236m |
| Net debt, 30 September | $5,063m, or $1,826m excluding PTFI downstream processing debt |
| Sensitivity | about $75m of 2026 operating cash flow per $0.10/lb of Q4 copper |

At its own sensitivity, Q4 copper at $5.68 rather than Q3's $6.54 is roughly $0.65bn less
operating cash flow in the quarter. That is my arithmetic on Freeport's stated rule of thumb, not a
company figure. Two further facts reduce what a shareholder owns. FCX's share of PTFI falls from
**48.76% to about 37% from 2042** under the February MOU, and the extension still needs an amended
IUPK from Indonesia. In November PTFI refinanced $750m of **4.763%** notes due 2027 with $750m of
**6.90%** notes due 2033 ([8-K, 24 November](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/831259/000083125926000055/fcx-20261124.htm)).

**Price, provisional** (Yahoo chart API, 25 November close): **$48.55**, against a 52-week high of
**$80.24**, which is −39.5%. US-listed, with its largest asset in Indonesia.

### ANTO.L, London Stock Exchange: Antofagasta, new

- **Why:** almost pure Chilean copper, quoted in sterling. It is also the cleaner test of the
  "operating leverage plus cost inflation" explanation, because its 2027 growth comes from projects
  rather than from repairing an outage. The CEO says the Centinela and Los Pelambres projects are in
  their final phases, with construction and commissioning completing in 2027 ([Q3 production report, 15 October](https://www.antofagasta.co.uk/media/4976/20261015_aplc26q3prodreport-oct26-vf.pdf)).
- **What would have to be true:** Centinela's Second Concentrator commissions on time in 2027, and
  the cost inflation the company blames on diesel and sulphuric acid eases (I take both to be
  Hormuz-exposed; the company does not say so)
  rather than becomes permanent.

| Q3 copper production | 151.9kt, +7.0% on Q2 |

| 2026 cash-cost guidance before by-products | raised to $2.60–2.70/lb, from $2.40–2.60, on diesel |
| Net cash cost guidance | $1.15–1.35/lb, unchanged |

The 2027 guidance midpoint (660kt) is barely above 2026's (640kt), so the "growth" is not yet
visible in volumes. A union representing about 20% of the company's own workforce had declared a
strike, with negotiations continuing. The site name did not survive the PDF text extraction, so I
have not stated it. By-product credits fell 15% on the quarter as gold weakened, and gold is −17.3%
over three months locally.

**Price, provisional** (Yahoo chart API, 27 November): **2,711.6p**, against a 52-week high of
**4,475p**, which is −39.4%.
