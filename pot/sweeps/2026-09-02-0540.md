# Sweep — 2 September 2026

## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** The oil-and-rates pressure is still the dominant market fact: WTI is $90.51, up
8.4% in seven days, and the US ten-year yield is 4.796% ([local market
data](../../prices.json)). Euro-area inflation rose to 3.3% in August from 2.9% in July, but the
increase was concentrated in energy, which accelerated to 14.3% from 10.3%; inflation excluding
energy remained 2.2% ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-euro-indicators/w/2-01092026-ap)).
**Inference.** This is an input-cost shock more than a fresh broad demand boom. The useful hunting
ground is not the already-loud oil producer, but a business whose equipment cuts customers' energy
use and earns a return even after the shock fades.

**Observation beyond the local macro block.** Australia grew 0.4% in the June quarter, but nearly
half of its 1.4% rise in discretionary household spending came from vehicle purchases; electric
and hybrid sales reached a record as households sought lower running costs. The rest of
discretionary spending was subdued, and households used less fuel amid elevated prices
([Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/national-accounts/australian-national-accounts-national-income-expenditure-and-product/jun-2026)).
At the same time, the BIS warns that an AI-capex pullback could hit indebted suppliers and that
opaque circular financing links chip makers, hyperscalers and AI labs
([BIS Annual Economic Report](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2026e.pdf)). **Inference.** The
consumer's move toward proven fuel-saving technology is more investable today than adding another
data-centre supplier whose recent growth may depend on the financing cycle continuing unchanged.

## 3. Candidates

### 7203.T — Toyota Motor, Tokyo Stock Exchange

**Why:** Australia's record electric and hybrid purchases are a real consumer response to fuel
costs, and Toyota already has scaled supply: first-quarter electrified sales rose 12.4%, including
6.7% growth in hybrids, taking the electrified mix to 55.3% even as total Toyota and Lexus retail
sales fell 4.1% ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/national-accounts/australian-national-accounts-national-income-expenditure-and-product/jun-2026),
[Toyota FY2027 Q1](https://global.toyota/pages/global_toyota/ir/financial-results/2027_1q_presentation_en.pdf)).

**What would have to be true:** hybrid scale must turn the shift in mix into durable automotive
returns rather than merely defend volume; the fall in operating margin to 7.9% from 9.5% must not
be structural; yen translation, financial income and the Middle East disruption must be separated
from the underlying business; and independently fetched valuation and capital returns must pass
Leo's filter.

### SPX.L — Spirax Group, London Stock Exchange

**Why:** Spirax is a second-order energy-cost candidate rather than a commodity bet: its steam,
electric thermal and fluid-control systems help industrial customers use heat and process inputs
more efficiently. First-half organic revenue grew 5%, organic adjusted operating profit grew 6%
and adjusted operating margin reached 19.8%, while the company put global industrial-production
growth at only 1.5% ([Spirax half-year RNS](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/2026-half-year-results-c3sc0kct7q101et.html)).

**What would have to be true:** customer savings must sustain above-market organic growth after
energy prices normalise; high margins must reflect durable returns on capital and asset turnover,
not only price or restructuring; first-half cash conversion must recover from 54% and 1.6x
leverage must keep falling; and the independently fetched valuation must clear the strategy's
filter.

Both names were added to the watchlist. This is candidate discovery only; no order, target price
or position size is implied.
