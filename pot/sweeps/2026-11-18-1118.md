### BNR.DE, Xetra: Brenntag (new)

- **Why:** this is a second-order bet on Europe's chemical closures. A chemical made in Rotterdam and one made in Texas both need a distributor to reach a European customer, so a distributor may hold up while European producers shut. The Q3 release ([Brenntag, 11 November](https://corporate.brenntag.com/en/media/news/brenntag-reports-q3-financial-results.html)) has sales of **€4.3bn, +12.7% at constant currency**, operating gross profit **€1,064m (+10.4%)** and operating EBITDA **€385m (+14.5%)**. By segment, Essentials made **€276m (+12.4%)** and Specialties **€110m (+2.5%)**. EPS was **€0.89** against €0.78. The company confirms its 2026 operating EBITDA outlook of **€1,350–1,450m**, "towards the upper end".
- **What would have to be true:** volumes have to grow as European capacity closes. **The filing does not show that yet.** It says "volumes remained broadly stable", Specialties volumes "somewhat pressured" and APAC "pressured". So this quarter's growth is price, plus the Airedale acquisition, which the release does not quantify. The test is the FY2026 results: EMEA Essentials volume, and whether gross profit holds as chemical prices fall with gas. The press does report closures (INEOS idling its Hull acetyls units in September, for example, [The Manufacturer](https://www.themanufacturer.com/articles/ineos-suspends-operations-at-hull-chemical-plants-as-uk-gas-prices-soar/)). But the link from those closures to Brenntag's volumes is **my inference, not the company's**: the release has no sentence on it.

**Evidence against, and the 7532.T test:** free cash flow fell to **€183m from €315m**, and net financial liabilities rose to **€2,944m from €2,528m** at the end of 2025 (same release). Growth that consumes cash while volumes are flat is what a price spike looks like. The 7532.T mistake here would be reading an energy-driven price rise as a structural gain. The shares are **€58.26, +19.4% over a year**, below a 52-week high of **€66.86** (Yahoo, **provisional**). It offsets on geography (Germany) and on sector: chemical distribution, not tech. Its APAC exposure is pressured, as section 1 would predict.

### SIKA.SW, SIX Swiss Exchange: Sika (new)

- **Why:** a construction-chemicals compounder at about half its peak, with the cause in its own filing and now shrinking as a share of sales. The nine-month release ([Sika, 23 October](https://www.sika.com/en/media/media-releases/2026/nine-month-results-2026.html)) has sales of **CHF 8,711.8m**, **+4.8% in local currencies** (+3.5% organic) and **+6.1% in Q3**. The release says "the Chinese construction industry posted a double-digit decline". Excluding that business, growth was **+6.2%**, and Asia/Pacific organic growth was **+7.7%**. By region, EMEA grew **+8.3%** and the Americas **+4.1%**, with **+6.7%** in Q3. The **2026 guidance is reiterated**: 3–6% local-currency growth and an EBITDA margin of **19.0–19.5%**.
- **What would have to be true:** China construction has to keep shrinking as a drag rather than spreading to the rest of Asia/Pacific. EMEA's 8.3% also has to be more than one good year. The test is the FY2026 results in February: Asia/Pacific excluding China, and a margin inside 19.0–19.5%. After that, the Fast Forward benefit of **CHF 150–200m from 2028**, which is the release's only quantified forward figure.

**Evidence against, and the 7532.T test:** profit is not keeping up with sales. EBITDA was **CHF 1.68bn, +1.9%**, the margin was **19.2%, flat**, and currency took **3.2 points** off reported growth (same release). A strong franc is a standing headwind for a Swiss reporter with global sales. The shares are **CHF 190**, against a five-year monthly-close high of **CHF 380.20** and a 52-week low of **CHF 120.35** (Yahoo, **provisional**). So the stock has already risen about 58% from its low. The 7532.T risk is buying a rebound story after the rebound. It is not cheap just because it is below its 2021 peak, and that peak was set at a zero-rate multiple. The valuation filter has to decide on its own five-year history. It offsets on sector (materials) and geography (Switzerland), and it carries China construction risk that the book already holds through CK Asset and BYD's home market.

**Considered, not carried:** Kingspan. Its 9 November update ties 2026 profit growth to data-centre sales ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/11/09/kingspan-forecasts-spike-in-2026-trading-profit-as-data-centre-sales-surge/), press, not checked against the statement). That is the AI capex the book already holds, which is the same reason HLMA.L was rejected. Kingspan is not a rejected company, though, so this is a judgement, not a rule.
