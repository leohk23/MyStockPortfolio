### PRU.L — London (also 2378.HK, PUK) — Prudential plc (new)

- **Why:** **972p** today, against **1,451p** five years ago (**−33%**), **1,047p** a year ago
  (−7.2%) and a 12-month low of **880.8p** on 18 October (Yahoo chart endpoint, provisional). By
  its own Q3 update of 5 November
  ([company release](https://www.prudentialplc.com/en/newsroom/company-news/2026/prudential-plc-q3-business-performance-update/)):
  - Q3 new-business profit (NBP) of **$709m, +1%**, on APE sales of **$1,596m, −6%** at
    constant exchange rates. The NBP margin was **44%**, up from 41%.
  - Nine months: NBP **$2,093m, +6%** at constant rates (+7% actual), with APE flat at $5,026m
    and the margin up 3 points to 42%.
  - **Excluding the Chinese mainland life business**, NBP grew **13% in Q3 and 11% year to
    date**.
  - Hong Kong NBP grew by double digits on domestic bancassurance, "tempered by a slower
    conversion of the sales pipeline from some Chinese Mainland visitor customers".
  - The 2026 NBP growth guidance "is now more stretching than anticipated at the time of the
    Half-Year 2026 Results". Buybacks were **$1.0bn** in nine months, rising to **$1.5bn** by
    the year end.
  - The press reports a mainland NBP decline of 10–15% this year, caused by bancassurance
    expense caps ([HL](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/share-research/202611/prudential-q3-results-china-weighs-on-growth),
    narrative only; I did not find this in the release's own text).
- **What would have to be true:** the mainland drag has to be a regulatory one-off and not the
  first of several, and the 11–13% growth outside China has to hold without the mainland visitor
  flow. The dated test is the FY2026 results, which it says will show "double-digit growth
  across our four Key Financial Metrics". The release lowered the odds on one of them.

**Exposure:** it adds to Financial Services and to Hong Kong and South-East Asia, and offsets
the US and megacap tech. I have filed it under `Old Hongkong` in `watchlist.json`, because Hong
Kong is the market the release reports on first. Its home is London, so re-file it if Leo
disagrees. It reports in dollars, and the Hong Kong dollar is pegged. **Thesis:** none of the
three. **Reading:** it is a UK-incorporated plc, not a VIE. Burry's 26 February piece expects

**7532.T test: yes, partly, and the release says so.** Part of the Hong Kong book is sold to

### WKL.AS — Euronext Amsterdam — Wolters Kluwer (new; RELX is a related watched name)

- **Why:** **€90.68** today, against **€178.70** at its February 2025 peak (**−49%**), **€92.76**
  a year ago and **€57.00** at its June low (Yahoo chart endpoint, provisional). By its own
  nine-month update of 4 November
  ([company release, Ritzau distribution](https://via.ritzau.dk/pressemeddelelse/15269032/wolters-kluwer-nv?publisherId=90446)):
  - revenue **+6% organic**, +4% at constant currency and +2% reported. Recurring revenue,
    **85%** of the total, grew **7%** organically. Adjusted operating profit **+10%** at constant
    currency.
  - organic growth by division: Health 5%, Tax & Accounting 6%, Financial & Corporate Compliance
    4%, Legal & Regulatory 5%, Corporate Performance & ESG 7%.
  - 2026 guidance **reaffirmed**: an adjusted operating margin of about **28.0%**, adjusted free
    cash flow of **€1,300–1,350m**, ROIC of **18–19%**, and high-single-digit growth in diluted
    adjusted EPS. Net debt is €4,070m, **2.0x** EBITDA. €264m of an up-to-€500m buyback had been
    spent by 3 November.
  - its AI figures: **over 95%** adoption of UpToDate Expert AI among US enterprise customers,
    **over 400** firms subscribed to the CCH Axcess agentic modules, and **more than 17,000** user
    licences sold for Libra AI Workspace in Europe.
- **What would have to be true:** recurring organic growth has to stay around 7% while its AI
  products are adopted. The last of those figures is the sharp edge for T2: Libra is sold **per
  user licence**, so the company is selling AI by the seat. T2 says buyers will need fewer
  seats. The FY2026 results will show whether recurring growth holds and whether price or volume
  is driving it.

**Exposure:** it adds to Europe and offsets the US. Its sector label comes with the pipeline's
fetch, so I do not assert one. It does **not** diversify away from T2: Leo already holds EXPN.L
and NOW and watches REL.L, INTU and GWRE, all of them inside T2. Its claim over those is that it
has fallen furthest and still publishes the adoption numbers T2's "where to look" asks for.
**Thesis:** T2, as the thing it tests, not a tailwind. **ROIC** is guided at 18–19%, which is
the capital-return measure the Djukanovic reading values above margin.

**Disclosure:** the press ties this name's June fall to a product from Anthropic, and this lane
runs on an Anthropic model. I have given only the company's own figures and the dated prices,
and Leo should weigh my judgement here with that in mind.

**7532.T test:** no visitor or currency story. The trap here is the opposite one: buying a
de-rating as if it were sentiment, when it might be the business being permanently repriced.
