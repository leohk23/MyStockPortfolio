## 3. Candidates

### WLY — Wiley — New York Stock Exchange

**Why it deserves work:** First-quarter Research Publishing revenue rose 12% and Research EBITDA
rose 9%, with margin up 130 basis points to 29.6%; the same release also shows only $14m of AI
licensing versus a $29m prior-year comparison and a consolidated GAAP loss. This is a testable
proprietary-content toll-road thesis, not an “AI” label pasted onto a publisher. Source:
[Wiley's SEC-filed first-quarter release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/107140/000162828026060252/ex991earningsrelease.htm).

**What would have to be true:** Publishing growth must survive excluding Emerald, AI licensing must
become recurrent without cannibalising subscriptions, Emerald must deliver its promised synergies,
and free cash flow must reduce the acquisition-raised 2.7× net-debt/EBITDA. The fetched valuation
must pass before a Deep dive; none was estimated here.

### 8411.T — Mizuho Financial Group — Tokyo Stock Exchange

**Why it deserves work:** First-quarter consolidated gross profit plus ETF-related gains rose 39.1%
to ¥1.0701tn and net business profit plus those gains rose 81.9% to ¥575.8bn. Management attributes
the move to non-interest income, equity sales and trading, banking revenue and higher yen rates, so
the quarter exposes several separable drivers rather than one macro slogan. Source:
[Mizuho's first-quarter performance page](https://www.mizuho-fg.co.jp/investors/financial/performance_trend.html).

**What would have to be true:** Loan and asset yields must reprice faster than deposits, the gain
must persist without relying on trading, credit costs and bond losses must stay contained, and the
independently fetched valuation must still leave room after Japan's bank rally.

These are candidates, not orders. Toro, Brady, Snowflake, dormakaba, Japan Post Insurance and Sompo
were checked and not advanced: their current cases were respectively acquisition/working-capital
dependent, unchanged from the earlier rejection, loud and valuation-sensitive, transformation-led,
offset by shrinking policies in force, or too exposed to insurance and securities marks for the
clean rate-normalisation test sought here.
