### NHY.OL — Oslo Børs — Norsk Hydro — re-look (watched since 18 September)

- **Why.** The share has fallen 17.8% in a month, 27.1% in three and 38.7% in six (`prices.json`), while the business improved. Hydro's [Q3 release](https://www.hydro.com/en/global/media/news/2026/hydros-third-quarter-2026-solid-results-and-strong-performance/) has adjusted EBITDA of NOK 9,089m against NOK 5,996m a year earlier and NOK 8,923m in Q2. Adjusted EPS was NOK 2.3 against NOK 1.0, and Aluminium Metal EBITDA NOK 7,031m against NOK 2,732m. The damage sits in Bauxite & Alumina: NOK 283m against NOK 1,290m, after a gas supply disruption at the Alunorte refinery in Brazil. Hydro says it secured LNG instead and restored alumina production. Locally it trades at 9.8x trailing EPS of NOK 6.66, at the 33rd percentile of its five-year range, with ROIC of 17.0% in FY2025 and a 4.6% yield (`prices.json`). Basic Materials is too small to appear in the book's sector list (`signals.json` `book`), so it offsets the tech concentration and adds no US exposure.
- **What would have to be true.** Alunorte's gas has to come back on contract rather than at spot-LNG cost. The release gives no date. The press reports a further Q4 cost, which was not checked at source and so is not quoted here. The aluminium price behind the metal segment's tripled EBITDA has to hold, which makes this a cyclical bought below mid-cycle multiples, not at a trough: the 33rd percentile is not the cheapest decile (§6.1). **Discrepancies to resolve:** the press reported adjusted net debt *rising* to NOK 21.9bn from NOK 16.3bn. The release has net debt falling to NOK 15.4bn from NOK 16.3bn, and adjusted net debt falling to NOK 21.9bn from NOK 22.8bn; the press mixed the two measures. `earnings.json` has Q3 net income at NOK 5,378m against the release's NOK 5,263m. The difference is probably minority interests, and it needs reading in the full report. **The 7532.T test:** the risk is mistaking a commodity price for the business. The test is Bauxite & Alumina EBITDA recovering in Q4 and Q1 at the aluminium price of the day, not the share price recovering.

### 6301.T — Tokyo Stock Exchange — Komatsu — new

- **Why.** Komatsu's [half-year release](https://www.komatsu.jp/en/newsroom/2026/20261029_1) (US GAAP, six months to 30 September 2026) has net sales of ¥2,158.6bn, up 14.1%. Operating income was ¥286.6bn, up 3.5%, with the operating margin down 1.3 points to 13.3%. Net income attributable to Komatsu was ¥191.9bn, up 9.2%. The company raised its full-year projections from those of 29 July. The share fell after the release, and has since been caught in the Nikkei's AI sell-off: ¥6,207 against a September month-end close of ¥7,633, 18.7% lower (Yahoo, provisional). Komatsu has nothing to do with data centres. Its customers are mines and builders, so this is a Japanese industrial sold alongside chip stocks. It adds to Industrials (7.19% of the book) and Japan (8.31%) and offsets the tech concentration, but not the yen exposure Leo already carries through 5332.T, 7532.T, 8001.T and the receipts.
- **What would have to be true.** The margin squeeze has to be tariff cost rather than lost pricing power: sales growing 14% while operating profit grows 3.5% needs explaining from the segment tables. The press reports a net tariff cost in the full-year plan, which was not checked at source and so is not quoted (see section 4). Mining demand has to hold at metal prices well off their highs (aluminium is the one we can see, through Hydro). **The 7532.T test, and here it bites, because Leo named the yen in that regret.** The plan assumes ¥154.5 to the dollar, up from ¥152.1 in July; the local rate is ¥158.04 (`prices.json`). Part of a yen exporter's profit growth is currency. The yen strengthened on the midterm result, and Komatsu's guidance is already more cautious than the spot rate. The Deep dive has to split out the currency effect before pricing the growth.
