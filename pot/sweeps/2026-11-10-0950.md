### SAMPO.HE — Nasdaq Helsinki — Sampo (new)

- **Why:** a Nordic and UK property-and-casualty insurer whose underlying business is growing while the reported-profit comparison looks much worse: Q3 like-for-like top-line growth **6%**, underwriting result **€435m**, up **9% at constant currency**, and combined ratio **82.6%**; reported net profit fell **64%**. The previous year included a NOBA IPO gain, while this quarter includes market-value movements and an IT impairment. The earnings bridge needs examination before choosing a valuation basis. Sources: [issuer's Q3 release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/04/3395014/0/en/sampo-group-s-results-for-january-september-2026.html), [Sampo's Q&A](https://www.sampo.com/investors/ir-blog/january-september-2026-results-qa/).
- **What would have to be true:** policy growth must retain underwriting discipline through softer pricing and normal weather losses, rather than depend on favourable claims or reserve releases; inspect the **17 November investor update** and subsequent results. Value reported and operating earnings separately, account for investment risk, and establish a comparable historical basis before calling the shares cheap. The issuer explicitly attributes part of its outlook upgrade to favourable claims ([release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/04/3395014/0/en/sampo-group-s-results-for-january-september-2026.html)).

**Exposure and thesis:** adds Financial Services in Europe. T1/T2 prompted a second-order question: can an insurer buy cheaper automation while retaining the physical claims network? Management argues that agents do not replace repair networks, but also acknowledges easier comparison and switching ([Q&A](https://www.sampo.com/investors/ir-blog/january-september-2026-results-qa/)). That is management's opinion, not verified incremental AI profit. **7532.T test:** the underwriting evidence is already in the accounts; a forecast rerating or an AI label is unnecessary. Familiarity with the actual Nordic and UK franchises remains work for the Deep dive.

### AAK.ST — Nasdaq Stockholm — AAK (new, recovery watch)

- **Why:** a supplier of tailored vegetable oils and fats offers a less obvious way to investigate food manufacturers' pressure to reformulate and reduce costs. Its [business model](https://www.aak.com/about-AAK/business-model/) makes that a plausible lead, not evidence that demand has recovered. Q3 volumes were **509,000 tonnes versus 510,000**, operating profit **fell 8%**, and operating cash flow was **SEK1,496m versus SEK542m**. Issuer-defined rolling ROCE excluding comparability items was **19.1%**; this is not our pipeline's ROIC. Sources: [23 October release](https://www.aak.com/news-and-media/press-releases/2026/aaks-interim-report-for-the-third-quarter-2026/).
- **What would have to be true:** the next results must show whether volume and profit per kilo resume growth, with cash generation surviving normal working-capital needs. This is my recovery test, not company guidance that growth resumes next quarter. Q3 profit per kilo fell to **SEK2.27 from SEK2.47**; working capital contributed **SEK143m**, against an outflow of **SEK673m** previously. Much of the cash improvement is therefore a working-capital swing, not faster earnings growth ([Q3 report, pp. 2–4](https://storage.mfn.se/46b85c4c-a56a-40c3-9b38-d9b4ee246267/aak-interim-report-q3-2026.pdf)).

**Exposure and thesis:** adds food ingredients, economically Consumer Defensive, with global customers; the pipeline should confirm its sector classification. No standing AI thesis drove it. **7532.T test:** this is explicitly a flat-volume, falling-profit recovery case under strategy §2.4b. The report describes more competitive pricing and normalising favourable conditions. Do not mistake cheaper food inputs, a working-capital release or a high historical return on capital for a return to growth. Add the name to learn its valuation and follow its accounts, not to anticipate a rebound.
