## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** The oil shock is easing at the price edge, not yet in the physical balance. Local WTI is **$93.00**, down **9.21%** over seven days but still **46.66%** higher over one year, while the US 10-year yield is **4.963%** ([prices.json](../../prices.json)). The IEA says world oil supply is projected to average **100.7 mb/d** in 2026, down **5.7 mb/d** year on year, and observed inventories fell by **507 million barrels** from February through August ([IEA September Oil Market Report](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-september-2026), [IEA 18 September commentary](https://www.iea.org/commentaries/oil-markets-strain-to-plug-the-gap-left-by-middle-east-supply-shortfall)). The seven-day price fall therefore does not by itself establish that the constraint has cleared.

**Observation.** A separate constraint became more measurable on 21 September. The European Commission proposed a common rating for data centres above **500 kW** covering actual energy and water use, grid support, waste-heat reuse and clean generation; it expects the first labels in 2027 and has opened consultation on minimum performance standards planned for the second quarter of 2027. The same release says the EU wants to triple data-centre capacity over five to seven years and that the sector could consume more than **3%** of EU electricity by 2030 ([European Commission rating proposal](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/making-data-centres-energy-efficient-thanks-new-eu-rating-system-2026-09-21_en), [standards consultation](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/minimum-performance-standards-data-centres-europe-public-consultation-launched-2026-09-21_en)). This goes beyond the local macro block, which carries energy prices but no data-centre resource-use or efficiency series.

**Inference.** The new label does not create electricity or cooling demand; the capacity build already does that. Its investment significance is narrower: energy and water performance are moving toward visible, comparable operating constraints, which may favour equipment that can prove lower resource use rather than merely attach an AI label. That led to Munters. The guard is that this is still part of the crowded data-centre capital cycle, not a hedge against it, and high order intake is not shareholder value until it becomes margin and cash after the factory ramp.
