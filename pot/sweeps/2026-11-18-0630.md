**Observation: European gas is still tight while crude eases.** WTI is **$84.66, −16.8% over a month** (`prices.json`). That matters most for businesses whose contracts move gas costs on to the customer, and for those whose contracts do not. Air Liquide below is the first kind.

### ABF.L, London Stock Exchange: Associated British Foods (new)

- **Why:** Primark is a business Leo can watch from the shop floor, and it now has a dated catalyst that the market has gone quiet on. The Board will demerge Primark from the food businesses, with completion expected **before the end of calendar 2027** ([ABF, outcome of structure review](https://www.abf.co.uk/media/news/2026/ABF-announces-the-outcome-of-its-review-of-Group-structure)). The FY2026 results (52 weeks to 12 September, published 3 November) report adjusted operating profit of **£1,508m, −14% at constant currency**, and adjusted EPS of **156.0p, −11%**. Primark's like-for-like sales were **−2.6%**: **+0.5% in the UK and Ireland, −4.7% in continental Europe**. Its margin was **10.2%** against 11.9%, and Sugar lost **£47m**. The outlook says: "In 2027, the Group is expected to deliver adjusted operating profit and adjusted EPS below 2026" ([annual results announcement](https://www.abf.co.uk/content/dam/abf/corporate/Documents/investors/results-and-presentations/2026/abf-annual-results-2026-announcement.pdf.downloadasset.pdf)).
- **What would have to be true:** continental Europe's like-for-like decline has to be about stores and execution, not a structural loss to online ultra-cheap fashion. The first test is Primark's like-for-like in the H1 FY27 trading update. The second is whether the 2027 margin stays at the guided ~10% rather than sliding further.

**Sourcing caveat:** ABF's site returned 403 to every direct fetch. These figures come from the search index of ABF's own announcement, and the outlook sentence was confirmed by one scout lookup. The Deep dive must read the PDF before using any of them. **This is a shrinking business** (§2.4b): profit is down this year and guided down next year, so any proposal must argue when growth resumes.

**Market reaction and the 7532.T test:** the shares rose from **1,900p to 2,021p (+6.4%) on results day**, despite a guided profit decline. The price is now **2,034p, −4.8% over a year** (Yahoo, 17 November close, **provisional**). That says the market had priced something worse. The 7532.T mistake would be letting "I shop there" stand in for the continental Europe numbers, which are the weak part. The offset is real: a UK consumer and food business against a book that is 56% megacap tech.

### GSK.L, London Stock Exchange: GSK (new)

- **Why:** the 2025 noise about US pharma tariffs and pricing has gone quiet because GSK settled it. It has "entered into an agreement with the US Administration to lower the cost of prescription medicines", with "Section 232 tariff relief through 20 January 2029". Q3 sales were **£8.9bn, +5% CER**, with Specialty Medicines **£3.9bn, +15%**. Full-year guidance was reaffirmed: turnover **+3–5%**, core operating profit and core EPS **+7–9%**, and an ambition of **more than £40bn of sales in 2031** ([Q3 6-K on EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001131399/000165495426009181/a6993a.htm)).
- **What would have to be true:** Specialty growth has to carry the business through dolutegravir's loss of exclusivity. The company itself dates that to **2028–2030**, and HIV is still **£2.1bn a quarter** (same 6-K). Management says operating margin will be "stable to improving" through that period. That claim is the falsifier: watch HIV and core operating margin in each annual report from FY2027.

**Evidence and weakness:** Q3 **core EPS was 54.6p, only +1% CER**, against a full-year guide of 7–9%, and **total EPS was 38.5p, −21%**. Vaccines fell **2%** and net debt is **£20.7bn** (6-K). The shares fell from **1,923.5p to 1,831.5p (−4.8%) on results day** and are now **1,869p**, 15.5% below their 52-week high weekly close of **2,213p** (Yahoo, **provisional**). **The 7532.T test:** the risk here is not a rebound narrative. It is a known patent cliff, which the market can see coming and may already price. That is why the valuation filter has to decide. It adds to Healthcare, which is 1.3% of the book.

### AI.PA, Euronext Paris: Air Liquide (new)

- **Why:** this is a direct test of section 1's gas inference. Air Liquide's industrial gas contracts are built to pass energy costs to the customer. Q3 2026 revenue was **€7,259m**, with Gas & Services **+3.6% on a comparable basis**. The company says it is confident it can raise operating margin by **+100bp** and grow recurring net profit at constant FX in 2026. It is executing a **first €1bn buyback tranche** by year end and has launched a plan, **BEYOND**, running to 2030 ([Q3 2026 release](https://www.airliquide.com/group/press-releases-news/2026-10-29/q3-2026-results-air-liquide-accelerates-driven-sales-growth-and-launch-its-strategic-plan)).
- **What would have to be true:** comparable growth and the margin target have to hold while energy costs pass through, so that the gas shock inflates revenue and not risk. The test is the FY2026 results: compare the reported margin, the margin excluding energy, and ROCE, not the headline margin, which pass-through distorts.

**Sourcing caveat:** the release page returned 403. The PDF downloaded, but its fonts could not be decoded (section 4). These figures come from the search index of airliquide.com, restricted to that domain, and need reading against the document before use.

**Exposure and the 7532.T test:** it offsets the book on sector (Basic Materials) and geography (France). The company's growth is led by Electronics, though, which is gases for chip fabs, so part of it is the same AI capex cycle the book already holds. **It is not cheap on its face.** The price is **€190.94, +27.2% over a year**, close to its 52-week high of €193.32 (Yahoo, **provisional**). It goes on the list for its resilience, not for a bargain, and the valuation filter may well keep it there for a long time.

At **493.2p** (Yahoo, **provisional**) it is a merger spread of about 2.6%, not a business decision, and it closes the open item from the previous sweep.
