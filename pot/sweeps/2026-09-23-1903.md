## 1. Where the world is

**Observation: long rates.** The US 10-year yield is **5.122%**, up **3.2%** on the day and **23.5%** over one year. The 5-year is **5.005%**, and the dollar index is up **2.23%** over one month ([prices.json](../../prices.json)). This morning's sweep quoted the 10-year at 4.968%, so it has crossed 5% since. The OECD's interim outlook, published today, says 30-year sovereign yields sit at levels "unseen in the past decade or two" and that higher long-term rates are "weighing on equity valuations". It projects G20 headline inflation of **4.1%** in 2026 and **3.6%** in 2027, and world GDP growth of **2.9%** in 2026 and **3.0%** in 2027 ([OECD Interim Economic Outlook, September 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-outlook-interim-report-september-2026_f751d02b-en.html)). The OECD pages refused a direct fetch (HTTP 403). These figures come from the text of the OECD pages as returned by search, not from the PDF.

**Observation: El Niño, where I went beyond the macro block.** The OECD names "a very strong El Niño" as a key downside risk to growth and food prices. NOAA's Climate Prediction Center, on 10 September, gives a **greater than 90%** chance of a very strong event this northern winter. It also gives a **75%** chance that October to December exceeds +2.5°C, which would be the strongest since records begin in 1950 ([NOAA CPC ENSO discussion](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_disc_sep2026/ensodisc.shtml)). One physical effect is already in force. On 20 August the Panama Canal Authority cut daily slots to **34** from 3 September and **32** from 15 September, with only **9** of them at the Neopanamax locks. It also set a **47.5 ft** maximum draft from 1 October, all because of "below-expected precipitation in the Canal watershed" ([ACP announcement](https://pancanal.com/en/panama-canal-adopts-additional-measures-to-address-reduced-precipitation-in-the-canal-watershed/)). The canal's dry season still lies ahead. No earlier sweep in `pot/sweeps/` mentions El Niño at all.

**Inference.** Two things follow, one for the book and one for discovery.

- *The book.* About three-fifths of it is long-duration megacap growth, and a 10-year above 5% is the discount-rate pressure the OECD describes. That is not a reason to sell anything. It is a reason to prefer candidates whose earnings do not depend on a low discount rate.
- *Discovery.* The obvious El Niño trade is palm oil, and its price has already moved (see *Screened* below). The less-covered effect is on logistics: a throttled Panama Canal while Hormuz stays shut. That lengthens routes for US LPG going to Asia. BW LPG's own filing describes exactly this, and it is not a sector Leo owns.

No standing thesis in [`theses.md`](../theses.md) drove either candidate. Both sit outside T1 to T3.
