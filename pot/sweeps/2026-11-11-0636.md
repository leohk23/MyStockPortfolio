**What is true: memory chip prices are hurting hardware makers Leo holds.** On 3 November Apple warned that memory chip inflation would squeeze its gross margins through the holiday quarter, and European chip stocks fell 2–4% that day ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://au.finance.yahoo.com/news/european-shares-falls-apple-margin-084013991.html)). The memory story also sits in the same place as the AI story: SK Hynix (held) is down **11.3%** over a month in our data even with memory prices rising.

### SN.L — London Stock Exchange — Smith+Nephew (new)

- **Why:** the company is telling the market that its weak division might be sold. On 4 November Smith+Nephew said it had approached Johnson & Johnson about Orthopaedics, that the talks had ended, and that it would "continue to actively explore the full range of strategic options for the business" ([6-K, 4 November](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000845982/000110465926122324/snn-20261104x6k.htm)). Orthopaedics was **$590m** of **$1,551m** Q3 revenue, or 38%. Sports Medicine & ENT grew **7.9%** underlying, Joint Repair grew double digits for a fourth quarter, and group underlying growth sped up to **3.4%** from 1.6% in Q2. The year's guidance was still cut: underlying revenue growth about **3%**, trading profit about **$1,250m**, free cash flow about **$750m**. A **$500m** buyback is half done ([Q3 trading update, 4 November](https://smith-nephew.stylelabs.cloud/api/public/content/f2a63b2b34d844008dc600fe90e5e66d?v=1861658c), read with `pdfText`).
- **What would have to be true:** the growing businesses must carry the group on their own merits. Sports Medicine and Wound Management have to keep growing at mid-to-high single digits, and the trading profit must convert to cash at the guided ~95%, whether or not Orthopaedics is ever sold. If the case depends on a deal happening, it is speculation. Test it at the full-year results.

**Exposure and thesis:** adds Healthcare in the UK, against a book with almost none, and is unrelated to AI capex. No thesis drove it. **7532.T test:** the risk here is the reverse of 7532.T. That was a story priced before the fundamentals were checked. This is a set of known-bad fundamentals (US hips and knees) that might be removed. The trap would be paying for a break-up that has not happened. The pipeline's P/E history will show whether the shares already price one in. The press reports a large activist shareholder, which I have not checked against a holding notice.

### 7733.T — Tokyo Stock Exchange — Olympus (new)

- **Why:** the US regulator has stopped imports of more of its products, which is the loud news, while the business underneath is reported to be growing. On 20 October the FDA widened its import alert on Olympus devices made in Japan. It now covers ureterorenoscopes, bronchoscopes, laparoscopes, endoscope reprocessors, capsule imaging systems and enteroscopes, adding to the alert of 24 June 2025, after warning letters from 2022 to 2026 for quality-system violations. Hospitals may keep using devices already installed ([FDA letter](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/letters-health-care-providers/update-import-alert-certain-olympus-medical-devices-manufactured-japan-letter-health-care-providers)). Olympus reported its first half on 10 November. Press reports describe strong growth and raised guidance, but I could not read Olympus's own documents (see section 4), so I quote no figure from them. Taking the H1 numbers from the filing is the first job for the Deep dive.
- **What would have to be true:** the import alert has to be a fixable manufacturing problem with a date on it, not a lasting loss of US share. The test is whether the next filings show US revenue in the affected lines holding up, and whether the FDA lifts or narrows the alert. Until one of those happens, the risk has no date, and the Deep dive's Rule 4 halves any order.

**Exposure and thesis:** adds Healthcare in Japan. Japan is already 8.45% of the book, but Leo's Japan exposure is a trading house, games and consumer names, not medtech. No thesis drove it. **7532.T test:** the 7532.T mistake was ignoring yen depreciation. A Japanese exporter whose US sales are being blocked is exposed to the same currency in reverse, since a weak yen flatters the reported growth. Separate constant-currency growth from reported growth before calling it cheap.
