## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** August US CPI rose **0.4% month on month** and **3.4% year on year**. Energy rose
**2.1% in the month** and gasoline **3.9%**, while core CPI rose **0.3% in the month** and slowed
to **2.4% year on year** ([BLS release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)). The local
pre-release snapshot has WTI at **$99.50**, down **2.91% on the day** but up **6.95% over seven
days**, and the US ten-year yield at **4.944%** ([prices](../../prices.json)). **Inference.** The
energy shock is reaching households, but the lower core annual rate argues against calling it a
broad reacceleration from this release alone. It strengthens the case for testing pricing power,
customer cash budgets and energy efficiency in names already under review; it does not make an oil
producer or an efficiency supplier automatically attractive.

**Observation.** UK real GDP rose **0.4% in July** and **0.4% in the three months to July**.
Services rose **0.4% in the month**; the ONS says computer programming and consultancy rose
**3.5%**, with many of the largest turnover reports connected to AI and cloud computing, but it
cannot quantify their exact contribution. Consumer-facing services fell **0.4% in July**, and
wholesale trade fell **1.4%** ([ONS release](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproductgdp/bulletins/gdpmonthlyestimateuk/july2026/pdf)).
**Inference.** The headline is stronger than the household-facing detail. It is evidence for real
AI and cloud activity, but not for the economics of any supplier and not a reason to ignore T1 or
T2's attack on model and seat-pricing margins. The consumer split keeps the burden of proof on the
existing discount-retail and discretionary candidates rather than creating a new one.

The IEA launched its [September Oil Market Report](https://www.iea.org/events/oil-market-report-september-2026)
today, but the accessible page supplied no September balances or highlights. I did not carry
August figures forward as though they were the new report.
