Stryker's release gives no cause beyond the quarter falling "short of our expectations". None of the three releases mentions GLP-1 drugs.

**Inference, and it is mine, not theirs:** hips are still growing at Zimmer, so this is not a collapse in elective surgery. The weakness is specific to knees, and it shows up at all three companies, so it is not one company losing share. The knee is the joint most tied to body weight. A knee market that stops growing while weight-loss drugs spread fits the obvious hypothesis, but nothing here proves it, and I hold no prescription data to test it. It could as easily be US hospital capacity, a pause before cementless-knee launches (Smith+Nephew's release points to the US approval of its cementless LANDMARK), or price. The companies have not said, and the next observable is their fourth-quarter releases in late January and February. Novo Nordisk (NVO), already watched, is the other side of the same question.

### ZBH — NYSE — Zimmer Biomet (new)

- **Why:** the largest knee-and-hip maker. It is the cheapest of the three on its own guidance, and the only one that *raised* its guidance this quarter. Provisional Yahoo price **$104.56** on 12 November, 52-week range $79.12–112.55 ([chart endpoint](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/ZBH?range=2y&interval=1wk)). On the $8.55 midpoint of its adjusted EPS guidance that is about **12.2x**, against about **21.0x** for Stryker ($312.39 on the same endpoint, $14.85 midpoint). That is my arithmetic, on *company-adjusted* forward figures.
- **What would have to be true:** the knee decline has to be a pause, not GLP-1 shrinking the patient pool, because knees are Zimmer's biggest line ($767.5m of $2,062.9m in Q3, −2.6%). The gap between adjusted and reported earnings also has to be one-off. Q3 diluted EPS was **$1.00** against **$1.90** adjusted (same release), and amortisation and restructuring that recur every year are not one-offs.

**Exposure:** adds Healthcare, the book's thinnest sector, but adds to the US. **7532.T test:** this is the shape of that mistake if the knee story is structural. A low multiple on a shrinking main product is a value trap, and the low P/E is the very thing that makes it look safe. Its value to the watchlist is that the pipeline will now fetch five years of *reported* P/E history, so the Scan can say whether 12x adjusted is cheap by its own standards or just the market pricing a smaller knee market. Debt is **$7.5bn** against **$400m** of cash at 30 September (same release). Its nine-month free cash flow of about **$926m** is my derivation from the cash-flow lines, not a figure the company states.

### COH.AX — ASX — Cochlear (new)

- **Why:** the hearing-implant leader. It is healthcare outside the US, in a country the book has no exposure to, and its price has already done the damage. Provisional Yahoo price **A$151.37** on 13 November. Weekly closes run from A$312.83 in July 2025 down to **A$96.33** in May 2026 ([chart endpoint](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/COH.AX?range=2y&interval=1wk)). On 22 April it cut FY26 underlying net profit guidance to **A$290–330m**, from A$435–460m. The reasons it gave were softer developed-market implant demand since January, hospital capacity constraints, fewer referrals from the hearing-aid channel, and Middle East uncertainty ([ASX, 22 Apr](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260422/pdf/06yqjm0ttfryc1.pdf)). FY26 came in at **A$322.4m** underlying, −22%. Sales were A$2,343.4m, −1% reported and +2% in constant currency, and implant units were **56,692, +5%**. FY27 is guided at **A$330–350m** underlying net profit, on low single-digit constant-currency sales growth ([FY26 release, 18 Aug](https://coh.live.irmau.com/irm/pdf/b0352dff-31d2-4c0a-83bc-49f4f6d81992/Platform/ListPage/FY26-Result-ASX-amp-Media-Release.pdf)).
- **What would have to be true:** the April problem has to be hospital throughput, not a lasting loss of referrals. The hearing-aid channel feeding fewer candidates would be a structural change, and that is the one to rule out. Units still grew 5% while revenue was flat in constant currency, so price and mix fell, and that needs explaining too.
