The repo has Berkshire at $570.89, up 10.7% in a month in which the Nasdaq fell 10.2%, and at the 99th percentile of its five-year P/E (`prices.json` `pePctile` 0.993).

**Observed: someone has been switching off Europe's supermarket fridges.** On the night of 11 to 12 October, attackers reached refrigeration controllers in French supermarkets over the internet and forced them into defrost cycles. On 12 October CERT-FR, France's government computer emergency team, reported "numerous compromises" of Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC controllers. On 13 October it added Copeland's and Dixell's xweb consoles. It told operators to remove these units from the public internet, to use a VPN, and not to keep end-of-life equipment exposed ([CERT-FR, CERTFR-2026-ALE-012](https://www.cert.cyber.gouv.fr/alerte/CERTFR-2026-ALE-012/)). A second wave on 2 November hit chains in Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Sweden, Ireland and Germany, among them Ahold Delhaize's Delhaize and Colruyt ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/02/cyberattack-on-fridges-hits-supermarkets-across-europe.html), narrative). The press reports up to 700 French stores in the first wave, credentials as simple as "admin" with the date as the password, and controllers that Danfoss stopped supporting in 2016 and 2021 ([Wikipedia's summary of Reuters, the Guardian and Claroty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_French_supermarket_refrigerator_hack), narrative). Danfoss's own advisory refused this run's request with a 403. No earlier sweep has mentioned the attack.

**Observed: whey protein has come off its peak.** USDA's Dairy Market News for the week of 2 to 6 November says regular WPC 80% prices are "in the $8s and $9s" a pound, and whey protein isolate "in the mid-$13s to mid-$14s" ([USDA AMS](https://www.ams.usda.gov/mnreports/dywweeklyreport.pdf), read with `pdfText`). The trade press puts the summer peak near $13 and the price a year ago near $5.70 ([Baking Business](https://www.bakingbusiness.com/articles/67168-whey-protein-rally-falters-but-demand-holds), narrative, not checked at source). That matters to the candidate below.

**Inference.** Berkshire is a case where two of the repo's numbers disagree. The 99th-percentile P/E is struck on GAAP earnings, which carry the investment gains. Those halved this quarter while operating earnings fell 5.8%. Management, which can see the operating businesses, was buying more of its own stock, not less. Neither number settles it. The question for the Review is whether a P/E on GAAP earnings is the right test for Berkshire at all, and it should read the 10-Q, not this summary. The soft underwriting is also worth knowing for Tokio Marine (TKOMY, held), which reports on 19 November and has a large US specialty business. The fridge attack is the kind of story this lane exists for: it is loud in the security press, nobody has traced it to a company, and it is spreading. But the fix CERT-FR asks for is network hygiene, which is cheap. Only the unpatchable end-of-life units have to be replaced. So the second-order effect is likely smaller than it sounds, and section 3 says why it is not carried.

## 3. Candidates

### GL9.IR — Euronext Dublin — Glanbia (new)

- **Why.** Glanbia, the owner of Optimum Nutrition protein powders, has raised its 2026 guidance twice. In February it guided adjusted EPS growth of 7% to 11% at constant currency, on 2025 adjusted EPS of 134.93 US cents, against 140.03 cents in 2024 ([FY2025 results](https://www.glanbia.com/media/press-releases/full-year-2025-results)). On 4 November it raised the range from 17%–20% to 22%–24% ([Q3 2026 IMS](https://otp.tools.investis.com/clients/uk/glanbia/rns/regulatory-story.aspx?newsid=2119136&cid=512)). The share fell 8.5% to €19.42 on Friday, two days later ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/11/06/european-shares-tumble-while-us-stocks-soar/), narrative). That is 21.8% below its weekly high of €24.84 in the week of 28 June, but 29.9% above a year ago (Yahoo, provisional). The IMS shows where the growth comes from:

  | Like-for-like revenue growth | Total | Volume | Price |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|
  | Optimum Nutrition, Q3 | 26.9% | 0.8% | 26.1% |
  | Performance Nutrition, year to date | 18.0% | 4.3% | 13.7% |
  | Group, year to date | 9.9% | 4.2% | 5.7% |

  The chief executive describes the price rises as "pricing actions in response to significant whey inflation". Net debt was $704m. In 2025 the Performance Nutrition EBITDA margin fell to 13.0% from 16.9% on "record whey input costs" (FY2025 results). Whey has now come off its peak (section 1). If the price rises hold while whey falls, the margin recovers, and the guidance does not yet include that. On the guided range, 2026 adjusted EPS would be 164.6 to 167.3 US cents. At today's $1.1143 per euro (`prices.json` `macro`) that is about 13x at €19.42, and 16.0x on 2025's adjusted EPS. This is this lane's arithmetic, on an adjusted basis, with the FX conversion done here. The pipeline will give the reported multiple once it has the ticker. **What it does to the book:** it adds Consumer Defensive (3.3% of the book) and offsets Technology and Communication Services (56.0%), in Europe (8.1%). It diversifies by sector more than by currency: Glanbia reports in dollars, and the Americas are 60% of Performance Nutrition. No thesis drove it.
- **What would have to be true.** The price has to stick when the input cost falls, without losing the shelf. Volume at Optimum Nutrition grew only 0.8% in Q3, so a 26% price rise has already slowed it nearly to a stop. If competitors cut prices as whey falls and Glanbia has to follow, revenue reverses and the margin recovery is smaller than it looks. Two things to read first. **One:** the Q4 and full-year statement, which will show the volume and price split on falling whey. **Two:** the gap between adjusted and reported earnings. Glanbia's 2024 reported basic EPS was 63.21 US cents against 140.03 adjusted (FY2025 results). Leo is a CPA, and the reconciliation in the 2025 annual report is where that gap is explained, or not. **The 7532.T test:** this is the closest thing in this sweep to being "blinded by the rebound". The story is a protein boom with GLP-1 users said to be buying it, and both are loud. The test is volume, not revenue. A brand growing 0.8% in volume, after a price rise, is not a boom. It is a pass-through that has worked so far. The valuation filter only passes if the business is worth ~13x at a normal whey price.
