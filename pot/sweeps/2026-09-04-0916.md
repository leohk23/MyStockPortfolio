# Sweep — 4 September 2026

## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** The repo's 09:09 UTC snapshot has WTI at $90.57, up 5.6% over seven days and
46.4% over a year, while the US ten-year yield is 4.762%. Core PCE is still 3.34%, and the BEA
reports that real consumer spending was essentially flat in July. **Inference.** This is an
input-cost and discount-rate squeeze landing on demand that was already soft. I therefore looked
for contracted infrastructure and products that can prove a cash saving, while treating a high
margin or an AI label as inadequate on its own. Sources: [local markets and inflation](../../prices.json)
and [BEA personal income and outlays](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-july-2026).

**Observation.** The latest EIA table puts US commercial crude supply at 24.4 days for the week to
28 August, down from 24.8 days a week earlier. The agency also corrected its prior summary: US
distillate inventories fell 2.2m barrels in the preceding week rather than rising. **Inference.**
The useful exposure is not merely "oil goes up". Storage, handling and efficiency become more
valuable when routes, grades and refined products are dislocated, but only if contracts and
customer savings outlast the headline. Sources: [EIA crude days of supply](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/pet_sum_sndw_a_epc0_vsd_days_w.htm)
and the [EIA correction](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/includes/revision-notice.php).

**Observation.** The BLS's August employment report is scheduled for 12:30 UTC today, after this
sweep's cutoff. The latest available release says July payrolls fell by 23,000 and revised May and
June down by a combined 103,000. **Inference.** The central macro question is unresolved rather
than bullish or bearish: weaker labour may restrain demand, while oil can keep inflation and bond
yields high. I did not turn a pre-release guess about the Fed into a candidate. Source: [BLS July
employment report and release schedule](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.htm).

## 3. Candidates

### VPK.AS — Royal Vopak — Euronext Amsterdam

**Why it deserves work:** Vopak's first half held proportional occupancy at 91%, produced a 15.3%
rolling operating cash return and converted about 74% of proportional EBITDA into operating free
cash flow. Long-term gas and industrial contracts, plus active oil and chemical terminals, make it
a second-order energy-security candidate rather than a bet on one commodity price. Source:
[Vopak's first-half release](https://www.vopak.com/newsroom/news/vopak-reports-strong-first-half-2026-results-increases-fy-2026-outlook-and-announces?language_content_entity=en).

**What would have to be true:** The €2.3bn of committed growth projects must lift cash flow per
share without pushing proportional leverage beyond management's 2.5–3.0× range, and the new battery
projects must earn returns comparable with the contracted terminal estate rather than dilute its
15.3% cash return.

### IOT — Samsara — New York Stock Exchange

**Why it deserves work:** Second-quarter revenue and ARR each grew 30%, operating cash flow was
$73.5m, free cash flow was $64.7m and this was the fourth consecutive GAAP-profitable quarter. Its
connected-operations platform is a direct way for fleets and physical businesses to attack fuel
and operating costs rather than merely endure them. Source: [Samsara's SEC-filed results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1642896/000162828026060438/samsaraepr-q22027.htm).

**What would have to be true:** Customer savings must be large enough to sustain retention as
growth moderates, cash earnings must outgrow stock-based compensation, and the independently
fetched valuation must leave room for that moderation. A 30% ARR headline alone is not enough.

These are candidates, not orders. Aon, Valero, Guidewire and Lululemon were checked but not
advanced: Aon's USI purchase depends on debt and sizeable forecast synergies; Valero is a direct
refining-cycle exposure whose current earnings already reflect unusually strong margins;
Guidewire's improving recurring economics still require a valuation case; and Lululemon's falling
comparable sales are a brand turnaround, not yet evidence of one.
