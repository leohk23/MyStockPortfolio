### ML.PA — Euronext Paris — Michelin (new)

- **Why:** a familiar replacement product with a measurable split between brand strength and a weak industry: Michelin reports nine-month MICHELIN-brand replacement volumes **+4%**, while group tyre volumes were **−0.8%**. Higher fuel costs make efficiency worth investigating, but do not prove customers will pay more. [Company Q3 release](https://www.michelin.com/en/investors/presentation-events/results-sales).
- **What would have to be true:** replacement growth must survive weak vehicle demand, and price/mix must protect cash without masking continuing unit decline; test that at the next full-year release, scheduled for **11 February 2027** on the same company results page, alongside the pipeline's valuation filter.

**Evidence and weakness:** nine-month organic revenue growth was only **+0.1%** at constant scope and currency. This is a near-flat recovery candidate, not broad growth. In [H1](https://www.michelin.com/en/publications/group/financial-information-at-june-30-2026), reported revenue fell **2.6%** while constant-currency revenue rose **0.5%**; adverse FX was **3.1%**. Segment operating income was **€1.45bn**, an **11.4%** margin, and free cash flow before M&A was **€282m**, versus **−€102m** a year earlier. Those company-defined measures are not net profit or cash after acquisitions. Q3 reaffirmed full-year growth in segment operating income at constant scope/FX and **over €1.6bn** free cash flow before M&A. A margin gain alone does not demonstrate attractive capital returns.

**Exposure and 7532.T test:** adds European manufacturing and tyre-replacement economics, but adds to existing consumer-cyclical risk. The mistake would be treating an energy-efficiency story as proof of earnings growth or overlooking FX and cheap competing tyres. The filing's declining volumes are the warning. Yahoo resolved the symbol, but its search quote was dated **6 November** and live access returned **429**; no current price or multiple is asserted.

### BXB.AX — Australian Securities Exchange — Brambles (new)

- **Why:** CHEP's reusable-pallet network has a specific operational recovery to test: the company attributes **US$90m** of FY26 Underlying Profit drag to US repair constraints, while reporting **22.6% ROCI**, its own alternative capital-return measure. [22 October AGM address, pp. 4–6](https://www.brambles.com/Content/cms/pdf/2026/Brambles-2026-AGM-Addresses.pdf).
- **What would have to be true:** repair constraints must be resolved by the end of H1 FY27 as management expects, without recurring emergency purchases or overtime consuming the cash; its next half-year results are **18 February 2027** ([results calendar](https://www.brambles.com/results-centre)).

**Evidence and weakness:** the AGM says FY26 like-for-like volumes fell **2%**, offset by new business. FY27 guidance is **+2–4% sales** and **+2–6% Underlying Profit**, both constant currency, but free cash flow before dividends is guided at **US$800–950m**, below FY26's **over US$1bn**. This is a recovery with weaker near-term cash, not an unqualified compounder. Management says new repair capacity's benefit has so far been absorbed by inefficiencies. Its ROCI is not the repo's after-tax ROIC.

**Exposure and 7532.T test:** adds an Australian-listed Industrials business, but not predominantly Australian demand: FY26 CHEP Americas revenue was **US$3,867.1m** of group **US$7,042.9m**, about **54.9%** (calculated from the [FY26 issuer release, p. 9](https://www.brambles.com/Content/cms/FY26-Results/pdf/Brambles-FY26-Result-ASX-Media-Release.pdf)). It diversifies the business mix more than US economic exposure. The trap is accepting a temporary-bottleneck explanation while overlooking structural repair costs and extra capital tied up in pallets. Diesel tightness is also a cost risk, not automatically a benefit. [Yahoo's indexed quote](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BXB.AX/) is **A$22.20 as of 16 November, provisional**; direct access returned 429, so this is not asserted as today's close. No earnings percentile is estimated.
