## 1. Where the world is

**Observation.** The local market record is not showing a broad bargain. The S&P 500 fell 1.1% and the Nasdaq 1.9% in the latest session but remain up 15.7% and 17.7% over one year. Japan moved more sharply: the Nikkei fell 4.4% in the latest session and 6.2% over seven days, yet remains up 34.1% over one year. WTI is $94.01, up 63.6% over one year, and the US 10-year yield is 5.190% ([local market data](../../prices.json)). **Inference.** A one-day fall inside strong one-year equity returns, high oil and a high discount rate is a stress test rather than an indiscriminate entry point. It does not justify turning the low Japanese, European or Healthcare weights into a quota.

**Observation beyond the local macro block.** The IMF's October Global Financial Stability Report says market resilience rests partly on AI optimism, while weaker AI investment or profitability, rising sovereign yields and cyber incidents could trigger stress. It also identifies leverage and liquidity pressure in private credit, and crowded hedge-fund positions that can amplify a shock ([IMF report](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/GFSR/Issues/2026/10/13/global-financial-stability-report-october-2026), [IMF launch briefing](https://mediacenter.imf.org/news/imf---global-financial-stability-report-annual-meetings--2026/s/f55368c1-983b-45b7-ba7c-1978f67c168c)). The BIS separately reports that 28.7% of AI firms' investment deals by value from 2021 to 2025 went into other AI firms; among AI-to-AI deals, 46.4% by value also involved a commercial supply-chain relationship between investor and target ([BIS Bulletin 137](https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-137-circular-relationships-among-ai-firms)). **Inference.** T1's cheap-model argument is not the only threat to Leo's crowded technology exposure: the financing network can correlate businesses that look separate. The second-order beneficiary would need demand independent of hyperscaler capex, but today's sources did not identify a new company whose own accounts prove that distinction.

**Observation beyond the local macro block.** A Bank of Japan Policy Board member said today that underlying inflation appears anchored around 2%, the policy rate rose to about 1.25% after September's increase, and the Bank should not be overly cautious about further rises. She also said construction costs and project delays, rather than rates so far, are the main drag on business-investment plans ([Bank of Japan speech](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/about/press/koen_2026/data/ko261015a1.pdf)). **Inference.** Japan is no longer a simple low-rate diversification story. A Japanese candidate now has to show pricing power and returns after higher funding and construction costs; buying the market's sharp daily fall without that company evidence would repeat the 7532.T mistake.
