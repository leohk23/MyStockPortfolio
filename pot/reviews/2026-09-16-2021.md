## The thresholds are calibrated tighter than the headline margin suggests

The last review's complaint that the accepted break could not see its own P6 risk is **resolved**:
the top-up Leo filled carries a standalone asset-return route, and the section above shows it
reading. A different calibration problem has taken its place, and it runs the other way.

MWA's fiscal Q4 (September) and Q1 (December) are its two seasonally weakest quarters, and those are
precisely the two post-entry quarters the break tests. Filed GAAP operating margins, with the
September quarter derived as year minus nine months:

| fiscal quarter | revenue $m | operating income $m | GAAP margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sep 2023 | 301.4 | 24.9 | 8.26% |
| Dec 2023 | 256.4 | 22.8 | 8.89% |
| Sep 2024 | 348.2 | 28.4 | 8.16% |
| Dec 2024 | 304.3 | 47.4 | 15.58% |
| **Sep 2025** | 380.8 | 69.6 | **18.28%** |
| **Dec 2025** | 318.2 | 56.7 | **17.82%** |
| Jun 2026 | 395.9 | 80.6 | 20.36% |

Three consequences, none of them a sale:

- **The 18% leg has 0.3 and 0.2 points of headroom.** A September 2026 quarter at last year's 18.28%
  and a December at last year's 17.82% would leave the second break route needing only a 0.3-point
  slip in September, with the volume leg already pointing the right way after Q3. A year of no
  deterioration at all could come within rounding of declaring the thesis wrong. The proposal set
  18% "below the locally recorded FY2025 margin" — the full-year margin, 18.2% or so, not the margin
  of the two quarters it then chose to test.
- **The 16% leg is the sounder one**, with 1.8 to 2.3 points of headroom against the same two
  quarters last year, though it was breached in both of them as recently as FY2024.
- **The 11% asset route cannot trip at the November report.** Nine-month FY2026 operating income is
  already $217.7m, and 11% of a plausible FY2026 average asset base near $1.91bn is about $210m. It
  would take a negative September quarter. The route is live for the December report, not the first
  one.

The fix is a re-proposal that tests these two quarters **against the same quarters a year earlier**
rather than against an absolute level drawn from a full-year figure. That is a change to P3, not a
trade, and no instruction follows from it.
