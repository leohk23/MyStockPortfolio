### The first checkpoint cannot see the risk P6 names, and an earlier version of this proposal knew that

P6's dominant risk is that *"rivals and cheaper automated protocols take fee income faster than
electronic trading expands the addressable activity"* — Tradeweb keeps winning the market while the
toll on it shrinks. Of the four routes in P3, two test monetisation (fees per million, credit revenue
against credit ADV) and two test share. The monetisation routes do see the risk, so this is not the
blind falsifier the brief warns about.

The calibration problem is which one arrives first. **The earliest observable named in P6, and the
8 October review date built on it, is a market-share test** — the one measure that stays green under
exactly the failure P6 describes. The 8 October checkpoint will therefore report calm by
construction, whatever is happening to the toll, and the first date that can say anything about the
dominant risk is 29 October.

The [13 September 18:23 proposal](../proposals/2026-09-13-1823-TW.md), written the day before the one
Leo filled, had already made this argument in its own words: *"All three are revenue-per-unit-of-volume
or profitability measures rather than market-share measures, deliberately: the dominant risk in P6 is
that Tradeweb keeps winning the market while the toll on it shrinks, and a share statistic would stay
green throughout that."* The [14 September 00:38 version](../proposals/2026-09-14-0038-TW.md) kept
that discipline. The accepted 09:06 version put the share test back, and made it the earliest
checkpoint.

A share test is not worthless — losing customers and losing pricing are different failures, and the

### The multiple is now being held above its own floor by a vendor EPS revision

Worth one paragraph because it is exactly the kind of figure this repo exists to distrust.

`signals.json` rule 6.1 reports MWA at a P/E of **16.07** against a five-year floor of **15.39**, so
**vsFloor +4.4%** — it reads as if the stock stopped just short of its own cheapest week. The two

close against the EPS **published by that date**, and its `lowEps` is **$1.42**, the filed trailing
figure through the June quarter. The current multiple is `price ÷ quote.eps`, and `quote.eps` was
**cut from $1.42 to $1.36 in this morning's 04:32 UTC fetch**, with no MWA filing between the two
runs. On the filed basis the two agree exactly: $21.85 ÷ $1.42 is **15.39×**, the floor was struck
**today**, at **today's price**, and MWA sits at the **0th percentile of 261 weeks**.

So the honest statement is that MWA is at its five-year low multiple, not 4.4% above it, and the gap
is an artefact of mixing a vendor trailing figure with a filed one inside a single row. This is the
same class of hazard the `oneOff` and `epsStale` annotations exist for, and neither fires here —
`epsStale` watches whether our EPS is behind the company's reporting, which it is not; nothing
watches whether the two sides of `vsFloor` are on the same basis.

None of this is a sell reason or a buy reason. Valuation is not in §5.1 and this lane produces no
instruction from it. It is recorded because the next lane to quote "4.4% above its floor" would be
quoting a number that is not comparing like with like.
